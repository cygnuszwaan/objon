# Lasers, arcs and mirrors

The shared beam solver (`LaserBeam`), the laser turret, the arc turret and the arc, and the mirror. Read `objon.md` (the core: setup, layers and groups, contracts, invariants, docs index) first.

Covers: Laser beam solver; Laser turret; Freeze turret; Arc turret (The arc; Hazard; Water; Power; Checkpoint; Placeholder art); Mirror

- **Laser beam solver** (`res://scripts/laser_beam.gd`, `class_name LaserBeam`): stateless, shared by the laser turret and the arc (see Arc turret); `LaserBeam.trace(space, tree, origin, direction, exclude, mask)` returns a `Result` (world-space `points` polyline, `end_collider`, `end_point`, `end_direction`). Each step casts a physics ray (mask 1|2, so actors and world) and separately tests the ray analytically against every node in group `"laser_mirror"`; the nearer wins. Mirrors are analytic rather than physical so the reflection normal is exact at any angle and a rotating mirror never depends on its collider's transform being synced this frame — mirror bodies are excluded from the physics ray for the same reason. Mirror contract (duck-typed): `get_laser_segment() -> PackedVector2Array` (`[a, b]`, world space) and `get_laser_normal() -> Vector2` (front face, world space), plus an optional `laser_body_is_physical() -> bool`: a mirror answering true keeps its own body in the physics ray (the see-saw plank, whose underside and ends must still stop the beam), and its segment then has to sit a pixel in front of the collider so it wins the tie on the front face. A ray hitting a mirror's front reflects (up to `MAX_REFLECTIONS` 8); its back, its edge, or anything else solid stops the beam — including enemies (opaque but harmless). Catwalks and glass never stop it: a hit on a `world_tilemap` cell whose `surface_type` `SurfaceTypes.laser_passes()` accepts (`"catwalk"` or `"glass"`; the same tile lookup as the player's `_is_catwalk_ray_hit()`), or on a body in group `"catwalk"`, is stepped past and the ray continues. After stepping past a catwalk, the new start point is tested with `intersect_point`: a physics ray that starts inside a shape ignores it, so without that test a beam clipping a catwalk's corner directly under a block would be nudged into the block and pass straight through it — if the point is inside an opaque body, the beam ends there. Cannon bullets are `Area2D`s and are ignored.
- **Laser turret** (`laser_turret.gd`): `@tool` `Node2D` root with a `Body` (`StaticBody2D`, layer 2, joins `water_solid`) and a `Muzzle` `Marker2D`; the beam leaves along `Vector2.RIGHT.rotated(global_rotation)`, so aim is the instance's own rotation, same convention as Cannon. Every physics frame it solves the beam (own body excluded), draws it with `_draw()` (glow + core + impact spark, in local space via `to_local`), and dispatches on whatever ended it: anything with `on_laser_hit(point, direction)` is told it's lit, and the player (group `"player"` with `take_hit`) is hurt every frame the beam is on them, polled like Sawwheel/Crusher so grace-period expiry re-hits a player standing in it. An enemy (group `"enemy"` with `take_damage()`: grunt, Drillbot, Stingbot) is hurt by one point of damage (`take_damage(1)`) per `enemy_damage_interval` (default 0.5 s, exported) rather than per frame, since Drillbot ignores the amount and would die in three frames of polling; the first frame of contact hits at once, and the clock is kept per enemy (`_enemy_hit_times`, instance id → time of the last hit, pruned as clocks run out), so a beam swept off and back onto one enemy can't skip the interval. The gate is the group, not the method: `Mirror` also has `take_damage()` (a kick turns it), and a beam ending on a mirror's back or edge must not turn it. Enemies stay opaque, so the beam stops at them and a receiver behind a Stingbot flying through the beam flickers (its `hold_time` covers that); a kill uses the enemy's own death (explosion, or the grunt's). The hit clocks aren't saved, so an enemy still in the beam after a restore is hit at once. `damage` and `beam_color` are exported; `enabled`/`set_enabled()` shuts the beam and its `traps/laser_hum` loop off (what `set_powered()` calls: powered = beam on). Housing art is drawn in code (`draw_placeholder`) until sprites exist; the editor shows a dashed aim preview. **Laser boiling** (roadmap 5.4, opt-in): with `boils_water` on (default false) the turret hands its beam polyline to the level's `WaterManager.boil_along_beam(points, delta)` each physics frame (manager looked up lazily). Boiling needs both flags: `boils_water` here and `boilable` on the `WaterBasin` (default false). The beam has no collision with liquid, so the manager tests it against the basin's liquid polygons; the first point it enters the liquid (or its first point, if it starts inside) is the crossing. A boiled basin emits steam there through `emit_steam()` (`BOIL_STEAM_VOLUME_PER_SEC` per second of contact, independent of any scalar) and loses `BOIL_DRAIN_VOLUME_PER_SEC` × the basin's `boil_volume_scale` × delta through `remove_volume()`. Slag is never boiled (read from the basin's runtime liquid), liquid sealed under a crust it touches is skipped, and a crust stops the beam as before. A pool a faucet refills settles where the beam meets the surface. Glass blocks the steam like any solid tile. The sound `traps/laser_boil` (in `Audio.PLANNED`) plays at the turret when a beam starts boiling something. A **frozen crust** (see Frozen crust in the Liquid system) is a laser target too: the beam ends on the crust's body, which forwards `on_laser_hit()` to its basin, and `melt_time` seconds of sustained laser thaw the whole crust; a reaction crust just stops the beam, as before.
- **Arc turret** (`res://scenes/traps/arc/arc_turret.gd` + `arc_turret.tscn`, `arc.gd` + `arc.tscn`; behaviour built, art is **placeholder shapes**): the laser turret's discrete cousin. It fires an `Arc` every `fire_interval` (2 s) along its own rotation (same convention as Cannon and the laser turret). `@tool` `Node2D` root with a `Body` (`StaticBody2D`, layer 2, joins `"water_solid"`), a `Muzzle` `Marker2D` and a `PowerTarget`; in group `"saveable"`. Fully deterministic: the last `TELEGRAPH` (0.45 s) before each shot is a growing glow at the muzzle, and `phase_offset` staggers several turrets. Exports are authored values only (`start_enabled`, `fire_interval`, `phase_offset`, `damage`, `arc_speed` 220 px/s, `arc_range` 480 px, `invert`); runtime state is `_enabled` and `_clock`.
  - **The arc** (`arc.gd`, a `Node2D` with no body and no layer, so no query sees it: the same inertness as a Spark, but it is not a physics object): each physics frame it calls `LaserBeam.trace()` from its own position and heading with mask 2 (world only) and walks `speed * delta` along the polyline, so it reflects off every mirror a beam would (`Mirror`, a see-saw plank with `mirror_surface`), reads a turning or moving mirror live, and is stopped by whatever stops a beam (tiles, doors, blocks, a mirror's back or edge); catwalks and glass pass it. Mask 2 means it flies through enemies and the player's layer unharmed and unstopped. After crossing a mirror it is placed at least `MIN_VERTEX_CLEARANCE` (0.75 px) past it, because a solve that starts on a plate ignores the plate. The firing turret's own body is excluded for the first `OWN_BODY_GRACE` (12 px) of travel, so a reflected arc can hit its own turret. It dies out at `arc_range` of travel, and a spent arc shows a short harmless flash (`FADE_TIME`).
  - **Hazard**: at every `SAMPLE_STEP` (4 px) of a frame's travel an immediate circle `intersect_shape()` (radius `HIT_RADIUS` 4, mask 1, group `"player"`) looks for the player, so a fast arc cannot step over a thin collider; a hit calls `take_hit(damage, position)` and spends the arc whether or not the hit landed (i-frames). Enemies, mines, blocks, buttons and laser receivers do not react to it. **Receivers**: when the polyline runs out on a body (the arc is spent on it), the arc calls that body's optional duck-typed `on_arc_hit(point, direction)` once (the arc's counterpart of a beam's `on_laser_hit()`); `ArcReceiver` implements it (see Arc receiver). A body without the method, or a mirror's back or edge, is just an obstacle.
  - **Water**: at the same sample points it offers its lowest point (`point + (0, RADIUS)`) to `WaterManager.find_basin_at_point()` and `WaterBasin.try_electrify_at()`, exactly as a Spark does, with the same refusals (see Electrified water in the Liquid system); a basin that accepts spends the arc, one that refuses lets it fly on. Both calls run from the sampled points, so an arc crossing the surface at a steep angle cannot skip the strike.
  - **Power**: powered = firing. Switching on from off fires one arc at once (deferred, like Cannon) and then the clock runs from zero; switching off stops the clock and clears the glow, and arcs in flight keep flying. `set_powered_initial()` (the startup and restore apply from `PowerTarget`) sets the state with no shot. Unwired, or already powered at load, the first shot comes after `fire_interval` less `phase_offset`.
  - **Checkpoint**: saves `enabled` only. The clock restarts from zero, so a restore can never put an arc on the respawning player at once; arcs in flight are not saved.
  - **Placeholder art**: the housing and the arc are drawn in code (a bright ball with a jagged tail that crackles each physics frame); replace the housing with a sprite and set `draw_placeholder` false. **Live pitfall**: the arc's trail is seeded on its first physics frame, never in `_ready()`, because the turret sets `global_position` after `add_child()`, so at `_ready()` the arc is still at the world origin and a trail seeded there draws a pale line from the origin to the arc for its first few frames. The editor draws a dashed aim preview (straight, ignoring reflections). Sounds `traps/arc_fire`, `traps/arc_reflect` and `traps/arc_fizzle` are in `Audio.PLANNED` (a pool's zap is the basin's own `interactive/basin_zap`).
- **Freeze turret** (`res://scenes/traps/freeze/freeze_turret.gd` + `freeze_turret.tscn`, `@tool`; roadmap N3.2): the cold of the beam family. The laser is heat and instant, the arc is a charge and one hit, the freeze ray is **cumulative**: its effect lands after a dwell that lives on the target, so one turret serves targets with different dwell times. Same layout and facing as the laser turret (a `Body` on layer 2 in `water_solid`, a `Muzzle`, a `PowerTarget` child; the beam leaves along `Vector2.RIGHT.rotated(global_rotation)` and is solved by `LaserBeam.trace()`, so mirrors reflect it, catwalks and glass pass it, and everything that stops a laser stops it, a crust included) and the same beam art format (`beam_texture`, `hit_texture`; `freeze.png`, `freezehit.png` and `freezeturret.png` are hue-shifted placeholders of the laser's). It is **non-lethal** and keeps no per-target state. Each physics frame it dispatches on what ended the beam: anything with `on_freeze_hit(point, direction)` is told it is lit (the freeze receiver); the player, if it has `apply_chill()`, is chilled at strength 1 (see Chill); an enemy is chilled the same way (see Chill under Enemies in `enemies.md`) and is opaque to the beam. It also hands the beam polyline to the level's `WaterManager.freeze_along_beam(points, delta)` (manager looked up lazily, like boiling), which adds `delta` seconds of frost to every `freezable` basin the beam crosses (`WaterBasin.add_frost()`) and freezes it at its `freeze_time` (see Frozen crust in the Liquid system). Exports: `start_enabled` (authored; the runtime `enabled`, written by `set_enabled()`, `set_powered()` and `load_state()`, is a plain variable, so the export audit stays clean), `invert`, `beam_color`, `draw_placeholder` and the beam art. Saveable: `enabled`. Powered = beam on. Sound `traps/freeze_hum` (a loop) is in `Audio.PLANNED`.
- **Mirror** (`mirror.gd`): `@tool` `StaticBody2D` on layers 1|2 (see Physics layers), a thin `plate_length` × 3px collider that is also solid to the player. Front face is local −Y (up at rotation 0). Kicks, power and (opt-in) bullets turn it, tweened in `TWEEN_PROCESS_PHYSICS` over `rotate_time` so the beam visibly sweeps. One checkbox per instance, `toggle_between_aims`, picks the **control mode**; the Inspector shows only the fields of the selected mode (`_validate_property()` hides the others without dropping their values, so switching back keeps what was authored).
  - **Step mode** (checkbox off, the default): the instance's rotation in the editor is step index 0. A kick, or a rising edge of power, turns it one `rotation_step_degrees` step (default 45°) in `rotate_direction` (a Clockwise / Counter-clockwise enum). Falling edges do nothing and the startup state is not an edge (`invert` makes the wire's falling edge the turn). A kick or edge during a turn is dropped, so mashing cannot queue a spin.
  - **Toggle mode** (checkbox on): two authored aims, `aim_0_degrees` (default 0; unpowered, and where it starts) and `aim_1_degrees` (default 90; powered), each the node's own rotation in degrees, the number in the Inspector's Rotation field. The instance's editor rotation is therefore ignored at run time; the editor draws both aims over the plate (blue for aim 0, orange for aim 1, flag `show_aim_preview`). Power follows the level, not the edge: powered = aim 1, unpowered = aim 0, on both edges. A power change during a turn is not dropped; the mirror turns back from where it is, so it always ends on the aim the power state names (a full sweep takes `rotate_time`, a part-sweep proportionally less). The startup state snaps to its aim (`set_powered_initial()`: no turn, no sound). The sweep runs the numeric way between the two angles and nothing is wrapped: 0 to 270 sweeps 270°, 0 to −90 is the short way round. A kick (`kick_rotates`) or a bullet (`bullet_rotates`) flips to the other aim and is dropped mid-turn; the next power change overrides it. A configuration warning (`get_warnings()`) flags two equal aims.
  - **Shared**: a kick reaches it via `take_damage()` (returns 0) from any side. With `bullet_rotates` on (default off, so bullets fly through as before) a cannon bullet hitting the plate does what a kick does through `on_bullet_hit()` and is consumed; a hit during a turn is dropped but still consumes the bullet. Public API (also used by power): `rotate_step(steps)`, `rotate_to_step(index)`, `is_rotating()`, and `kick_rotates = false` to stop kicks turning it. In toggle mode `toggle_aim()` and `rotate_to_aim(index)` are the direct controls (both do nothing in step mode), `rotate_step()` flips on an odd count, and `rotate_to_step()` maps index 0 to aim 0 and any other index to aim 1. Saves its target rotation, last power level and aim index. A restore runs before the `PowerTarget`'s startup apply, and a restored toggle-mode mirror keeps its saved aim (the startup apply then only records the power level), so an aim set by a kick survives a respawn. Plays `interactive/mirror_rotate` at the start of every turn (not on a startup snap). A `Mirror` can be a child of a moving platform: its segment and normal are read from the live global transform at each solve, so the beam sweeps with the platform (one physics step behind it, like the see-saw plank), and only its own local rotation is saved. A mirror on a swing is not planned.
