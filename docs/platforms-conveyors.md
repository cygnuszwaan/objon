# Platforms, conveyors, jump pads and the breakable barrier

Moving platform, drop platform, conveyor belt, jump pad (the object) and the breakable barrier. Read `objon.md` (the core: setup, layers and groups, contracts, invariants, docs index) first.

Covers: Moving platform; Drop platform; Conveyor belt; Jump pad; Breakable barrier

- **Moving platform** (`moving_platform.gd`): `AnimatableBody2D` + a looping `Tween` on `position` (`TWEEN_PROCESS_PHYSICS`, `sync_to_physics = true`). Riding needs **no player code at all** — `CharacterBody2D.move_and_slide()` already inherits a floor body's velocity, as long as the platform's motion happens inside the physics step (hence the physics-process tween). Hanging on a moving platform works by shifting the hang's pinned world-space points (`hang_anchor`, `mantle_target`) by the platform's per-frame position delta; the mantle tween accordingly uses `tween_method`/lerp between two world points rather than `tween_property` to a fixed target, since a fixed target can't be shifted mid-tween. A one-way platform that should also be ledge-grabbable needs thin one-way end-cap `CollisionShape2D`s (one-way direction facing sideways) at its outer edges — a plain one-way top shape produces no side contact for the grab to latch onto, and a solid cap would block jump-through at the ends.
- **Drop platform** (`drop_platform.gd`): a generous `Area2D` sensor (covers both standing-on-top and hanging-at-the-edge) latches a countdown once a player is detected on/hanging from it; committed once triggered (leaving early doesn't cancel). On drop, collision is disabled (deferred) and a hang from this platform is force-released. Reappears via a `VisibleOnScreenNotifier2D` made `top_level` so it stays pinned to the platform's original spot even after the body has fallen away.
- **Conveyor belt** (`conveyor_belt.gd`): a plain `StaticBody2D` on physics layer 2. `@tool`, so it builds itself: assign `left_cap_frames`/`mid_frames`/`right_cap_frames` (its three pieces of source art, each with `"left"`/`"right"` animations for the two travel directions) once per belt, and the exported `segments` count then drives everything, live in the editor. `segments` is signed and means total tile width, not mid-tile count: `0` clears the belt entirely (`Pieces` empty, collision disabled); `1` places a single mid tile with no end caps; `abs(segments) >= 2` places end caps at both ends with `abs(segments) - 2` mid tiles between. The sign controls which side of the node's origin the belt extends to — positive builds rightward from `x=0` (origin at the belt's left edge), negative builds the same layout leftward (origin at the belt's right edge) — so two belts can be placed origin-to-origin and extend away from each other, or a belt's origin can sit at whichever edge is more convenient for level layout. `_rebuild()` regenerates `Pieces` and resizes/repositions `CollisionShape2D`'s `RectangleShape2D` (or disables it, at `segments == 0`) to match; a shared `_segment_layout()` helper (returns tile count + build direction) is used by both `_rebuild()` and the debug draw below so they can't disagree. Rebuilding replaces whatever's currently under `Pieces` (the old children are removed from the tree at once so the new ones keep their names), so it isn't hand-edited directly; the pieces are given no `owner`, so they are never saved into a belt or level scene (a belt still being dragged in isn't under the edited root yet, and setting an owner then raises "Invalid owner"). Carries the player, any `MeltableBlock` resting on it and a rail bot's `Spark`. Duck-typed like `take_hit()`/`collect_gem()`: the belt exposes `get_conveyor_speed()`, and the player's `_conveyor_push_speed()` reads that off whatever it's resting on each physics frame (via `get_slide_collision_count()`, filtered to roughly floor-facing normals) and adds the result straight onto `velocity.x` before `move_and_slide()` — a plain addition, so moving with/against a belt just adds two numbers rather than overriding either. `MeltableBlock` reads the same contract from its own previous-move slide collisions, but adds the belt speed only around its `move_and_slide()` and removes it again afterwards (its `velocity.x` is its own kick motion, which must neither inherit the belt nor fight it through slide friction). One frame of lag (the collisions read are the previous frame's) is inherent and not noticeable. Deliberately **not** added to the `"ground"`/`"catwalk"` groups and not part of `world_tilemap` — `_surface_type_at()` only recognizes those, so the ledge-grab system can never latch onto a conveyor, rounded end caps included, with no special-casing needed. The animation's `speed_scale` is derived from `belt_speed / REFERENCE_SPEED` (a hardcoded constant, measured once against the actual art at 10 px/s) rather than the SpriteFrames' own FPS, so belt speed and visual pace can never drift apart and a runtime speed change (`set_belt_speed()`, for a future speed-control button) automatically retunes the animation too. `enabled`/`set_enabled()` pauses the animation mid-frame (rather than hiding it) and zeroes the push, and is what `set_powered()` calls (powered = running). `debug_draw_bounds` (default on, per-instance, mirrors `player.gd`'s `debug_draw_hang_probes`) outlines the belt's actual built width/direction, ticks each tile boundary, and draws a small travel-direction arrow directly in the editor viewport via `_draw()`, redrawn continuously while `Engine.is_editor_hint()` is true (`set_process(false)` outside the editor, so it costs nothing at runtime) — useful for placing/sizing a belt before its art is assigned. **Editor-only pitfall, still live**: after placing a new conveyor instance in a level scene (or toggling Editable Children on an existing one), its `@tool` script can go on running stale — sprites not rebuilding, debug draw not appearing — until that level scene's tab is closed and reopened; the editor doesn't reliably refresh a nested instance's live tool-script state on its own. If a belt in a level looks wrong or its debug outline is missing, reopen that scene before assuming the script itself is broken.
- **Jump pad** (`jump_pad.gd`): `AnimatedSprite2D` root (idle holds frame 0, same shape as Cannon) with a child `Area2D` trigger. On `body_entered`, launches whatever overlapped it at `launch_speed` along `Vector2.UP.rotated(global_rotation)` and plays the `"default"` animation through once (`stop()` on `animation_finished` returns it to frame 0). Rotating the instance rotates the launch vector with it, the same convention as Cannon's muzzle direction. Duck-typed like `take_hit()`/`collect_gem()`/`get_conveyor_speed()`: it calls `launch(vector)` on anything that has the method rather than checking a group name, so only the player and `MeltableBlock` (see its Jump pad launch sentence) are affected; enemies have no `launch()` and walk over it untouched.
- **Breakable barrier** (`res://scenes/interactive/breakable_barrier.gd` + `.tscn`, `@tool`): a `StaticBody2D` wall that a fast surf breaks through and a slow one stops against. Layers 1|2, mask 0 (layer 2 solid to the player, layer 1 what the bowl sweep and the kick see), group `"water_solid"` and not `"ground"`/`"catwalk"` (never ledge-grabbable). It implements the surf contract `bowled_over(direction, surf_speed) -> bool`: at or above `break_speed` (default 450, range 300..800) it breaks and returns true, so the surf keeps its speed and the player's collision exception lets this frame's move through; below it, or from the wrong side, it returns false and stays a wall (the surf ends against it). `break_from` is `EITHER`, `LEFT` (the surfer comes from the left, travelling right) or `RIGHT`. The sweep only runs from the player's `surf_bowl_speed` (300), so a `break_speed` under 300 behaves as 300, one above the dry top speed (600) is reachable only on wet ground, and one above 800 never (the Inspector warns at both ends; the numbers are mirrored constants in the script). Breaking retires it at once: out of `"water_solid"` and `"saveable"` (so a checkpoint taken that frame excludes it and liquid resting on it falls), collision disabled (deferred), `interactive/barrier_break` played at its centre (in `Audio.PLANNED`), placeholder debris (small `ColorRect` shards thrown the way the surfer travelled, each owning its tween, in the barrier's parent) and `queue_free()`. Saveable with an empty state: a broken barrier is absent from the snapshot, so it stays gone after a restore. Size is `size_tiles` (default 1×3, 16px tiles), with the collider rebuilt from it on every load (a per-instance `RectangleShape2D`; a child's shape edits aren't saved in a level, so edit the export); node origin is the **top-left** corner on a 16px grid point. Art is drawn in code (planks and iron bands, `wood_color`/`band_color`; the editor adds arrows for the break side and the break speed) until sprites exist. It also blocks Drillbot and Stingbot (mask 2), blocks and the laser beam like any wall, and a cannon bullet passes through it (bullets only react to the player and `world_tilemap` bodies).
