# Liquid system (summary)

The short version of the liquid system: particle manager, basins, faucets, liquid types (water, slag, steam), crust, electrified water. The full reference is `water-mechanic.md`; read that only for a deep change. Read `objon.md` (the core: setup, layers and groups, contracts, invariants, docs index) first.

Covers: Liquid system

- **Liquid system** (`water_manager.gd`, one instance per level scene — not an autoload; `WaterBasin` in `basin.gd`; `WaterFaucet` in `faucet.gd`; `LiquidConstants` in `liquid_constants.gd`, renamed from `water_constants.gd` once a second liquid existed): flowing liquid is pooled particles — position/velocity/type in parallel arrays, no per-particle nodes or collision shapes — sized by `LiquidConstants.ACTIVE_PRESET` (particles-per-tile/pixel-size/volume all cascade from one preset constant, shared by every liquid type; the active value is still being tuned, so read it from `liquid_constants.gd` rather than trusting a number written here). Each particle also carries a `LiquidConstants.LiquidType` (`WATER`, `SLAG`, or the gas-only `STEAM`); every physical property that differs by liquid — creep speed, bounce, friction, push force, hazard, color, lifetime — is read per-particle from `LiquidConstants.profile(type)`, so the simulation itself has one implementation regardless of which liquid is flowing. Each physics frame every (non-`STEAM`) particle raycasts against the tilemap and any `"water_solid"`-registered body — **except catwalk tiles, which water ignores entirely** (a hit on a cell whose `surface_type` is `"catwalk"` is stepped past and the ray re-cast from just beyond it, up to 4 passes, in `_cast_ignoring_catwalks()`; so water falls through them, never rests on, bounces off or creeps along one, while they stay solid for the player) — (moving platform, drop platform, crusher's `ArmBody`, cannon's `StaticBody2D`, the see-saw, and a basin's own solidified crust — see below), bounces or slide-creeps depending on impact speed and its own type's profile, and is absorbed into whichever `WaterBasin` its column belongs to once it reaches that basin's current surface height. A `WaterBasin` is a hand-placed `@tool` node (same placed-and-sized-by-hand convention as the conveyor belt) with its own `liquid_type`; it samples the tilemap's actual per-tile collision geometry under it at edit time (counting only ground-like tiles, `"ground"` and `"glass"`, as floor and wall, never catwalks) to derive a per-column floor height (fractional on sloped/half-height tiles) and treats its two end columns as walls. `fill_volume` and the crust's two faces (`crust_top_row`/`crust_bottom_row`) are the only things mutated at runtime: a same-type particle reaching a not-yet-full, crust-free basin credits `fill_volume`; an OPPOSITE-type particle reaching a basin that already has some settled liquid instead solidifies a slice of it into a crust and emits steam — the "water meets slag, or vice versa" reaction. **Steam**: `WaterManager.emit_steam(world_pos, amount)` is the only entry point (`amount` is the volume in tiles that reacted); the crust reaction calls it, once for the crust's formation and once for every opposite-type particle that reaches a crust's top face, starting the puff at the particle's x and no lower than half a particle above the crust (`_steam_origin()`). It makes `amount` × `STEAM_PUFFS_PER_TILE_VOLUME` (16: one puff per arriving particle at the 16-per-tile preset, rounded at random, so a stronger faucet makes a denser column) `STEAM` particles, each rising at a constant `STEAM_RISE_SPEED` (80 px/s, ±`STEAM_SPEED_JITTER` 10%) inside a ±`STEAM_CONE_DEGREES` (6°) cone about straight up, with no gravity or deceleration, so a column is `STEAM_COLUMN_HEIGHT` (rise speed × `STEAM_LIFETIME` 1.6 s = 128 px, 8 tiles) tall in the open. Each steam particle casts one ray a frame and dies on the first solid it meets (the world tilemap or a `"water_solid"` body; catwalk tiles are stepped past as for liquid), so a column stops at a ceiling; steam is only emitted while `STEAM_POOL_RESERVE` (256) pool slots are free, so it can't starve the liquid. Steam isn't saved: after a restore a column rebuilds over about one lifetime. **Steam lift query**: `WaterManager.get_steam_lift(rect, previous = 0.0, delta = 0.0) -> float` is a 0..1 coverage of a world-space rect (the board's footprint) by steam. Steam particles within the rect's height count by the horizontal span they cover, merged over at most `STEAM_LIFT_MAX_BINS` (32) equal bins of the rect's width, so a dense column reads the same as a thin one of the same width and the reading depends on neither the particle preset nor the rect's height; coverage of `STEAM_LIFT_FULL_COVERAGE` (0.5) of the width reads 1. The raw reading is noisy, so a caller passes its previous smoothed value and the frame time and gets `max(reading, previous - delta / STEAM_LIFT_DECAY_TIME)` back (instant rise, linear decay over `STEAM_LIFT_DECAY_TIME`, 0.3 s, like the surf's wetness); with the defaults it is the raw reading. It is stateless (the manager keeps no per-caller value), only steam counts, and the surfing player is its only caller (see Steam lift under Surf). The `debug_steam_lift` export (default false) shows an on-screen readout of the raw and smoothed values for the player's current collider rect. Crust is stored geometry (a top and bottom row across the wet columns), rigid and walkable via a runtime `StaticBody2D` with one rectangular collision shape per column. It forms once at a fixed 0.75-tile thickness (`CRUST_THICKNESS_TILES`) and never moves, grows or thins afterwards, and nothing is ever absorbed *through* it — same-type liquid arriving on top just rests on the crust, while an opposite-type particle evaporates into a steam puff on contact. A basin reads as full once its outer surface (the liquid, or the crust's top face if crusted) reaches its shorter wall's height, at which point arriving same-type particles creep out over that wall using the ordinary ground-creep rules — this is also how basins chain into each other with no adjacency data at all. An optional `initial_fill_depth_below_surface` authors a basin as starting partway (or fully) full. `SLAG`'s profile is marked hazardous: both flowing slag particles (within `PLAYER_PUSH_RADIUS`) and a settled slag basin's still-liquid surface (not any crust that's formed over it — crust reads as safe, walkable ground regardless of which liquid reacted to form it) call `take_hit()` on the player each frame of contact, and the player swims in a hazardous basin's liquid with buoyancy and takes damage each frame. The settled body draws as a real per-column staircase polygon (crust between its two stored faces, liquid from its own floor-anchored surface down to the seabed, distinct colors for each; any gap between them after a drain is a void and is simply not drawn; a skinned liquid's body is drawn as one filled rectangle per ripple strip, not a polygon, because `draw_colored_polygon()` triangulates all-or-nothing and a ripple's whole-pixel staircase could occasionally be rejected, blanking the whole body for a frame — unskinned liquid and crust, whose tops are flat, still draw polygons, and `debug_check_body` on a basin warns whenever the polygon form of the body would fail to triangulate); slag, water and every crust are skinned with art from `sprites/liquids/` (per liquid type: `SKIN_BY_TYPE` for the surface, with water looping faster than slag, and `CRUST_SKIN_PATH_BY_TYPE` for the crust, so water can cool from water colour; a missing file falls back to flat colour / the slag crust art) — the art is the top layer; a liquid's body below it is the art's bottom-row colour, while crust art has its non-neutral fringe cut transparent so the liquid shows through it, or over a drained void only the fringe inside the crust is filled neutral and the underside keeps the art's own jagged edge (see `LiquidSkin` and the Skins note and Crust rendering in `water-mechanic.md`); the body, surface art and crust also extend `wall_overlap_px` behind each wall column so a transparent strip on a wall sprite shows no gap (see Wall overlap in `water-mechanic.md`); everything is drawn behind the tilemap so any approximation at a sloped tile's edge stays hidden. Moving particles push the player horizontally within a small radius of them (`PLAYER_PUSH_RADIUS` — currently reads too large, flagged for retuning), summed by direction and capped both by a flat ceiling and by whichever contributing particle is currently fastest, so a crowd of slow particles can't shove the player faster than the liquid is actually moving. `WaterFaucet` is tile-anchored (node origin at its tile's top-left, `spawn_offset` picks the spawn point inside the tile) and emits particles at a per-instance `flow_rate` (volume per second, independent of the particles-per-tile preset) while `is_open`. Sitting on the floor tile of a basin's interior column, it is a **drain**: each drip debits `PARTICLE_VOLUME` from that basin's `fill_volume` and spawns a particle of the basin's liquid type, leaving any crust hanging over the void it opens; nothing comes out once no liquid lies over the tile. Anywhere else it is a plain **source** dripping its own `liquid_type` (water or slag); the melt burst API (`spawn_melt_particles(tile_pos, liquid_type, world_center = INF, cell_rotation = 0)`, covering both ice→water and metal→slag) is called by `MeltableBlock._melt()` once per 16px cell it covers; with a finite `world_center` the burst is spread over the cell in the block's rotated frame, inset by `SPAWN_INSET` (0.85) so every particle starts inside the block's volume, otherwise over the snapped, axis-aligned tile. **Carry and load**: a particle resting on a registered body is carried by that body's whole transform change: each frame `_solid_motion` maps every body whose `global_transform` changed (`_registered_solid_prev_xform` holds last frame's) to `new × old.affine_inverse()`, and a resting particle's position is mapped through it, so a platform's translation carries it and the see-saw's rotation swings it round the pivot; particle velocity is untouched. `forget_motion(body)` resets a body's stored transform so a teleport (a checkpoint restore) isn't carried. A resting particle on a registered body that has `get_pivot_global()` (a `RotatingBody`) also loads it: `LiquidProfile.weight_per_tile_volume` (block-mass units per tile of volume: water 1.0, the same as ice; slag 3.0, under metal's 4; steam 0) × `PARTICLE_VOLUME` × the particle's horizontal offset from the pivot, summed per body each frame and read through `get_resting_torque(body)`; particles on a basin's synthesized surface add nothing. **Surface velocity**: `WaterBasin.get_liquid_surface_velocity_y()` (px/s, negative = rising) is what a floating block rides. It is estimated from the change of `get_liquid_surface_world_y()` between physics frames, smoothed over `SURFACE_VELOCITY_SMOOTH_TIME` (0.25 s, since fill arrives in particle-sized steps) and clamped to ±`SURFACE_VELOCITY_MAX` (240), lazily and once per physics frame however many bodies ask; the first query, or one after more than `SURFACE_VELOCITY_GAP_FRAMES` (3) frames without a query, reads 0 and restarts the estimate, so a restore or a teleported fill can't read as a flood. Runtime scratch only (`_surface_vy`, `_surface_prev_y`, `_surface_vy_frame`), never saved; ripple isn't part of it. **Surface ripple** (cosmetic, in `basin.gd`): a per-basin wave simulation over 4px samples bends only the drawn top edge of the settled liquid, driven by `WaterBasin.disturb(world_x, velocity, radius)` from landing particles, drain drips, player swim entry/wake and ice/metal block splashes. Viscosity is per-liquid data in `LiquidProfile` (`ripple_speed`, `ripple_damping`, `ripple_strength`): slag's waves are slow, heavily damped and weakly stirred. It costs nothing while calm and never affects `fill_volume`, queries, collision or crust. See `water-mechanic.md` for the full data-flow/API/tuning-constant reference and what's still open. A surfing player reads the liquid through two pull queries, `get_floor_wetness()` and `find_skim_basin()`, and a basin raises a runtime one-way skim floor for it on layer 3 (see the Water bullet under Surf and `water-mechanic.md`). **Laser boiling** (roadmap 5.4): `WaterManager.boil_along_beam(points, delta)` boils the WATER of every `boilable` basin a beam polyline crosses, emitting steam at the crossing and draining the basin at its `boil_volume_scale` (see Laser turret); `BOIL_STEAM_VOLUME_PER_SEC`, `BOIL_DRAIN_VOLUME_PER_SEC` and `BOIL_ENTRY_NUDGE` set its rates. `WaterBasin` gains the authored exports `boilable` and `boil_volume_scale`. **Electrified water**: a Spark (see Rail bot) or an Arc (see Arc turret) reaching the settled surface of a crust-free WATER basin (or the top of a frozen crust over one, see Frozen crust) electrifies the whole pool for `electrified_time` (default 1 s, exported; a later strike restarts the countdown, never adds to it). `WaterBasin.try_electrify_at(world_point)` is the only entry (it applies the refusals listed under Rail bot, then sets the runtime `_electrified_left`, kicks a cosmetic ripple through `disturb()`, plays `interactive/basin_zap` at the surface and returns true); `is_electrified()` reads it and `is_hazardous_now()` (a hazardous liquid, or electrified) is the one question the player's swim check and `WaterManager.find_skim_basin()` ask, so a future transient hazard goes there and nowhere else. `conducts_sparks` (default true) and `electrified_time` are authored exports; the countdown is runtime-only and **not saved**, so a restore comes back calm. While electrified the pool is hazardous like slag but only to a submerged player (`ELECTRIC_CONTACT_DAMAGE`, 1 per `take_hit()`, paced by its i-frames, in every held state), and a surfer cannot skim it (no skim floor is raised, so a board over it falls in, ends the surf and is hurt). It affects nothing else: floating blocks, flowing particles, the basin sensor and power circuits do not read it. The look is placeholder code in `_draw_electric()` (flickering tint, bright surface line, zigzag arcs, a bolt at the strike point, tapering over the last `ELECTRIC_FADE_TIME`); `_process` stays on while it runs. **Frozen crust** (the freeze ray, roadmap N3.2): a second **kind** of crust, formed by `WaterBasin.freeze()` instead of the water/slag reaction, recorded in the saved `crust_frozen` flag (`is_frozen_crust()`). `FreezeTurret` hands its beam to `WaterManager.freeze_along_beam(points, delta)`, which, for every basin whose `can_freeze()` holds (the authored `freezable` export on, liquid present, no crust) and whose liquid the beam crosses (the same polygon test as boiling), calls `WaterBasin.add_frost(delta)`; the basin keeps a runtime `_frost` (seconds of beam, decaying at `FROST_DECAY_PER_SEC` once the beam leaves, tracked by physics frame so turret order never matters) and freezes at the authored `freeze_time` (default 2 s). `freeze()` is valid for any crust-free basin that holds liquid, **water or slag, electrified or not** (an electrified pool keeps its remaining time on the crust), and forms the same slab as the reaction crust (top face at the liquid surface, `CRUST_THICKNESS_TILES` thick, the liquid between the faces becomes crust, the liquid under it stays) with no opposite liquid and **no steam**; the cooling animation uses the frozen skin of the basin's liquid (`FROZEN_CRUST_SKIN_PATH_BY_TYPE`: water `sprites/liquids/spsh_freezing.png`, a liquid-blue-to-pale-ice palette; slag `sprites/liquids/spsh_freezing_steel.png`, molten-glow-to-pale-ice; a missing file falls back to that liquid's own crust art, then to the flat `FROZEN_CRUST_COLOR`). A frozen crust is otherwise an ordinary crust (solid, walkable, hangable from beneath over a void, read as `CRUST` by a basin sensor), except that it is **slippery underfoot**: the crust's `CrustBody` answers the player's `is_slippery()` floor contract (true only for a frozen crust, so a reaction crust is ordinary ground) and the player gets the ice-rink acceleration and friction of an ice block (the surf's friction scale is not changed). **Frozen slag is temporary**: `LiquidProfile.frozen_is_temporary` is set for slag only, so the crust body forwards `on_freeze_hit()` to `on_crust_freeze_hit()`, which stamps `_freeze_hold_frame`; `THAW_GRACE_FRAMES` (4) physics frames after the last hit, `_begin_thaw()` starts a **thaw that plays the cooling frames backwards**: `_thawing` is set and `_crust_cool_t` falls from where it stood (the last frame for a fully cold crust, the reached frame for one still freezing) at the freezing speed, the crust staying **solid** the whole time; `_advance_skin()` calls `thaw()` only when the clock reaches 0. A freeze hit while `_thawing` clears it and the cooling resumes forward from the same frame. **Any frozen crust that has not reached its final cooling frame is held the same way** (`is_temporary_frozen()`: the liquid's profile says temporary, or `_crust_cool_t < CRUST_COOL_TIME`, or a player is embedded, since an embedded player holds the animation on the frame before the last): an interrupted water freeze, including one still holding a swimming player at the moment the beam is redirected, reverses and thaws. A frozen water crust that has reached its final frame is settled and stays until a laser melts it. A held freeze is saved as the plain liquid; `_thawing` is runtime-only and cleared by `thaw()`, `convert_to_crust()` and `load_state()`. It differs from a reaction crust in three further ways. **A laser thaws it**: the crust's body is a small `CrustBody` class whose `on_laser_hit()` forwards to `on_crust_laser_hit()`; the basin keeps a runtime `_heat` (rising per frame of beam, decaying like frost) and at the authored `melt_time` (default 2 s) `_begin_thaw()` starts the same reversed cooling animation (from the last frame for a settled crust), the crust staying solid until it reaches the first frame, when `thaw()` removes the whole crust and its collision and returns the band of liquid it held (clamped to capacity), with no steam (frozen slag is molten slag again); the thaw lasts only while the laser stays on the crust: once it has been off for `BEAM_GRACE_FRAMES` the thaw is interrupted and the cooling resumes forward from the same frame (`_thaw_by_heat`; the crust is then `_beam_exempt`, so it finishes cooling without a freeze beam, except frozen slag, which is held by a beam as always), `_heat` is capped at `melt_time` and decays from there, and a freeze hit on the crust interrupts the thaw too (and resets `_heat`); a reaction crust ignores the laser and the beam just stops on it. **It conducts**: `try_electrify_at()` accepts a strike at or below a frozen crust's top face over a column that carries crust (a reaction crust, and any slag, still refuse); while electrified, the flash is drawn along the crust's top and `_poll_electric_crust()` (physics, only while electrified) calls `take_hit(ELECTRIC_CONTACT_DAMAGE)` on a player standing on it (on the floor, feet within `FROZEN_STAND_TOLERANCE` of the collision top, over a crust column), paced by the player's i-frames; a player in or under the crust is hurt by the electrified liquid through the swim check instead. **It never seals the player in**: `freeze()` latches the player as **embedded** (`_latch_embedded()`, set once, at the freeze) if any part of its collider is below the crust's collision top over a crust column. The crust's body then ignores the player (`add_collision_exception_with()` on the static body, honoured by the player's `move_and_slide()`), the player's swim check measures depth against the crust's top face (`get_swim_surface_world_y()`, so the band counts as water: the player swims up through it at chilled speed and leaves swimming at the top), the player is chilled (`apply_chill(1.0)`) while any of it overlaps the band, and `_tick_embedded()` ends the latch, making the crust solid for the player, once the player's centre is above the crust's top face or it has left the crust's columns. While a player is embedded the cooling animation holds on the frame before the last, and the last frame shows when the latch ends. Frost, heat, the latch and electrification are runtime only; `load_state()` clears them and restores `crust_frozen` (a save without the flag is a reaction crust). Basin physics processing is enabled only while frost, heat, an embedded player or an electrified frozen crust needs it (`_update_physics_state()`).
