# Puzzle primitives and concepts

The small reusable patterns (logic, spatial, timing, resource) that the current objects can build, and a catalogue of puzzle concepts that combine them. **The concepts are design proposals derived from the documented rules, not playtested**; one that leans on behaviour the docs only imply is marked **(untested)**, and the mark is removed once it has been tried in a level. What each object does, its knobs and its limits are in `level-design.md`; read `objon.md` (the core: setup, layers and groups, contracts, invariants, docs index) first.

Covers: Puzzle primitives (Logic; Spatial; Timing; Resource); Puzzle concepts (A. Light, mirrors and receivers; B. Water, slag and steam; C. Weight, balance and swinging; D. Momentum and the surf; E. Traversal and spatial; F. Timing, rhythm and traps; G. Enemies as mechanisms; H. Information, memory, routing and pacing; I. Full-room recipes)

## Puzzle primitives

The atoms. Each can be built from the objects in the Object catalogue (`level-design.md`).

**Logic**
- **AND**: several sources on one target with `all_required`. Receivers and plates are best (steady). For arcs/bullets use TIMED latch.
- **OR**: several sources, default. Gives alternate solutions for free.
- **NOT**: `invert` on the device or target. A door that closes when you press the button; a floor that vanishes when you press it (an inverted catwalk layer).
- **Latch / memory**: TOGGLE button, TOGGLE bullet/arc target, a reaction crust (never thins; a frozen crust is the version a laser can undo, so a CRUST sensor over a `freezable` basin is a latch with a reset), a jam (latched until power cycle), a see-saw with `restoring_torque` 0 that stays at the stop a steam vent drove it to until a load or a vent under its other end pushes it back (C13), or a **Latch node**: set by one source and cleared by another, so a source that can only set and a different one that can only clear are separate things, and the reset wins while it is held.
- **Dwell (leaky integrator)**: a freeze receiver or a `freezable` basin only acts after the beam has rested on it for `dwell_time` / `freeze_time`, and what it has built decays 1 s per second once the beam is gone. A brief sweep pays nothing, a beam that leaves and returns keeps the remainder, and the dwell is set on the target, so one turret serves targets with different patience. It is the Delay node's cousin for a beam: that one forgets the instant its input drops, this one drains slowly (A12, A13).
- **Timer**: TIMED button / speed trap `hold_time` / receiver `hold_time` / melting ice on a plate / a zone sensor's `hold_time`, which keeps it on for that long after the last body leaves (a delay on leaving, where a TIMED button is a delay after a press); a **Delay node**'s `on_delay` and `off_delay` on any source; a **Pulse node**'s `duration`.
- **Presence and absence**: a zone sensor is on while `min_count` bodies of the enabled kinds (player, enemies, blocks) are inside it, so `invert` makes it absence ("the room is empty", "nobody is in the shelter"). It sums across kinds, so "the player and a block together" is `min_count` 2 with both on, and an enemy can power it (a weight plate also reads enemies, but by weight and only on the floor, so a flying Stingbot counts in a zone and not on a plate).
- **Edge, not level**: a dispenser spawns on a rising edge only, so a laser, plate or switch that stays on gives one block, not a stream, and the player has to make a new edge for the next (a momentary pad, a TIMED button running out, the crouch-cycle). `invert` moves the spawn to the falling edge, so a block can arrive when the player *lets go* (C9). A Counter in front of it makes every Nth edge count.
- **Counter / sequence**: a step-mode mirror turning one step per rising edge. Aim at the receiver only at step N. Or a **Counter node**: on after N rising edges, with a reset and an optional auto-reset. It has no art, so unlike the mirror's angle the count is invisible unless something visible follows it (A2).
- **Filter (delay on arriving)**: a Delay node's `on_delay` passes the input only if it stays on the whole time, so a plank that bobs, a plate a block rattles on or a zone a player runs across never pays, and one that is held does. A zone's `hold_time` is the delay on *leaving*; this is the delay on *arriving* (H8).
- **Stretch and single shot**: a Pulse node turns any source (a receiver that lit for a blink, a plate, a zone) into an event of fixed length, whatever the input does next (A5). Shorter than a turret's `fire_rate` or `fire_interval`, it makes a powered cannon or arc turret fire once per edge however long the source stays on; `invert_input` makes the *fall* the trigger.
- **Order**: a chain of Latches, each taking the one before it as a second source with `all_required`, accepts A then B then C and ignores anything out of order (H7). Mind **which versus how many**: a Counter counts edges, so one pad pressed three times and three pads pressed once each both reach 3, where an AND of Latches asks *which* ones.
- **Interlock**: two Latches each with the other in its `reset_sources`; the first one set wins and locks the other out until a shared reset (H9). It is a loop of logic nodes, so the Inspector warns about it.
- **Selector (two-way route)**: a toggle-mode mirror sends one beam to one of two places by the *state* of a source (powered = aim 1, unpowered = aim 0), so exactly one of two receivers is lit and the other is dark (A8). Mirrors in series make a truth table out of geometry: the beam arrives only when every mirror is on the right aim (A11). Unlike the counter it needs no clicks to be counted, because the mirror's angle always shows the source's state.
- **Pulse**: MOMENTARY target; a cannon firing faster than `pulse_length` holds it on.
- **Analogue threshold with hysteresis**: angle sensor, basin sensor, weight plate. Can't chatter. A plate sums the player (2), blocks (ice 1, metal 4, carbon fiber 0.5 per tile, so a carbon fiber block's size is its weight: 1×1 is 0.5, 2×1 is 1, 2×2 is 2) and enemies (grunt, drillbot and crawler 2; mine and landed Stingbot 1) in one reading, so the threshold decides **who can pay**: at 2 a lone grunt, crawler, drillbot, player or metal block does and a lone ice block, 1×1 carbon fiber block or mine does not; at 3 or 4 it takes two payers.
- **Comparator / XOR (a two-sided plank)**: a see-saw with an angle sensor on each side (`side` CLOCKWISE and COUNTER_CLOCKWISE) reads **which of two inputs is larger**. The two sensors in OR read "not balanced" and the same two into an inverted target read "balanced". The inputs can be weights, poured liquid and, with `steam_torque_scale`, a vent under each end, so two beams or two buttons make an XOR or an XNOR out of physics, with no logic node and a state the player can see (C12). The plank's thresholds (15° with 3° of hysteresis) absorb the steam's noise; do not set a sensor near level. **(untested)**
- **Clock**: crusher/arc/cannon/rail bot cycles with `phase_offset`; a patrolling crawler or rail bot crossing a zone sensor (G8); a motorized see-saw read by an angle sensor produces a square wave, and with `launch_when_motorised` it also throws its rider at every stop **(untested)**; a see-saw lifted by a boil column whose angle sensor switches that laser off is a clock with no motor, one that burns water as fuel (B9, H10) **(untested)**.

**Spatial**
- **Gate**: door, breakable barrier, crust (a frozen one is a gate the player can open with a beam and a laser can shut again), mirror-shielded beam, a retractable spike field (a gate that hurts instead of blocking, so it also works on a surf route), a missing bridge or ladder (a gate that opens by *creating* a route).
- **Gap crossing**: a see-saw throw (the catapult), catwalk hang, crust bridge, frost bridge, ice raft, slag raft, a carbon fiber raft that does not melt, a pushed block as a step, a steam-lifted block as a lift, swing, rail slide, ramp-and-skim, pad, a summoned bridge.
- **Held route**: frozen slag lasts only as long as the beam rests on it and thaws within a few frames of its leaving, so a crossing that exists only while something holds the beam (a block on a button, a plate, a mirror kept still). The route is the dual of a TIMED bridge: it is there *while a condition is kept*, not for a fixed time (B10).
- **Slick floor**: a frozen crust is ice-rink ground for a walker, so a stretch of it keeps the player's momentum and needs room to stop. A surfer is not affected (B10, D5).
- **Summoned route**: a powered catwalk or ladder layer is terrain that exists only while a source says so. It extends at once and harmlessly, and the event is the *retract*: whatever stands on it drops, a hanger is released, a climber falls. `invert` turns it round: a floor a source takes away.
- **Foreshadowed terrain**: `show_ghost` draws a retracted layer faintly where it will be, so the player can plan a route through terrain that is not there yet. It says *where*, never *when*. With it off the same layer is a hidden route, a secret rather than a promise.
- **Alternation**: two layers on one source, one of them inverted, are never both out except during the blink: the one that is powering up extends at once and the other holds for `warn_time`. That second is a handoff window in both directions; with `warn_time` 0 it is a hard swap.
- **Occupied slot**: a dispenser refuses to make a block while a tile, the player, an enemy or a block is inside the block's footprint at the spawn point, and answers only with a sound. A block left under it, or the player standing there, is a lock the player has to clear first; a spawn point over a drop is one that never locks (C9, E11).
- **Pushed block**: a carbon fiber block is the one block the player moves by walking, so it is the only block that can be *placed* exactly. A push runs at up to 75 px/s (half walk speed) and the player is slowed to match, the block stops dead when the push ends on an ordinary floor (a slippery floor, air and liquid let it coast), and a kick sends it about four tiles, so push is for precision and kick for distance. A chain of up to 8 pushes together, slower per block; an ice or metal block, a tile or an enemy in the line refuses the lot (E13); a crouched or sneaking player never pushes, so a low corridor cannot be pushed through. A wall ahead refuses the push, and a drop ahead lets the block fall off the edge like any block.
- **One-way push**: there is no pull, so every push is a commitment: a block pushed into a corner or against a wall stays there unless the player can get round to its other side (a catwalk, a ladder, a hang) or a dispenser makes another. That makes the *order* of pushes the puzzle, and a dispenser the undo (E12).
- **Bullet cover**: a cannon's bullet no longer flies through blocks. Metal absorbs it for good (a permanent shield, the bullet counterpart of A3's laser shield), ice and carbon fiber break on the first hit. So a block in a cannon's lane is a decision about how long the cover must last (F8).
- **Steam lift**: a column of steam carries a carbon fiber block up at up to 80 px/s, to the column's top (about 8 tiles above its source) or a ceiling, and sets it down when the steam stops. A surfer already rides the same column (D6), so one source serves a surfer's route and a block's, and a plank above it can take the column's push (B13). The size of the lift is a **weight per tile** question, so a 1×1, 2×1 and 2×2 carbon fiber block rise alike while ice and metal do not (a level opts them in; ice only slows its fall). It is **acceleration with a load limit**: one rider (the player or an enemy standing on it) slows the block to about 19 px/s and two ground it, so *how many are aboard* is a dial. The source is a switch when it is a laser boil (a button, a mirror aim, a plate) and a one-off when it is a crust reaction. Steam dies on the first solid it meets and so ends under the block, so a block cuts the column above it and only the lowest of a stack reads it; a catwalk lets it through. A block's size sets how much of a narrow column it needs: half its underside width reads full. **(untested)**
- **Steam push**: a see-saw with `steam_torque_scale` above 0 is lifted by steam that dies on its **underside**, about 1.17 torque per pixel of lever arm for every particle. So the lever arm is the dial: a column under the pivot does nothing, one near the tip pushes hardest, and columns under opposite ends oppose. Arrivals add and fade over 0.3 s, and at scale 1 a full default boil (about 16 particles a second) at the tip of a 2-tile plank is about 45, between the player's weight there (32) and a 1×1 metal block's (64). At the same lever arm a standing player's weight is matched at about 11 particles a second at scale 1 (each particle a second pushes 0.18 per pixel; the player weighs 2 per pixel), so a full boil under the player lifts them and a half-width one does not. It is a lift only and cannot press a plank down, so the way back is a load, the plank's spring or a vent under the other end. The plank shadows what is above it, and a block in the column shadows the plank: a carbon fiber block over the vent is a **valve** that the column lifts to the plank and that keeps the steam off it (B13). At the 25° stop an end rises only about 0.4 of the plank's half-length, so the plank is a step or a trigger, not an elevator (B11 is the elevator). **(untested)**
- **Keyhole**: a corridor only a crouch/sneak fits, a catwalk only a hang passes, a door you can't hang from.
- **Trip zone**: an invisible rectangle that reacts to a body being somewhere (a sealed room, an ambush, a camera look-ahead, a tripwire). Make it deeper than the fastest body moves in a frame (10 to 13 px for a surf), and give the player something visible to read, since the zone itself is neither drawn nor heard.
- **Catapult**: a see-saw with `launch_gain` throws what rests on its raised end when the far end drops onto a stop. The reach is proportional to where the thrower stands (its distance from the pivot) and to how fast the plank arrives, so a heavy drop is a long throw and the player's position is a dial. It flings you up and back toward the end that came down, so the landing is on the drop's side of the plank. **(untested)**
- **Weighted lever**: see-saw with a threshold sensor. With `enemy_weight_scale` above 0 on the plank, an enemy is a load too (C8), and with `steam_torque_scale` above 0 steam under an end lifts it, so a boiled pool is a drive for the plank as well as a weight (B13).
- **Aiming**: mirror steps, a toggle-mode mirror's two aims (a sweep the player starts, A10), a tilted mirror see-saw, a moving platform with a mirror, liquid poured as a variable load.

**Timing**
- **Telegraphed cycle**: crusher (dwell windows), arc turret glow, rail bot glow, grunt windup.
- **Time-to-live**: ice (2 s in a beam), metal-on-slag (8 s), carbon fiber has none (a block with no clock, removed only by a bullet, a crusher, a fall or a dispenser), a block held up by steam (it is held only as long as the column, about 0.3 s after the steam stops), flipped crawler (4 s), drop platform (1 s), TIMED hold, a powered bridge (TIMED `duration` plus the layer's `warn_time`).
- **Heat lag**: steam rises at 80 px/s, so every tile between a boiled pool and a plank adds 0.2 s before the push starts, the push fades over 0.3 s after the beam stops, and the plank's own inertia and spring come on top. The gap from pool to plank is a delay a designer sets by placing the pool (up to the steam's reach of about 8 tiles), and it is why a heat-driven door lags its button (B13, B9). **(untested)**
- **Race**: a TIMED button and a door/route you have to reach before it resets.
- **Warned retract**: a catwalk or ladder layer blinks at 5 Hz for `warn_time` (1 s) before it goes, still solid and still climbable, and a power return during the blink cancels it. A TIMED button pulses in its own last second as well, so a bridge has two tells in a row. The spike layer has no such phase.
- **Unannounced edge**: a powered spike layer extends with no warning phase, so the telegraph has to be built around it: the player's own action that causes it, a visible bullet or beam on its way, a camera cue, or a place to stand that is never spiked. A zone sensor adds an edge the player cannot see at all, so a zone-driven trap uses one of the same telegraphs and puts the zone's edge well before the hazard.

**Resource**
- **A block as tool**: shield, weight, bridge, plug, battery of mass, the hammer that fires a catapult, or (ice, which takes the throw whole) the projectile it throws. The three blocks are a choice of **lifespan and weight**: ice lasts about 2 s in a beam and weighs 1 per tile, metal about 3 s and weighs 4, carbon fiber *forever* and weighs 0.5. A permanent block turns a puzzle about timing into one about placement and about what can still destroy it (a bullet, a crusher, a pit, a dispenser).
- **A block on tap**: a block dispenser turns the one-use block into a budget. `max_alive` is the budget (1 means a new block takes back the old one wherever the player left it), `cooldown` the rate limit, and the spawn area has to be free. It makes the *consequence* of using a block cheap, so a puzzle can ask the player to spend one, lose one or place one where it will be taken back (A3, C9, C9, E11).
- **A liquid as tool**: load, crust-maker, steam-maker, lift (of a surfer, of a carbon fiber block and of one end of a plank), aim, and (with a freeze beam) a floor that can be summoned and, with a laser, taken away again.
- **A slowdown as tool**: chill halves walk, swim and every enemy's speed while a beam is on them, without hurting. It stretches an enemy clock (G8) and makes a walker's crossing longer, and it never touches a surfer or a launch.
- **An enemy as tool**: a key (parked at a ledge, standing in a zone), a clock (a fixed patrol that crosses a zone every lap), a counter (the zone stays occupied until the last one is dead), or a stepping stone (a flipped crawler), or a weight (parked on a plate, or tipping a plank it opts into). Killing it is then a choice with a cost.
- **Momentum as tool**: surf speed that is *banked* over a stretch and *spent* on a barrier, trap or launch.

## Puzzle concepts

Each concept lists **ingredients** and how it plays, then **twists** to scale difficulty. They're grouped by what the player has to *think about*. Many combine across groups; the last section has full-room recipes.

### A. Light, mirrors and receivers

**A1. Mirror relay.** *Laser turret + 2 to 4 mirrors + laser receiver + door.* The player kicks mirrors (45° steps) until the beam reaches the receiver. *Twists:* a mirror on a **moving platform** sweeps the beam, so the receiver only lights during a window (needs `hold_time` to bridge); a mirror the player can't reach is turned by a **button's rising edge** (step mode) or aimed by its state (toggle mode, A8) instead; set `kick_rotates` false on switch-owned mirrors; the beam crosses glass and catwalks so the solution can be visible from afar.

**A2. Combination lock.** *Mirror on power + a momentary button + receiver.* Each *click* (crouch while standing on the pad cycles it) advances the mirror one step; the receiver is lit only at the right step. The player has to count clicks from the layout. *Twists:* two mirrors on two buttons, both must align (AND of two receivers); a toggle button instead teaches that only rising edges count; a mirror past the receiver position forces going all the way around (8 steps). *Counter version (no mirror):* a Counter node (`count_to` N) behind the pad does the counting. Stepping onto the pad is a rising edge and so is each crouch on it (the crouch-cycle), so the step-on is click 1 and every crouch after it adds one; stepping off and back on counts again. The Counter is invisible and silent, so what the player sees has to come from somewhere else: a **ladder of Counters** (`count_to` 1, 2, 3 on the same pad, each driving a powered gem) puts one more gem on screen per click, which the mirror's angle gave for free. *Twists:* `auto_reset_time` shuts the door that long after the Nth click, a timed unlock (a door never crushes and waits if someone is in it); a reset pad in `reset_sources` holds the count at 0 and ignores clicks while it is pressed, so a decoy pad on the route wipes the tally; a TOGGLE button counts only its turn-ons, so a click costs two presses; `invert_input` counts the step-*off* instead; a MOMENTARY bullet target counts cannon shots, so the player has to stop the cannon (a mirror, a barrier) after exactly N. Several pads in one `sources` list count as one pad pressed that many times, so "press all three" needs Latches in AND (H7), not a Counter. **(untested)**

**A3. Block shield.** *Laser + a block + a thing that must not be lit, or a laser receiver on an inverted door.* A block in a beam is a shield, and its **lifespan is the clock**: ice gives about 2 s then a puddle (and refreezes when shaded), metal about 3 s then pours slag, and melting spawns the liquid at the block's position. Carbon fiber never melts, so its shield is permanent and the question becomes whether the block can be *got* there and *kept* (a bullet, a crusher or a fall removes it). A level that wants a disposable shield offers no carbon fiber. The shield can protect a target directly (a receiver, a hazard, a freeze receiver as a cold shield) or open a gate: a receiver lit by the beam on an inverted door keeps the door shut, so a block on a shelf in the beam shades the receiver and the door opens for as long as the shield lasts plus the receiver's `hold_time` after it melts. *Twists:* the shield stands on a see-saw (the melt shifts the load); metal melts onto a lower basin and crusts it; the player must *carry* the shield forward by kicking it (ice slides about 4 tiles, metal about 1). *Re-armable window:* one block is one window, and a block dispenser (ice, `max_alive` 1, on a button) makes the window repeatable: each press puts a new shield on the shelf, a melted one frees its slot, and there is never more than one. The player presses, then runs, so the length of the corridor is the length of the window. Twists: a `cooldown` longer than the window leaves a closed gap between windows that the player has to cover; two receivers in AND, so one shield is not enough; the button is on the far side of the door. **(untested)** *Standing shadow (carbon fiber):* the same door with no clock. A block pushed onto the shelf shades the receiver and the door opens, and the window does not close by itself, so the puzzle is no longer how long the window lasts but whether the block can be got to the shelf and kept there: it is pushed (so the approach has to leave room to walk up behind it and a floor that stops it dead), and anything that breaks it re-shuts the door (a cannon in the lane, a crusher, a fall). Twists: a cannon fires across the shelf, so the shield needs a shield: a metal block in front of it absorbs the bullets (F8) and the player has to place both; the shelf is the far end of a one-way push (E12), so a wrong first push closes the door for good; two receivers on two beams in AND need two blocks, and carbon fiber is the one that can be pushed to the second; the receiver is a freeze receiver (A12) and the block is a *cold* shield that never melts. **(untested)** *Raised shadow:* a boil turret (`boils_water`) on a switch, a boilable pool with a carbon fiber block on a catwalk shelf over it, a ceiling over the shaft and a second laser across the top of the shaft. The block rests below the beam, so the receiver is lit and the door is shut. The switch fires the boil turret, the column carries the block up to the ceiling where its body is across the second beam, the receiver goes dark and the door opens for as long as the steam lasts (and the receiver's `hold_time`). Cut the boil and the block sinks out of the beam about 0.3 s later, so the route is a held one (B10's idea, in light): the player has to run while something keeps the switch. A block never melts, so only the steam can remove the shadow. Twists: the switch is a MOMENTARY plate and a second block holds it, so the player has to deliver two blocks (D4); the pool drains as it boils (B5), so the shadow has a fuel budget (H10); the player wants to ride the block up to reach something beside the beam, but one rider slows the lift to about 19 px/s and two ground it, so riding and shading at once is slower than the shadow alone, and a grunt that climbs on grounds the block and shuts the door again. **(untested)**

**A4. Beam-and-block race.** *Laser aimed at a block that holds a button or plate down + an inverted spike layer or a door.* The block melts and the plate releases, so a timer is built from the physics. A source that is on retracts an inverted layer, so a block left on a plate (threshold above the player's own weight) holds the field open, and kicking it off re-arms the spikes on that frame: the player has to bring the block to the plate, then cross. Ice under the beam makes the window the melt time (about 2 s lit), so the player has to run; metal lasts about 3 s and then pours slag onto the plate. *Twists:* a mirror lets the player re-aim the beam to buy time (shade it, let it cool); two blocks on one plate are required together, or a second plate in AND needs a second block; a carbon fiber block takes the clock out (A3). The spike-layer version is **(untested)**.

**A5. Window receiver.** *Moving platform with a mirror, or a Stingbot crossing the beam, + laser receiver + a gate.* The receiver lights briefly each pass. With a short `hold_time` the player has to be at the door when it's lit; with a longer one it's an easy gate. Stingbots flicker receivers behind them by design (their `hold_time` covers it). Used on purpose, a stingbot is a *clock*. What the receiver drives decides the puzzle. *Window floor:* an inverted spike layer on the route is retracted only while the receiver is lit, so the player waits for the platform to come round and crosses before the beam moves on; a glass wall lets them see the receiver they are waiting on (A7). *Sweep bridge:* a powered catwalk layer over a pit instead. The bridge is out for the lit time plus the receiver's `hold_time` plus the layer's `warn_time`, so the blink is a telegraph that the spike field lacks: the player crosses while it is solid and hurries when it blinks. A laser passes catwalks, so the bridge never shades its own beam and the beam can run along the span in plain sight; `warn_time` 0 takes the telegraph away, and `show_ghost` on teaches where the bridge will be, off makes the player read it from the beam. *Fixed-length event:* a **Pulse** (`duration` 4) behind the receiver (`hold_time` 0) turns each crossing into a 4 s event, so the window is a number the designer picks rather than a product of the platform's speed or the scanner's sweep (A10), and a second crossing within the 4 s restarts the time with no new edge. *Twists:* a longer `hold_time` makes it forgiving; two receivers on two staggered platforms in AND make the window rarer; a Pulse shorter than a cannon's `fire_rate` on a held plate gives one shot per step-on however long the player stands; `invert_input` on the Pulse fires as the player steps *off*, so the bullet is already in flight as they run from the pad (F7); with `all_required` over two sources the Pulse fires only when both have come on, an AND that gives an event and not a level. **(untested)**

**A6. Arc ricochet.** *Arc turret + mirrors + arc receiver.* The arc is slow (220 px/s) and has finite range (480 px), so the path length matters. It passes enemies and the player's layer, so it can run through a room unseen. *Twists:* two arc receivers on TIMED latches (AND); `phase_offset` to stagger turrets; an arc that fizzles because the path is too long teaches range; water on the route electrifies the pool.

**A7. Shoot-through-a-wall.** *Glass wall + laser receiver on the far side.* Light is visible but unreachable until a door opens or a mirror is repositioned. Same for arcs. Reads well as a puzzle statement ("the receiver is right there").

**A8. Beam switch.** *Laser turret + toggle-mode mirror + two laser receivers + a TOGGLE button + two doors.* Aim 0 sends the beam to receiver A, aim 1 to receiver B, and the button picks. The mirror follows the button's state, so its angle always shows that state and there are no clicks to count (the contrast with A2). One beam lights one receiver at a time, so doors A and B are never both open: an either/or gate for free. *Twists:* each receiver's `hold_time` (0.3 s) keeps its door open a beat after the beam leaves, a short overlap for a player who flips and runs (0 removes it); `invert` swaps which state means A; with `kick_rotates` on, a kick sets the aim until the button next changes, so one mirror is aimed by kick or by switch; a third receiver on the sweep between the aims lights as the beam passes, as a blip at `hold_time` 0 and a window at the default. **(untested)**

**A9. Hold the beam.** *MOMENTARY button + toggle-mode mirror + laser receiver + a door + an ice or metal block.* The beam reaches the receiver only while the button is down, and the player cannot stand on the pad and be at the door, so a block has to hold it: kick one onto the pad (a block as tool). The crouch-cycle that advances a step-mode mirror (A2) does nothing to a toggle-mode one, so the pad has to stay held. *Twists:* the block is ice under a second laser (A4), so it melts and releases the pad after about 2 s and the mirror swinging back is the visible countdown on a route that closes itself again; a metal block lasts longer but has to be got to the pad; two pads on two mirrors in series (A11) need two blocks. **(untested)**

**A10. Scanner.** *Toggle-mode mirror with a long `rotate_time` (1 to 2 s) + laser + a laser receiver between the two aims + a button + a door.* Pressing the button swings the beam across the room and over the receiver, and the door is open for the pass plus `hold_time`. Where A5 waits for a platform, the player starts the sweep and can run it again by stepping off and on. Design around three details: the turn eases in and out, so the beam is slowest near its aims and fastest mid-sweep (a receiver mid-sweep needs a longer `hold_time`); a power change mid-sweep turns the beam back from where it is, so releasing early either sends it back over the receiver (a second window) or stops it short of one near the far aim; and the sweep runs the numeric way, so 0 to 270 scans the long way round and 0 to -90 the short way. *Twists:* two receivers at different angles on one door in AND, so one sweep must pass both inside the hold. **(untested)**

**A11. Optical gate.** *Laser + two toggle-mode mirrors in series + two sources (buttons, plates or sensors) + one receiver.* The first mirror sends the beam on to the second only at its aim 1, and the second sends it to the receiver only at its aim 1, so the receiver is lit only with both sources on: an AND made of geometry, steady and visible, with one receiver and no latch. The other three states end at a dead wall, the mirror's back or a decoy. The player reads the beam to see which mirror is wrong, a two-by-two truth table with exactly one answer where A2 needed counting. *Twists:* `invert` on one wire turns it into "press A, release B"; put a decoy receiver at each wrong endpoint, wired to something that bites (a spike layer, a crusher); a third mirror makes eight states and one answer; leave `kick_rotates` on and a mirror whose button is across a gap can be kicked into place instead (it stays until that button next changes). **(untested)**

**A12. Patient receiver.** *Freeze turret + freeze receiver (`dwell_time` 3, `hold_time` 0) + a patrolling grunt or crawler that crosses the beam + a door.* The enemy is opaque to the beam, so each time it walks through, the receiver goes dark and loses frost at 1 s per second. A laser receiver would follow every gap; this one pays only for a gap longer than its dwell, so the patrol's rhythm decides whether the door opens. Tune the dwell against the lane's clear time, and the player's tools are the kick (a flipped crawler stops, but still stands in the beam) and killing the patrol. *Twists:* a second beam from a mirror covers the same receiver from another side, so the enemy has to be in both to cut it; a Delay node in front of the receiver adds a second, invisible wait. *Visible patience:* a Delay node's wait is invisible (H8), but a dwell shows itself: the receiver's lens brightens as frost builds and the frost drains when the beam leaves, so the player can watch the wait being earned or lost. Use it wherever the puzzle is "keep it there" and the player should know it is working. The lens is placeholder art drawn in code; a real sprite is what this signal needs before it is more than a prototype. **(untested)**

**A13. Fire and ice.** *Laser turret + freeze turret + laser receiver + freeze receiver (`dwell_time` 2) + a door on AND (`all_required`) + a toggle-mode mirror both beams use.* The two receivers ask for different things: the laser receiver wants a touch, the freeze receiver wants the beam to rest. One mirror in both paths is the catch, as in I5: aim 0 sends the laser to its receiver and the freeze beam elsewhere, aim 1 the reverse, and neither beam can hold its target while the mirror swings. The solve is a second, fixed route for one of them (a second mirror, a repositioned turret), found by reading which target is impatient. *Twists:* the freeze receiver's `hold_time` keeps the door open for a moment after the swing, which is the window to reach it; the laser also melts an ice block the player was using as the freeze beam's shield (it has no lifespan against cold, only against heat). **(untested)**

### B. Water, slag and steam

**B1. Float up.** *Faucet + basin + ice block + a ledge above.* Fill the basin to float the block up to the ledge. Ice floats at about 80% submerged and follows the rising surface (even with riders). *Twists:* a metal block sinks, so it must be carried another way, while a carbon fiber block floats too (at 60%, a lower deck than ice) and never melts, so the lift lasts; `rest_fraction` past 1.0 means too heavy (a big block with riders sinks); a basin sensor opens a door at 50% fill and a drain brings the level back; riders are limited by `rider_load_tiles`. *Floating key:* a zone sensor (`count_player` and `count_blocks`, `min_count` 2) high in a shaft, where only a raised block can put the player. Fill the basin so the block floats up with the player riding it (a floating block follows the surface even with riders), and both are in the zone at once; either alone is a count of 1. A metal block sinks, so the lift is ice, which melts if a beam finds it, or carbon fiber, which does not. Shape the shaft so the player cannot jump into the zone from a ledge. A drain brings the level back, and the door closes `hold_time` after they drop out of the zone. Twists: `count_player` off makes the block a plug rather than a lift, so the player only has to get it up there; a basin sensor on the same basin in AND adds a fill condition; `hold_time` is the margin for hopping off at the top. **(untested)**

**B2. Crust bridge.** *Slag basin + water faucet.* Pouring water on slag crusts it into a walkable slab and makes steam. The player has to pour enough, in the right place. Pre-filled slag you can't cross becomes a floor. *Twists:* water has to come from a melting ice block (laser); the crust makes steam that is a **surf lift** column for the next section, or a lift for a carbon fiber block (B11); the steam rises into a ceiling gap and stops. *Crust latch:* a reaction crust never thins, so a CRUST basin sensor on it is a permanent latch, and a powered ladder or catwalk layer wired to it stays out for good once the pour has happened. One pour then gives two routes: the crust is a floor and a steam column for a surfer (D6), and a ladder appears for a walker. Crust fields are saved, so a restore keeps it. Twists: `invert` makes the same pour take a route away (the bridge you came by is gone once the slag is crusted), a point of no return the player chooses; the ladder is hidden until the pour (`show_ghost` off) so the player has to guess what the faucet does; a second basin chained after the first receives the overflow and has its own sensor in AND. *Cold tap:* an ice dispenser above the slag basin on a button makes the pour retryable. Ice in slag melts almost at once and bursts water onto the slag, which crusts it; one block was one try, and a try that lands badly (a basin still filling, the water landing where it is no use) was lost with it. Each press is another burst at the same spot, and the sensor latches the result. Twists: the spawn point is over the basin, so the dispenser's refusal rule is tested against liquid and not only tiles; a laser on the dispenser's own path melts the block early, so the pour lands off target; `max_alive` 1 means a block still floating when the next one is made is dissolved without a burst. **(untested)**

**B3. Under the crust.** *Crust + a drain + the crust underside hang.* Crust, then drain the liquid under it to open a void, then **hang and brachiate under the crust** across a hazard. The slag lake above is safe (crust), and the void below is the route. *Twists:* the void only exists while the drain is open; a basin sensor on CRUST powers the door that gives you access; a second basin chained after the first receives the overflow. *Frozen-water version:* no slag needed. A freezable water basin (`freeze_time` 2) and a freeze turret make the slab, then a drain (faucet) opens the void under it and the slab hangs over a pit that the underside hang crosses. A laser on a mirror that sweeps near the crust threatens it (`melt_time` of heat thaws the whole slab). The docs cover a hang letting go when liquid rises into the crust, not what a thaw does to a hanger, so check that in the editor before building anything on it. Twists: a basin sensor on CRUST powers a door that gives access, and it falls when the slab thaws (the resettable version of B2's latch); the drain is on a TIMED button so the void closes again. **(untested)**

**B4. Slag raft.** *Slag basin + metal block + a gap.* Metal floats on slag but lasts about 8 s. Kick it across and ride. A tight timeline. *Twists:* ice dropped on the slag instead bursts to water and crusts instead (the other solution); the raft is needed to *carry a weight* across onto a plate; a tread crawler flipped as a stepping stone is a free third option; carbon fiber is the same raft with the clock taken out (below). *Standing raft:* carbon fiber floats at 60% and never melts, so the raft is permanent and what the puzzle asks is *where it ends up*. The block can be pushed off the shore from the floor (it keeps its speed once it is in the liquid, and liquid drag then stops it within a few tiles) or kicked, so the width of the gap and what stops the block on the far side (a wall, a shelf, a second block) are the design. A floating block carries a rider up to its `rest_fraction`, and the player standing on it is standing above slag, so the block has to be big enough for the load (past 1.0 it sinks). Carbon fiber has no underside push, so unlike ice a swimmer cannot nudge it. Twists: the basin fills or drains and the raft rides the surface (B1); a second basin chained after it receives the overflow and has its own raft; a laser across the lane would melt ice or metal and does nothing to this block, so the beam is only a shadow; a bullet breaks it and a pit takes it, so one block is one try unless a dispenser is there (E12). Whether a rider standing on a drifting block can steer it is not documented, so test before building anything that needs it. **(untested)**

**B5. Evaporation.** *Laser (boils_water) + boilable basin + basin sensor.* Boiling drains the pool, so a pool that blocks the way becomes passable. The sensor releases a door when it's empty. The faucet refills it, and the pool *settles where the beam meets the surface*. *Twists:* a faucet-refilled pool that never empties unless the beam stays on; steam from boiling lifts a surfer, a carbon fiber block that is clear of the beam (B11) or one end of a see-saw plank (B13); slag is never boiled, so this only works on water.

**B6. Hydraulic scale.** *Water or slag poured onto a see-saw plank + angle sensor.* Liquid is load: water 1 per tile of volume, slag 3. A faucet pouring on one end tips the plank until the sensor trips. *Twists:* the plank has `mirror_surface` so the pour *aims a beam*; a laser melts an ice block on the plank so the load turns into water and keeps changing; a boil vent under the *loaded* end lifts against the pour, so the net is the pour minus the steam, where a vent under the other end tips the plank the same way as the pour (B13) **(untested)**.

**B7. Wet run-up.** *Melting ice on a slope + a surf.* Melt ice at the top of a slope: the water flows down and wets the floor, raising surf top speed from 600 to 800 and reducing friction. A barrier with `break_speed` 700 can only be broken on wet ground. *Twists:* the laser has to be redirected to start the melt; the water has to reach the floor or it dries (decays 0.4 s after leaving).

**B8. Safe water.** *Rail bot spark or arc turret over a pool.* The pool is electrified for 1 s after each strike. The swim is possible only in the gaps between strikes, and the telegraph glow tells you when. *Twists:* the pool can't be skimmed while electrified, so a surf over it fails; `conducts_sparks` off on a decoy basin; the strike comes from a ricocheting arc and electrifies a pool far from the turret. *Jump the pulse:* freeze the pool (a freezable water basin and a freeze turret) and the swim becomes a walk. A frozen water crust conducts: a spark or arc on its top electrifies it for `electrified_time`, and while it does the top hurts a player *standing on it*. Airborne is safe, so the crossing is a run of hops timed to the strikes, with the same telegraph glow. It is not the swim: a submerged player is hurt by the liquid either way, and the slab is slippery, so the landing skids. Slag does not conduct, so freezing the other basin makes a pure route. Twists: chill on the rail bot (G8) slows its travel, but its drip clock keeps its pace; a laser thawing the slab mid-crossing drops the player into the pool under it; the arc's ricochet strikes from a direction the player cannot see. **(untested)**

**B9. Relaxation oscillator.** *Basin + steady faucet + basin sensor FILL + drain faucet wired to the sensor.* The pool fills to 50%, the sensor opens the drain, it falls below the threshold minus hysteresis, the drain closes, and it refills. The result is a slow periodic signal driving doors, crushers, or a plate **(untested; the docs only guarantee one swing without inflow)**. *Thermal version:* a boilable pool + a laser (`boils_water`) + a see-saw above it (`steam_torque_scale` set, `restoring_torque` above 0) + an angle sensor on the plank that drives that same laser with `invert` on. Beam on, the pool boils, steam lifts the plank, the sensor trips at `threshold_degrees` and the inverted laser goes off, the steam fades, the spring brings the plank back, the sensor releases below the threshold minus its hysteresis and the beam returns. The loop closes through the plank and the steam rather than through a ring of logic nodes, so the editor has nothing to warn about, and it starts by itself: the sensor is off at load, so the inverted laser is on. The period is the plank's rise and return plus the heat lag (0.2 s per tile from pool to plank, 0.3 s of fade) and the hysteresis band, tuned with `inertia`, `restoring_torque`, `steam_torque_scale`, the gap and `hysteresis_degrees`. Whether it settles into a steady swing or hovers at the threshold with the beam flickering depends on those numbers, and the hysteresis is what stops a flicker, so widen it first. A second sensor on the same plank (or a Delay or Pulse on the first) taps the clock for a crusher, a door or powered gems. Twists: it burns water (1 volume a second while the beam is on), so with a faucet below that rate it is a clock with fuel that stops when the pool is dry (H10), a timer with a visible tank; a rider changes the period, so where the player stands tunes it; the beam crosses a corridor on the same beat, so the clock is also a hazard to time (F1); a checkpoint saves the plank's angle and not the steam, so a restore restarts the cycle from the saved angle once the column has rebuilt (about 1.6 s). **(untested)**

**B10. Frost bridge.** *Freezable water basin (`freeze_time` 2) + freeze turret (a button or a mirror aim) + a gap the pool fills.* The pool is a swim you do not want, or an electrified one. Hold the beam on it for the dwell and it becomes a walkable, **slippery** slab with no steam, which stays after the beam leaves (a laser, not time, is what removes a settled water crust). A walker keeps momentum across it, so it works as a gap crossing with a skid: build a spike pit or an edge at the far end, a place to stop, and the player has to ease off. A surfer crosses it at any speed. *Twists:* the pool is already electrified, and freezing it keeps the remaining time on the crust (B8); a mirror in the beam's path is turned by a see-saw, so the dwell is a patience test; two basins in one beam with different `freeze_time` freeze one after the other. *Held slab (slag):* the same beam on a freezable *slag* basin, with the freeze turret on a MOMENTARY button or plate. Frozen slag exists only while the beam stays on it: within a few frames of the beam leaving it begins to thaw, and the cooling plays backwards while staying solid until it reaches the first frame. So the crossing is a held bridge: drop an ice or metal block on the button (or plate) so the beam keeps resting, and run. The player's own weight on the button is the one thing that cannot be used, because it is the same player who has to cross. The reaction crust (B2) is the permanent answer to the same basin, so a level can offer both: slow and lasting, or quick and held. Twists: the plate's `hold_time` is the margin when the block is knocked off; the slab thaws along its way back, so a late runner steps onto crust that is reversing and stays solid until its first frame; an enemy that walks into the beam cuts it. **(untested)**

**B11. Steam elevator.** *Boilable water basin at the foot of a shaft + a laser turret (`boils_water`) on a TIMED button + a carbon fiber block (1×1 or 2×2) on a catwalk shelf over the pool + a ledge at the shaft's top out of jump reach.* Boiling the pool makes a column; the block rises through the shelf's catwalk and the player stands on it for the ride. A rider slows it: the lone player on a carbon fiber block rises at about 19 px/s (a full column carries the empty block at 80), so the ride is several seconds long and the button's `duration` has to cover it and the step onto the ledge. Two riders ground it, so a second body on the block (a grunt or crawler that has got on) makes it a one-person lift. When the steam ends the block falls and the player has to be off. The beam must reach the pool without crossing the block (a beam into the block is stopped, and a laser never melts carbon fiber, so nothing is lost but the boil), and the column starts at the point where the beam meets the liquid, which is narrow near its source: a 2×2 block reads the same column better than a 1×1 does and a crust reaction's wide base better still. *Twists:* the pool drains while it boils (1 volume per second by default, `boil_volume_scale`) and a faucet below that rate cannot keep it up, so each ride costs water and the ride is a budget (H10); a player hanging from the block is never counted as a rider, and a block stays hangable while it moves at 90 px/s or less (steam tops out at 80), so a hanger loads the lift like nothing and rises at the empty block's speed, which wants a test before a puzzle leans on it; the ledge at the top is under a ceiling, so the block is pinned there for as long as the steam lasts and the player steps off a pinned platform; a catwalk deck in the shaft lets the player hang under it and wait for the block; two columns side by side, one under each of two blocks, feed a ferry in both directions. The block also rides a crust-reaction column (B2): a pour of water onto slag makes steam only while the liquids are meeting, so the lift lasts as long as the pour and a restore after it leaves the block at the shaft's foot. Whether a rising block passes up through a catwalk and settles on it when the steam stops is not in the docs, so test that before building on it. **(untested)**

**B12. Width gate.** *A narrow boil column (a laser on a boilable pool) + carbon fiber blocks of different widths (1×1, 2×1, 4×1) + a ledge or a shaft that only the lifted one passes.* A block reads steam in a band across its whole underside, and a column that covers half that width reads full, so one narrow column lifts a 1×1 and thins out under a 4×1: the wide block gets only a partial reading, which slows its fall or hovers it and does not carry it. Column width grows with height (the cone is about 6° either way) and a crust reaction is wide at its base, so where the block meets the column matters as much as which block it is. A 2×2 and a 4×1 have the same area and the same limit, so width, not weight or area, is the lever. The player is shown three blocks and has to find which one the column lifts, and size is a key that no sensor reads. *Twists:* a dispenser with `size_tiles` set makes the right block on demand and the wrong one is dissolved by the next press; the gate is a shaft one tile wide, so only a 1×1 fits and the question is whether the column lifts it fast enough; a second column under the far end of a 4×1 lets it read full where one narrow column does not. How much width the lift needs at a given height is a placeholder in the water manager (`STEAM_LIFT_FULL_COVERAGE`), so measure it before sizing a gate. **(untested)**

**B13. Heat scale.** *Boilable water basin under one end of a see-saw (`steam_torque_scale` 1 or more) + a laser (`boils_water`) on a TIMED button or a mirror aim + an angle sensor on the plank + a door.* The beam makes steam at the pool, the steam dies on the plank's underside and lifts that end, and the sensor trips: a switch driven by heat, where nobody stands on anything. Because the push is a lift, the lever arm is the dial: a pool under the tip pushes hardest, one under the pivot pushes nothing, and the pool has to sit within the steam's reach (about 8 tiles) of the underside. The beam must reach the pool without crossing the plank, which stops it. A player on the lifted end rises with it if the column is full (a full boil beats a standing player's weight at the same spot, a half-width one does not), but an end rises only about 0.4 of the plank's half-length, so it is a step, not an elevator (B11). *Twists:* the plank returns by its spring after the steam fades, so a TIMED button's `duration` plus the heat lag (0.2 s per tile of rise, then 0.3 s of fade) is how long the door is held; the pool drains while it boils (1 volume a second by default), so a faucet below that rate makes each use a budget (H10) and a dry pool makes no steam; a metal block on the lifted end holds the plank down against a thin column and gives way to a full one; a carbon fiber block parked over the vent is a **valve**, because the column lifts it to the plank and it takes the steam that would have gone there, so the player has to kick it clear before the plank will move (what a block pinned under a plank does to the plank itself is not documented, so try the valve alone first); with `restoring_torque` 0 the plank stays where the steam left it (C13). **(untested)**

### C. Weight, balance and swinging

**C1. Counterweight.** *See-saw + metal block (weight 4 per tile) + angle sensor + door.* The player alone (weight 2) can't tip the plank, so the block must be positioned first and the player completes the load. *Twists:* the door only holds while the plank stays tipped, so something must be left on it; the plank's motor fights the player's weight and you only win while standing on the high end; the block is on the plank *and* the plate.

**C2. Plate budgeting.** *Weight plate with a threshold (e.g. 6) + blocks of several weights.* Weights: player 2, ice 1×1 = 1, metal 1×1 = 4 (a 2×1 metal is 8). The player needs to find a combination (metal block + player on top counts as stacked weight; 4 + 2 = 6; a carbon fiber block is only half a tile's worth per tile, so a 1×1 does not move a threshold of 2 on its own, see Light payers below). *Twists:* a plate under a melting ice block releases on its own (a timer); two plates wired AND; a plate that also *tips a see-saw* so the weight is spent twice; a grunt or crawler on the plate is worth 2, a payer you have to lure there (G7). *Light payers:* carbon fiber blocks in three authored sizes (1×1, 2×1, 2×2) on a plate at `threshold` 2. A carbon fiber block's weight is its area times 0.5, so the plate reads 0.5, 1 and 2: only the 2×2 pays alone, and a 1×1 or 2×1 needs a rider (the player standing on it adds 2) or another block stacked on it. The room shows three blocks and the player has to read the plate: which one is enough, which two are, and whether the rider is needed. Because the weight is in the size, the designer's dial is the block's size and the plate's threshold, not a material. Twists: at `threshold` 3 the 2×2 and a 2×1 together pay (3), and either with the player on top pays alone; a plate beside a zone sensor (`count_blocks`) is a decoy that refuses a block that the zone accepts (D4); the plate is the far end of a see-saw (C1), where a carbon fiber block is a small load and the player is the rest; stacking depth for the plate is up to 3. **(untested)**

**C3. Pump the swing.** *Swing + angle sensor on it + door.* The player grabs the bar and pumps with left/right until the arc passes the threshold. *Twists:* the sensor is on the *far* side so the player must release at the right moment; the arc crosses a spike pit; `free_rotation` for a full loop (the sensor is powered through that sector once per turn, so use a limited swing for a sensor); a moving platform under the arc.

**C4. Momentum into the swing.** *Surf ramp + swing.* A surfer in the air grabs the bar and passes the **whole surf speed** into the swing (400 px/s on a 64 px rod is about 6 rad/s), then releases to launch far past where a jump could go. *Twists:* the ±80° limit pins a fast catch and swings it back (use `free_rotation` for a big arc); a gem trail marks the release point.

**C5. Plank-as-ramp.** *See-saw on a surf route.* A tilted plank is a ramp from the low end, but the raised end is a lip that stops a fast surf like a wall. The player must arrive on the low side, or hit it when the plank is level, or make the plank level by standing a counterweight on it, or (with `steam_torque_scale` set) by boiling a vent under the low end, which turns the ramp into a switch (B13); a surfer over the plank gets no lift from that column, because the plank is the first solid the steam meets. Flat plank ends sit flush at extremes.

**C6. Motor and brake.** *Motorized see-saw + riders.* A heavy enough rider on the high side holds it against the stop. A see-saw that oscillates becomes a moving ramp/bridge the player times, and an angle sensor on it is a clock.

**C7. Headcount.** *Zone (`count_blocks`, `min_count` 3) + blocks of mixed weights + door.* The zone counts bodies, not weight (C2 counts weight), so a 1×1 ice block and a 1×1 metal block are worth the same. The puzzle is getting enough blocks into an area, and what is easy to place differs: an ice block slides about 4 tiles on a kick, a metal block about 1. *Twists:* a laser melts one of the three once it is in place (a melted block frees itself and leaves the count after `hold_time`), so it has to be shielded or timed (A3); the zone is over a basin, so floating ice counts but a sunk metal block below the zone does not; `count_player` as well, so the player has to stand in the zone with the stack. **(untested)**

**C8. Ballast.** *See-saw with `enemy_weight_scale` 1 + a grunt (weight 2) + an angle sensor + a door.* The plank ignores enemies until it opts in. With the scale at 1 a grunt weighs as much as the player, so a grunt lured onto one end (G3, G7) tips it without the player on it, and the sensor holds the door while the grunt stays. The grunt is the counterweight, which the player can spend, and also the thing the player has to keep from walking off. *Twists:* the plank is held level by a grunt against a metal block, so killing the grunt (a laser, a mine blast) drops the block's end and trips the sensor, the opposite of the plate in G7; a drillbot or crawler on a fixed patrol over the pivot rocks the plank and an angle sensor reads a square wave whose period is the walk (C6 without the motor); a mine (1) is too light to tip a plank against the player (2) and may be enough to level it. Whether an enemy keeps its footing on a tilted plank, and what a grunt does at a plank's end (a ledge test), are not documented, so test both first. **(untested)**

**C9. Spare weight.** *Metal dispenser (1×1) above a weight plate (`threshold` 4, `enemy_weight_scale` 0) + a button out of reach of the plate + a door.* The player cannot carry a block (walking pushes only carbon fiber, which is too light to pay a threshold of 4 alone, and a kick moves metal about a tile), so the weight is made where it is needed: the press drops a metal block (weight 4) on the plate and the door opens. Another press makes a new block and dissolves the old one, and the plate's `hold_time` (0.15 s) should bridge the swap, so the door does not blink. *Twists:* the threshold is 6, so the player has to stand on the plate as well as press a button reachable from it (C2); a laser melts the block after 3 s and the slag pours onto the plate; the plate is the far end of a see-saw (C1) and the block is the counterweight made on demand. *Slot budget:* a dispenser (`max_alive` 1) and two plates in AND (one at `threshold` 2, one at 4) and a door. The 4 plate needs a metal block, the 2 plate can be held by the player, and there is one block. A block left on a plate is taken back by the next press, so the player cannot make a block for each: the player has to stand on the 2 plate and make the block from a button within reach of it. With `max_alive` 2 the same room asks something else: the first block is under the dispenser and the spawn area is occupied, so the second press is refused until the first has been kicked clear (an ice block slides about 4 tiles, a metal one about 1). Twists: two dispensers on two buttons make the room solvable another way; a plate under the spawn point means the first block is on a plate, and a second press retires it at once. *Spawn on release:* an `invert`ed dispenser on a MOMENTARY pad spawns on the pad's falling edge, so the block arrives when the player steps off. With a plate at `threshold` 6 (the block 4 and the player 2) the order is forced: the player starts on the pad, leaves it, the block lands on the plate, then the player steps onto the plate too. Crouching on a momentary pad cycles it (a falling then a rising edge in one frame), so each crouch would make a block if the cooldown did not refuse the extras. Twists: a TIMED button instead, so the block arrives when the timer runs out and the player does nothing at all; a Counter between pad and dispenser, so a block comes on every third visit. **(untested)**

**C10. Catapult.** *See-saw with `launch_gain` (about 30 to 40 on a 4-tile plank) + a metal dispenser or a block on a ledge above one end + a ledge out of jump reach.* The player stands on the far end, the block is dropped onto the near end (a button on a dispenser, a kick off its ledge), the plank slams down and throws them up and back over the pivot onto the ledge. The throw is up and toward the drop's side, so the ledge sits above that end. *Twists:* the player's own weight opposes the block, so a light ice block barely tips it or arrives under `launch_min_speed` and fires nothing, while metal always does; standing at the tip throws furthest and near the pivot gives a short hop, so two ledges at different heights ask for two positions; a spike pit or a crusher between the ends punishes a short throw; a ceiling or a spike strip above the ledge punishes a long one; `launch_max_speed` low caps the reach however hard it lands. *Held:* an angle sensor on the plank and a door or an inverted spike layer. The block that fires the catapult stays on the low end and keeps the plank at its stop, so the sensor stays on and the door stays open while the thrown player is on the far side. Take the block away and the plank returns to level and the door shuts, so one plank is both the way over and the switch. Twists: the door is on the thrown player's return route, so the block must stay; a melting ice hammer is a timer on the door (the plank levels as it goes), with the player racing the melt; a second thrower is needed to go back. *Other hammers:* **a grunt** (plank with `enemy_weight_scale` 1): the player stands on the raised end and lures a chasing grunt onto the other (G7), and its weight (2) tips the plank and throws the player. The grunt is the hammer; the plank never throws it, since it has no `launch()`. A grunt weighs the same as the player, so with the player already on it the plank only tips once something heavier (a metal block, two enemies) is on the other end; a dormant mine weighs 1; a crawler on a patrol over the end throws the player on every lap, which is a clock (G8). **The motor** (`motor_torque` set, `launch_when_motorised` on, `launch_gain` set): the plank throws whoever is on the raised end at each stop, once per cycle, so the player waits on the raised end and is thrown at the top of the swing, with nothing to drop. A motor normally beats a lone rider (C6), so standing still does not hold it back. The throw is the same each cycle, so a landing platform on a clock (a moving platform) makes the player pick the cycle; leaving the opt-in off makes the same plank a plain moving ramp; it also throws a block parked on it, so a block ferry delivers one block per cycle to a plate. **Steam** (`steam_torque_scale` about 2 to 3, a boil vent under one end, a button or mirror aim for the laser): the end the player stands on is *lifted by the steam*, so when the plank arrives at its stop the player is on the raised end and the stop throws them. Nothing is carried or dropped, and the press that lights the laser is the hammer. Heat is slower and softer than a dropped block, so the arrival speed decides everything. On paper, with the default plank (`inertia` 120, `restoring_torque` 60, `friction_torque` 8), a full column at the tip under a 4-tile plank with the player on it never reaches the stop at scale 1, arrives at about 0.2 rad/s at 1.5 (the `launch_min_speed` line, so a throw or none) and about 0.4 at 2; the column's cone clips the tip, so plan for more. Treat these as estimates and tune the scale, `inertia` and `restoring_torque` in the editor. The throw leans toward the end that came down, so the ledge is over the *other* end, as for a dropped block. Twists: an ice block on the lifted end is thrown further than the player (C11), and then nobody needs to stand on the plank; the arming is gradual, so a player who steps off before the stop fires nothing; the gap from pool to plank delays the throw by 0.2 s a tile after the press; a vent under each end and two buttons make a two-way ferry; standing at the tip throws furthest but is also the hardest spot to lift, so two ledges ask for two spots. **(untested)**

**C11. Heavy drops, light flies.** *See-saw with `launch_gain` + a metal block as the hammer + an ice block on the raised end + a weight plate or a laser across a gap.* A block takes the throw divided by its mass, so an ice block flies about as far as the player would and a metal one hardly moves. Drop metal on one end and the ice on the other is delivered across the gap, onto the plate, into the beam to melt, or onto a basin. The player has to arrange the ice, then make the drop. *Twists:* the ice is also a load on the raised end and so resists the tip; an ice block is a one-shot (it melts) while a dispenser makes it a budget (C9); the player is thrown too if they stand on the raised end, so they must stand clear; a metal block thrown by mistake lands short; a 1×1 carbon fiber block flies as far as ice and is not consumed by a melt, but it weighs half as much on the raised end, so it barely resists the tip. **(untested)**

**C12. Balance of heats.** *See-saw (`steam_torque_scale` 1, `restoring_torque` above 0) with a boil vent under each end at equal lever arms + two lasers on two separate sources + two angle sensors, one per side, into one door.* Equal columns at equal distances from the pivot lift both ends alike and cancel, so with both beams on, or both off, the plank is level, and with exactly one on it tips so that the end over the steam goes up. The two sensors in OR make the door an XOR of the two beams, and the same two through `invert` make it an XNOR, "both or neither"; one sensor per side also says *which* beam is on, with no receiver. It is a comparator of heats, so the larger column wins. *Twists:* unequal lever arms make one vent count for more (a vent near the tip outweighs a wider one near the pivot), so both on tips toward the stronger one and A beats B; a metal block on one end biases the plank so that a lone vent cannot tip it, which makes the door an AND; the two lasers share one turret through a toggle-mode mirror, so the player chooses which pool burns; a player or a grunt standing on an end is a third input. The steam is noisy (the puff count is rounded at random), so the plank shivers, and the sensors' 15° with 3° of hysteresis absorb it; keep any threshold well away from level. **(untested)**

**C13. Set and reset.** *Limited see-saw with `restoring_torque` 0 (not `free_rotation`) + a boil vent under each end, each with its own laser + an angle sensor + a door or a powered layer.* With no spring, a plank stays at the stop it was driven to as long as `friction_torque` exceeds the torque left, so steam under one end sets it one way and steam under the other drives it back. It is an SR latch in plain view: the sensor is the output, the two beams are set and reset, and neither beam has to stay on. Unlike a Latch node the player can see it and touch it: standing at an end pushes that end down and so sets the plank as well, which makes the player a third input, and unlike a TOGGLE button it is set by *direction*, not by a count. A boil that stops half way leaves the plank half way, so it is also an accumulator. *Twists:* each short boil adds to the angle, and the door opens after enough of them, reset by the other vent (a counter you can see in the room); one vent sets it and the player's weight or a parked grunt (C8) is the reset, so the latch is something the player has to go and undo; a metal block on one end makes that end the default; the plank is also a ramp (C5), so its state changes the route; the angle is saved by a checkpoint and the steam is not, so a restore keeps the state and the beams restart. Whether a plank that has only been nudged holds its angle depends on `friction_torque`, so check it in the editor before building a counter on it. **(untested)**

### D. Momentum and the surf

**D1. Banked speed.** *Downhill + flat + speed trap or barrier.* The level is designed so the *only* way to reach 450+ is a particular line (downhill matched, wet floor, a clean landing). *Twists:* a belt against the surfer is a speed tax (or a speed assist); a tilt-mismatched section silently drains speed; a pad on the line launches without a tilt penalty.

**D2. Speed trap door.** *Speed trap + door.* The trap reads a *surfer's* ground speed as they cross its line, and powers the door for `hold_time` seconds (5 default). The door is far, so the player must surf *and then run* before it closes. *Twists:* `hold_time` 0 = permanent; two traps in an AND with different thresholds; the line is across a pool, so skim at 180+ speed *and* arrive at 450+. *Speed gate:* an inverted spike layer across a surf run in place of the door. Crossing the trap's line at 450 or more retracts the field for `hold_time` (5 s); a slower pass leaves the spikes out, and a surfer who rides into them is knocked off the board (a surf ends through `take_hit()`). Where the door stops a slow player, this punishes them. Twists: `require_surfing` so a walker cannot trip it; a `hold_time` short enough that the player has to keep the speed down a long field; a second trap with a higher threshold in AND. **(untested)**

**D3. Barrier gauntlet.** *Breakable barriers with different `break_speed`s along a run.* The player must still be fast after paying friction. Mix `break_from` LEFT/RIGHT so you must approach from the right side. Barriers don't pay points, but enemies bowled between them do. *Twists:* the barrier blocks a laser beam, so smashing it opens the line of fire (and *lights a receiver* through the hole); the run continues into a launch.

**D4. Shove delivery.** *Surf + ice block + weight plate far away.* The surf is a long-range kick: shove an ice block at speed and it slides up to about 18 tiles on its own to a distant plate, or into a mine cluster. *Twists:* a metal block moves much less per shove, a ledge dumps the block, a wall in front of the block refuses the shove (the surfer stops like at a wall), a stack of blocks; carbon fiber is the long version (below). *Long delivery:* the same shove with a carbon fiber block, which does not melt on the way. A surf shove gives it the most speed of any block (light blocks take more from the same hit) and costs the surfer the least, so the block slides far (the shove is capped at 200 px/s, about 18 tiles on a flat floor) and arrives intact. At the far end a plate at threshold 2 ignores a 0.5 block, and a zone sensor (`count_blocks`) or a button counts it whatever it weighs, so the player has to learn that two sensors read the same block differently. A wall in front of the block refuses the shove and stops the surfer like a wall, so the lane needs to be clear. Twists: the block has to cross a pit by a launch pad first (a 1×1 flies like ice); a crusher at the end of the lane shatters it unless the shove is timed to the dwell (F2). *Shove and lift:* a second stage. The lane ends in a steam column and the block's sliding stops in it, so the column carries it (up to 80 px/s) to a zone sensor or a plate high above a pit. The surfer can ride the same column up to the sensor's ledge, so one source serves both routes (D6), and the board climbs faster than the block, so a surfer who follows it up catches its underside. A block in the column also shades it: the steam ends at the block's underside, so the surfer rises only to the block that rose ahead. Twists: the lane is wet or slippery, so the block coasts past the column and the shove has to be softer; a crust reaction on the slag below makes the column only for the length of a pour, so the block has to arrive during it (H4); a plate at threshold 2 refuses the 1×1 block that a zone accepts. Whether a surfer who meets the underside of a rising block pushes it, stops under it or rises past it is not in the docs and is the thing to test first. **(untested)**

**D5. Skim crossing.** *Settled water pool + a surf run-up.* The pool is a road if you arrive at 180+. If you drop below, you swim (and you'd end up in an electrified or slag pool if it changes). *Twists:* crust over the far half makes it a plain floor; an arc/spark strikes while you're crossing; the basin's rim is a natural ramp out. *Two roads:* a freezable water basin and a freeze turret. A settled pool is a skim road only at 180+ px/s; below that you swim. Freezing it makes plain ground a surfer rides at any speed, so a slow player has a way across that a fast one does not need. A freeze beam across the lane chills a walker but never a surfer, so the same beam is a toll on one route and invisible on the other. Twists: frozen water is slippery for a walker but the surf's friction is unchanged; the electrified-crust top (B8) ends a surf by hurting the board; a laser thaws the road behind the surfer. **(untested)**

**D6. Steam launch.** *Steam column (crust formation, a boiled basin) + surf.* The board rises through the column (up to 120 px/s) to a ledge or an upper route. It only works while surfing, and ceilings stop it. A walking player can't use the column directly, but a carbon fiber block rides the same one (B11), so a surfer's shortcut can have a slow, loaded cousin for a walker. *Twists:* a second route for non-surfers that is longer, or a block lift on the same column; the column only exists while the laser boils; a block that has risen into the column ahead of the surfer shades it (D4).

**D7. Bowling lane.** *Grunts, tread crawlers and drillbots (from behind only) along a fast lane.* A chain pays 5/10/20 points within a 2 s window. Drillbots punish approaching from the drill side; mines stop the surf and arm; use a barrier to break the lane at a precise speed. *Twists:* a bowled-over grunt that is mid-windup is still bowled; a lane that bends so one enemy faces you.

**D8. Belt and brake.** *Conveyor running against the surfer + stall gate.* If belt speed ≈ the board's speed, the surf stalls. A powered belt lets a button *change what the run does*: belt off = easy, belt on = need more speed. A speed trap *reads* the surf plus belt ground speed.

### E. Traversal and spatial

**E1. Underside route.** *Catwalk (own layer) over spikes or slag.* The player hangs by crouch+jump from standing on the catwalk (or jumps up into it), brachiates across, and mantles at the end. A ladder cell in the path hands off to a climb. *Twists:* the catwalk ends and the player must swing/jump; terrain under the catwalk blocks brachiating; a crust underside as the continuation; the catwalk is a powered layer, so it lets go of the hanger when it retracts (E8).

**E2. Hang from things that move.** *Doors, blocks, see-saw ends, moving and drop platforms are all hangable.* A door that opens releases a hanger (a shaft with a door the player is hanging from), a drop platform releases at its drop, a floating block bobs. These are timed vertical routes. *Twists:* a block that melts under the player; the see-saw end is only hangable within 15° and slowly.

**E3. Rail ride.** *Steep and flat rail segments + rail bot.* Slide down the steep part (speed builds), brachiate along the flat part (friction brings you to a stop), and jump past a bot (contact hurts, and knocks you off). Catching a slope keeps a share of your speed. Jumping off carries the slide's velocity. *Twists:* the rail passes over a pool or slag; sparks drip down on the route below; a laser kills the bot (the usual solution) or a mine blast does.

**E4. Crouch gap + sneak.** *Low corridor* that only a crouched player fits, a long one needs sneak at 50 px/s while a Grunt or timing cycle pushes pressure. Standing back up is blocked by a real overlap check under a ceiling, so you creep out. *Twists:* crouching while a crusher cycles; a button *inside* the corridor requires a crouch-press (the crouch itself is the click).

**E5. Ladders and landings.** A ladder through a catwalk is a clean route up; you can brachiate onto it from beneath. *Twists:* a spike tile in a ladder cell breaks climbing (don't do that); a ladder that ends at a door; a ladder that is not there until a button summons it (E8).

**E6. Pad chains.** *Jump pads aimed by rotation + a moving platform or swing as the landing.* Pads keep the surf's speed. Powered pads are *live only when a source says so*, so a pad route can be a timed window. Blocks also get launched (lighter ones go higher).

**E7. Shut-in.** *Zone (`count_player`) covering a room + a door on `invert` at its entry + a puzzle inside whose exit is gated another way.* Put the zone just past the doorway. The inverted door starts open, closes behind the player as they step in, and stays shut while they are inside. A door never crushes and waits if the player stops in the doorway, so the trap cannot hurt anyone. Leaving by the exit empties the zone and the entry reopens `hold_time` later. A checkpoint inside the room restores it sealed, because the zone and the door's frame are both saved, so a retry never finds the door open. *Twists:* a `hold_time` of several seconds is a lock-out timer on the entry; the exit door is wired to the room's own puzzle, so the zone and the puzzle together are the only way out; two rooms share a zone for a sealed pair. **(untested)**

**E8. Summoned route.** *TIMED button + powered catwalk layer over a pit (spikes or slag).* The bridge extends the moment the button is pressed and goes `duration` plus `warn_time` later: the button pulses in its last second and the layer then blinks for `warn_time`. The crossing budget is that sum, and a player standing on it when it goes simply drops (the collision is gone), so size it for a real crossing with margin, as in the safe window below. The first bridge in a level can have `show_ghost` on, so the player sees what the button is for. *Twists:* a second TIMED button midway on a long span restarts the countdown (two buttons are OR, so it stays out while either is on); the way back is gone, so the far side needs its own button or the underside route (E1); a player hanging under the bridge is released as it retracts, with the regrab lockout, so the hang route has the same clock; `warn_time` 0 for a strict version. *Safe window:* a TIMED button and an inverted spike layer instead of a bridge. Pressing the button retracts the field for `duration` and the player crosses before it ends. The spikes extend with no warning and hurt a player still in them at once, so `duration` is the only clock: size it for a real crossing with margin. Twists: a second TIMED button midway restarts the countdown for a long corridor (two buttons are OR, so the field stays open while either is on); a button inside a crouch gap (E4) so the press is the crouch; a TOGGLE button lets the player latch it open and puts the question on what else the same source drives. *Race the ladder:* a TIMED button at the foot of a shaft and a powered ladder layer, optionally with a powered catwalk deck at the top on the same source. The ladder stays climbable through the blink and then goes, dropping anyone on it on the next frame, so the shaft height against `duration` plus `warn_time` is the difficulty. The ladder climbs at 100 px/s (`climb_speed` in the player script), so a shaft of N tiles takes about N × 0.16 s plus the step off; time a real climb before sizing `duration`. Twists: a ledge or button halfway up restarts the countdown for a tall shaft; an underside route (E1) ends at a powered ladder that takes the hanger by up/down, so the hand-off has the same clock; the ladder layer sits at the same position as the static `Ladders` layer so a static lower half and a summoned upper half join; a checkpoint at the foot, not on the deck (H4). *Building bridge:* three powered catwalk layers over a pit (one per segment) and two Delays. Segment 1 takes the button; segments 2 and 3 take it through Delays with `on_delay` and `off_delay` both 1 s and 2 s, each reading the button and not each other. Segment k then extends k seconds after the press and starts its blink k seconds after the button's own timer runs out, so the span builds itself ahead of the player and collapses behind them and the player has to keep moving. Each segment's blink (`warn_time`) is the telegraph for its collapse, and `show_ghost` shows where the next one will be. Size the delays from a timed walk across one segment. Twists: a static catwalk stone on its own layer between segments gives somewhere to wait; delays shorter than a crossing make the player stop and watch, longer ones strand them on a segment that is about to go; a ladder layer or a door at the far end with its own Delay finishes the route. **(untested)**

**E9. Trapdoor.** *Catwalk layer on `invert` + a button + a load standing on it + a destination below.* The floor is out while the source is off, so pressing the button retracts it (after the blink) and whatever stands on it drops: a metal block onto a weight plate or into slag (C2, B4), an ice block into a basin or into a laser line (A3), a block onto a see-saw end (C1). It delivers a block to a place a kick cannot reach. At the default `warn_time` the blink is a second of slack to kick the load off again or to change one's mind; at 0 it is instant. A catwalk lets water through, so a melting ice block on the trapdoor pours straight through it. *Twists:* the trapdoor sits over the same basin the block is meant for, so the player has to choose when; two trapdoors on one button drop two blocks together; the player stands on it too and has to jump clear when it blinks. **(untested)**

**E10. Flip-flop stepping stones.** *Two catwalk layers on one source, one with `invert`, laid as alternate stones across a pit.* Whichever set has just been powered extends at once and the other blinks for `warn_time` before it goes, so for that second both sets exist and the player can hop across; the window opens in both directions. The source can be the player's own TOGGLE button, or a clock (a motorized see-saw's angle sensor, C6, or a patrol through a zone, G8). *Twists:* `warn_time` 0 makes it a hard swap, and a stone of the wrong set drops its rider, so the player has to read the rhythm elsewhere; the clock is faster than the player can cross in one go, so a static catwalk stone midway (on its own layer, kept off the powered cells) gives somewhere to wait for the next window; a ladder layer on the same source joins the far end; the pit is slag, so the margin is a fall into it. **(untested)**

**E11. Block stack.** *Dispenser (`max_alive` 3, `cooldown` 0.5) high above a floor + a ledge out of jump reach + a button.* The spawn point sits over open air, so every block falls away and the area is always free, and the blocks land one on another into a stack. The player builds it first and climbs it second. The fourth press dissolves the **oldest**, which is the bottom of the stack, so the rest drop one level: a player standing on top is dropped as they press, so the button belongs at the foot of the stack. A free-rotating stack can topple, so the block scene may be an inherited block with `allow_rotation` off (a metal stack is heavier and not slippery; ice is slippery to stand on; a carbon fiber stack never melts and is light, so the stack can stand under a laser, and it breaks to a bullet or a crusher). *Twists:* a laser across the top level melts the highest block (A3) and the player has to build around it; the stack stands in a basin, so ice floats and rises with the level (B1) while metal sinks. **(untested)**

**E12. Pushed step.** *A ledge just out of jump reach + a carbon fiber block on the floor + a wall or pillar to push it against.* The ground has a ledge out of jump reach, and a 1×1 block against the wall is a step: the player pushes it to the foot of the ledge, climbs onto it and jumps (or hangs from the block and mantles, since blocks are hangable). It is a one-way push (there is no pull), so the first push decides it: a block pushed against the wrong wall, or past the foot of the ledge, is stuck, and the player needs another route round it to push it back. Design the room with *one* correct sequence and a way to see the dead end before committing: a pillar in the floor that a block cannot pass, a gap that eats it, a corner. A kick moves it about four tiles, so kick is for the long run to the room and push for the last tiles. *Twists:* two ledges at two heights and one block, so the first step is for the lower one and the block is moved on to the second (one-way, so it only works in the right order); the floor is slippery (a frozen crust, an ice block) and the block coasts past the foot of the ledge when the push ends, which turns a precise push into a slide; a catwalk above the pillar gives a route to its far side, which is the undo the player has to discover; a dispenser in the room makes a new block when the old one is dead (the undo below). *Undo:* a dispenser (carbon fiber, `max_alive` 1, `cooldown` 0.5) and a button outside the room turn a final mistake into one press. A press makes a new block at the spawn point and dissolves the old one wherever it is, so a block pushed into a corner is retired and replaced, and the player goes back to the start of the room with no restart and no reload. The spawn point has to be clear when the press arrives: the old block sitting on it is fine (it is the one being replaced), but the player, an enemy or a different block in the footprint refuses the press, so a spawn point over a drop or at the head of the lane is the safe design. The room's *cost* is the walk back, and a button inside the room is itself part of the puzzle. A checkpoint (H4) keeps the dispenser's blocks where they were and the cooldown, and a restore makes none. Twists: `max_alive` 2 gives two blocks and so a block that is parked while the other is used; the dispenser is the thing that is *out of reach* until the first push is done; the undo button is on a TIMED source, so the retry is a race. **(untested)**

**E13. Plug in the line.** *A lane for a pushed block with an ice block sitting in it + a laser (or slag) that can melt the ice + a carbon fiber block to move.* A push asks every block ahead in the line, and an ice or metal block refuses the whole thing, so an ice block in the lane is a plug that a push cannot move. The player has to clear it: melt it with a beam (A3: about 2 s, then it is water on the floor), or kick it out of the lane. Then the chain moves. A line of several carbon fiber blocks pushes together (up to 8, each extra block 0.75 as fast), so a long chain is slower and needs a long clear lane; a refusal at the *far* end (a wall behind the last block) stops the whole line. *Twists:* the plug is metal, which melts in about 3 s under a laser and pours slag, so the lane floods with a hazard and the push has to wait; the plug is on a plate that holds a door and clearing it shuts the door, so the block needs to be through first; a crusher in the lane (F2) is what the chain has to pass; an enemy standing in the lane refuses the push (a grunt or crawler is not a block), so the chain waits on a patrol. Whether an enemy that walks into a block is stopped or turns round is not documented. **(untested)**

### F. Timing, rhythm and traps

**F1. Phase corridor.** *Crushers, sawwheels or cannons with `phase_offset`.* The spike is the dangerous end and the arm (side) is solid; dwell windows are the safe beats. Stagger so the player has to learn a rhythm. *Twists:* a powered crusher parks *safe* when off, so a button is a pause; a TIMED button pauses it just long enough.

**F2. Jam it, or run it.** *Crusher + metal block.* Kick metal under the extending spike; the crusher jams, parks and stays harmless. The corridor is permanently safe, but the block is stuck there (and wired crushers reset on a power cycle, which makes a trap-reset you can trigger by accident). *Twists:* **ice is the wrong tool** (it shatters into water, which might be wanted: water onto slag crusts it); carbon fiber is the wrong tool too, and a quiet one (it shatters and fades with no liquid, and never jams); the metal must first be melted off a laser line, then moved. *Crusher run:* a corridor and a carbon fiber block to push through it. The crusher's spike shatters a carbon fiber block with no liquid and no jam, so the block is pushed through the spike's column during the dwell window and the player follows a beat behind it. The block moves at up to 75 px/s, so the window has to cover the block clearing the column and the player behind it. The slow alternative is the jam above: a metal block cannot be pushed, so it is *kicked* about a tile at a time into the spike's path to jam the crusher for good, and then nothing is at risk (the jam stays even if the metal is moved later). One corridor so offers a quick, risky route for the block that lasts and a slow, safe one for the block that jams. Twists: a crusher on `phase_offset` with another makes two windows the chain has to catch one after the other; a chain of two blocks needs a longer window; the crusher is powered and a TIMED button parks it, so the block is pushed during the pause; the block is wanted on the far side, where a plate or a zone sensor takes it (D4). **(untested)**

**F3. Shatter delivery.** *Crusher above a basin + ice block.* An ice block shattered by the crusher bursts into water **at the crusher**. Water onto slag below crusts it. A way to *deliver water on a timer*. Carbon fiber breaks the same way but leaves nothing, so it is how a block is *removed* on a timer, not how water is delivered (F2). **(untested)**

**F4. Bullet counter.** *Cannon + mirror with `bullet_rotates` + bullet targets.* Each bullet turns the mirror a step and is consumed. The bullet target gets hit only when the mirror aims right. A cannon firing every second advances it without the player. *Twists:* the player kicks it too (race); a TOGGLE bullet target latches the result.

**F5. Cannon pulse trains.** *Cannon + MOMENTARY bullet target.* Fire rate shorter than `pulse_length` keeps it powered; longer lets it flicker. Choose the cannon's rate to decide whether a door is steady or blinks. *Pulse floor:* add a spike layer. Each shot holds the target on for its pulse (0.3 s by default), and a `fire_rate` longer than that lets it flicker, so the field is out for a blink every shot and retracted otherwise. The bullet is visible in flight, so it is the telegraph: the spikes extend as it lands. Two cannons on different `fire_rate`s feeding two layers drift in and out of phase, which makes a rhythm the player has to learn (F1). Twists: an inverted layer is retracted for a blink and dangerous the rest of the time, which only suits a very short gap; a mirror or barrier in the bullet's path lets the player stop the pulses. **(untested)**

**F6. Telegraph reading.** *Arc turret and rail bot glows (0.45 s), grunt windup (amber).* Teaching language: amber = coming, red = live. Use it consistently.

**F7. Tripwire cannon.** *Zone (`count_player`) + cannon + a corridor.* A powered cannon fires one bullet at once on a rising edge and then every `fire_rate`, so entering the zone starts the barrage. Set the zone back from the muzzle so a bullet is in flight, and visible, before it can reach the player: the bullet is the telegraph. *Twists:* `hold_time` keeps the cannon firing for that long after the player leaves; `invert` gives a cannon that fires except while the player stands in a shelter zone, and `hold_time` is the grace before it resumes; `count_blocks` instead of the player lets the player kick a block into the zone to start it from a distance, and a bullet target at the far end turns that into a remote switch (F5). *Alarm:* a Latch node behind the zone (set by the zone, with a reset pad in its `reset_sources`). The tripwire stops when the player leaves (`hold_time`); the alarm does not: crossing the zone sets the Latch and it stays set after the player has gone, so the room is hostile until the player reaches the reset pad. The reset wins while it is held, so standing on the pad is a safe spot. Keep a visible telegraph (the bullet in flight, as above); a spike layer would extend with no warning. Twists: `start_on` begins the level already alarmed, and the puzzle is finding the pad; a Delay (`on_delay` 2) between the zone and the Latch is a grace period, since a player who crosses the zone in under the delay never sets it; two zones (front and back door) in OR on one Latch. A checkpoint inside the room restores the Latch as it was, so place it before the zone (H4) or the player respawns into a live alarm. **(untested)**

**F8. Cover.** *Cannon + a metal block + a corridor + a bullet target or the player on the far side.* A bullet no longer flies through blocks. A metal block takes it with a ping and is unharmed, so a block kicked into a cannon's lane is permanent cover; ice and carbon fiber break on the first bullet and are one-shot cover. The player can stand behind a cover block and kick it forward (a kick shoves it away from the kicker, about a tile for metal), step up and kick again, so a corridor under fire is crossed a tile at a time at the cannon's `fire_rate`. Size the block to the player or have them crouch (E4), since a bullet that reaches the player's height still hits. Carbon fiber, which can be *pushed*, is the quick cover, but it is gone at the first bullet it takes, so it covers one shot. *Twists:* the cover block is the same one that has to end up on a plate or in a zone at the far end, so the player cannot leave it behind; a bullet target hidden behind metal is not hit until the metal is kicked away, a gate in the form of a block (A7 for bullets); a mirror with `bullet_rotates` in the lane turns on every bullet that reaches it, so cover in front of it holds the mirror still. What height a bullet flies at against a 1×1 block, and where it hits a rotated block, is not in the docs. **(untested)**

### G. Enemies as mechanisms

**G1. Boot the mine.** *Mine + grunts.* Kick a mine into a grunt group (a mine that slides faster than 40 into a wall or any enemy detonates; a blast hits everything in 40 px and chains to other mines). *Twists:* a laser arms a mine from far away (arm, never damage); a rail bot overhead killed by a mine blast; mines on both sides of a door.

**G2. Crawler stepping stones.** *Tread crawler between two safe spots over spikes or slag.* Stomp it flipped (you bounce), then stand on it. It stays pinned while you stand there. After the top is clear it rights itself after 4 s. *Twists:* one stone, two players of time; the crawler flips on the far side of a pit because its patrol ends there.

**G3. Lure.** *Grunt + laser.* A chasing grunt stops at a ledge and walks into a beam; the laser does 1 damage per 0.5 s, so two beams or a long exposure kill it. *Twists:* an enemy in the beam *shields* a receiver behind it (a Stingbot crossing it flickers it); a grunt chased onto a plate presses it (weight 2) and one parked in a zone sensor powers that too (G7).

**G4. Stingbot windows.** *Stingbot landing for 2 s every 8 s.* The recharge is a safe window. Use it as a timed door-opening or a way to cross a hazard *under* its flight path. It doesn't avoid obstacles, so a wall can *trap* it. **(untested)**

**G5. Drillbot lane.** *Drillbot patrolling a narrow ledge.* Bowl it from behind (surf), or kick it three times *from behind* (its body is harmless), or cross over the top. *Twists:* the free-patrol (`patrol_distance` 0) walks to ledges, so it patrols the whole platform.

**G6. Clear the room.** *Zone over an arena (`count_enemies`) + a door on `invert` + grunts, a crawler and a mine.* The zone is powered while any enemy is inside, so the inverted door stays shut until the last one is dead (an enemy leaves the group as it starts to die, so the door opens about `hold_time` after the last kill). A dormant mine and a flipped crawler still count, so a mine has to be booted into the others (G1) and a crawler has to be killed, not just stomped. A Stingbot flies, so the zone must cover its airspace, and it has no pathing, so wall off anywhere it could stick or the room soft-locks. *Twists:* two arenas in AND; `min_count` 2 opens the door when only one enemy is left, which the player can then choose to leave alone; a checkpoint inside restores the enemies and the zone together. **(untested)**

**G7. Park the lure.** *Grunt + a ledge + a zone at the ledge's edge (`count_enemies`, `count_player` off) + door, with the player across a gap.* A chasing grunt stops at a ledge (G3), so it parks in a predictable place: put the zone there, and the door is held open for as long as it stands in it. G3 kills the lure; here it has to stay alive, and the player has to get through while keeping it interested. Leaving its aggro range may let it wander off and shut the door, which is the part to test. *Twists:* two grunts on two ledges in AND, with the player between them; a mine kicked beside the parked grunt would blast it, so the player's own tool becomes the failure state; the door is a spike layer on `invert`, so a grunt that wanders off re-arms the route. *Dead weight:* a weight plate at the ledge's edge (`enemy_weight_scale` 1, threshold 2) in place of the zone. A chasing grunt stops at a ledge, so it parks on the plate and holds the route open. The plate reads weight, so the threshold can demand more than the grunt: at 4 the grunt pays half and a block or the player pays the rest (C2). Kill the grunt and the reading falls the frame it starts to die, the plate lets go `hold_time` (0.15 s) later and the route closes, so a laser or a mine blast near the lure is the failure state. Twists: the plate is in AND with a second one a mine-kick away; the route is an inverted spike layer, so the grunt wandering off re-arms the spikes. Because a plate has no collider, a patrolling enemy also presses it as it walks across (G8 by weight), so a plate in an enemy's lane for the *player's* use needs `enemy_weight_scale` 0. *Pinned stone on the scale:* a tread crawler on a patrol over a flush plate (threshold 4) + a door. Stomp it flipped while it is over the plate (it weighs 2 even flipped), then stand on it: the player's 2 stacks through the crawler and the reading is 4, and standing on it pins it (G2). Step off and it rights itself after 4 s and walks away, so the door is a held position, not a latch. Twists: at 6 the player cannot pay alone and a block on the crawler's back or a second flipped crawler makes up the sum; a metal block (4) would also pay at 4, so raise the threshold to keep the crawler necessary; the plate sits on a patrol that a laser interrupts. **(untested)**

**G8. Patrol clock.** *Tread crawler (or a rail bot on its rail) on a fixed patrol + a zone over part of it (`count_enemies`) + a door or spike layer.* Each pass through the zone powers the source for the pass plus `hold_time`, a window that repeats every lap, and the crawler's turn pause at each end is part of the period. The player times a crossing to the lap. A rail bot gives a ceiling clock whose sparks also drip on the route below (E3). *Twists:* a mirror that turns one step per rising edge counts laps for free, so the player waits N laps (A2 without the button), and a Counter node on the zone counts them with no mirror and, with `auto_reset_time`, shuts the route again; flip the crawler and stand on it inside the zone (G2) and the pinned enemy holds the source on for as long as the player stays; kill the clock and the source stays off, so give the route a second way (OR) or the player can soft-lock. *Slow the clock:* add a freeze turret on the lane (a button, a mirror aim). The patrol runs at half speed while the beam ends on the enemy, so the zone is occupied for twice as long per lap and the door's window doubles; the player decides when the clock slows, and only while the beam is on a body. A rail bot's drip clock is not slowed, so on a chilled bot sparks land closer together along the rail. Twists: the beam is cut when the enemy walks out of the line, so the aim has to follow its lane; chill fades over 0.5 s, so the clock speeds up just after the beam goes; a grunt's chase and a Stingbot's lunges are slowed as well, so the same beam is a gift or a danger depending on who stands in it. **(untested)**

**G9. Cut the span.** *Catwalk layer on `invert` (the bridge exists while the source is off) + a momentary button on the far side + a chasing grunt.* A grunt chases the player across the bridge, the player steps onto the button, and the bridge blinks and retracts under the grunt. Where it lands is the design: a pit it cannot leave, or a spot that matters. A block kicked onto the button holds the cut after the player steps off (otherwise the bridge returns, since the button is momentary). Because the grunt only stops at a ledge when one is ahead of it, it keeps walking during the blink, so the player has to be on the button while the grunt is still on the span. What a grunt does after the fall is not documented, so test it. *Twists:* the grunt is parked at the far end by a zone (G7) and the cut is what keeps it there; two bridges and two grunts. **(untested)**

**G10. Short fuse.** *Dormant proximity mine on a weight plate (threshold 1) + an inverted spike layer along the route + the route passing within 32 px of the mine.* A mine weighs 1 whether or not it is armed, so a mine placed on the plate holds the field open. Walking past arms it (32 px across, 28 up); the fuse is 0.8 s, then the blast (radius 40) kills the mine, the plate lets go 0.15 s later and the spikes come back. The player's window is the fuse plus the hold. Do not kick it onto the plate: a kicked mine is armed and detonates on its fuse while it slides. A laser can arm it from far away, which gives the player a way to start the clock with no one standing near. *Twists:* a second door that needs the mine alive, so the player must use the first window; a grunt wanders onto the plate beside it (2 + 1) and keeps the field open after the mine goes. **(untested)**

**G11. Barricade.** *Grunt or crawler + a corridor + a carbon fiber block pushed into it.* Enemies cannot move a block (the shove guard stays on for every block, carbon fiber included), so a block pushed into a corridor is a wall for them that the player can later push on. A chasing grunt held behind a block gives the player time at a plate or a button; a block in a crawler's patrol shortens its lap. The player pushes it, so the grunt stands on the other side of it, and the block is a barrier the player is safe behind. What a grunt does against a block in its path (turn round, wait, stand there) and whether a crawler's patrol treats it as a wall are not documented. *Twists:* the grunt is on the plate side of the block (G7) and the block is what keeps it there; a laser cannot melt it, so the barricade stays until a bullet or a crusher takes it. **(untested)**

### H. Information, memory, routing and pacing

**H1. Gem trail on a switch.** *Powered gems + a source.* The gems of a trail appear only while powered. A timed source shows the route briefly; a latched one shows it permanently. Trails double as **route hints** for launch arcs. Powered gems are free to place in quantity. *Note:* use 5 or 10 point values (popups exist for 5, 10, 20, 50).

**H2. Bonus route.** *Bonus gems (50 pts, not part of the unlock %)* guard risky detours. Pair with a medkit at the end of the risky path.

**H3. Locked finish.** *Finish with `require_all`* gems + a button, or OR for two routes. A level where 100% gems are impossible without the button, and a level where the button is *optional*, change the pacing.

**H4. Checkpoint-aware design.** *Place checkpoints before expensive resets.* Blocks, doors, basins (fill and crust) and enemies are restored; liquid particles, steam, arcs, bullets and sparks aren't, so avoid designs where success depends on an in-flight particle (a block that was riding a steam column when the checkpoint was taken falls until the column rebuilds). A restore always sets dangerous things harmless (mines dormant, grunts recovering, drips restarted). A powered bridge or ladder is re-derived from its source: with the source off it gets one blink and retracts, so a checkpoint on a bridge drops the respawned player. Put the checkpoint on ground, or on a bridge whose source a restore brings back on (a TOGGLE button, a crust). A dispenser's blocks are saved with it and come back where they were, a restore never makes a new block, and the cooldown is saved too: a checkpoint just before the press that makes a block gives the player a fresh try at it.

**H5. Cue the opening.** *Camera cue + inverted spike layer on the same source.* The player presses a button; the field in a far shaft retracts and the camera pans to it, so the new route is visible without a hint sign. The cue plays on the rising edge of its own power and the layer is inverted, so both fire on the same press. Keep the hold short, because the world keeps running and input is not locked. *Twists:* `once` so the pan does not repeat on a TOGGLE button; a cue with `invert` on the same source shows the spikes coming back out, which teaches what the button did; a `delay` lines the pan up with the player leaving a calm spot; the same cue on a bridge's source shows the span extending (H6); wire the layer to the cue's pass-through instead of the button and the field retracts when the camera has arrived (`delay` plus `pan_time`), with no Delay node to tune, while a falling edge passes at once. *Look-ahead trigger:* a zone (`count_player`) and a camera cue (`once`) in place of the button: walking to a spot pans the camera to what has just changed, with no deliberate act. The cue plays only on a rising edge, never at startup or after a restore, and `once` is saved, so a retry does not replay it. Put the zone on a calm stretch of the route, because input is not locked while the camera is away, and use `delay` to line the pan up with a door opening. Twists: the zone sits on the far side of a hazard, so the pan is a reward and not a distraction; the same zone also drives the door itself, so one trigger opens it and shows it. **(untested)**

**H6. The telegraph ladder.** *Powered bridge + `show_ghost` + a camera cue on the same source.* Summoned terrain has three tells and they say different things: the **ghost** says where it will be, the **cue** (H5) says that it has appeared, the **blink** says when it ends. Teach in that order: the first bridge in a level has the ghost on and a cue, the second has the cue only, the third has neither and relies on the player expecting a button to do something. A ghost is a promise and a hidden layer is a secret, so turn it off only where the player is meant to hunt. The spike layer has no blink, so a ghost and a cue are its only tells. *Twists:* a lower `ghost_opacity` for a dark room; the ghost is on for the layer the player will need later and off for the one they need now. **(untested)**

**H7. Ordered switches.** *Three MOMENTARY buttons + three Latches in a chain + a reset + a door.* Latch A is set by the first pad. Latch B has the second pad and Latch A as `sources` with `all_required`, so the second pad takes only while A is latched, and Latch C does the same on B. The door follows C. A press out of order latches nothing, and with no sign of it, so each stage needs its own tell (a powered gem or a lamp door, A2). A decoy pad or a zone at the room's entrance in every Latch's `reset_sources` clears the chain and holds it clear while it is pressed. *Twists:* a Counter on the decoy pads (`count_to` 3) is "three strikes" and arms a trap (F7); a block left on pad B before A is pressed latches B the moment A does, since the AND then sees both on, which is a second solution unless the pads are out of reach of a kick (E4); the three sources are laser receivers swept by a scanner (A10), so the beam has to reach A, then B, then C, and each is remembered as the beam passes, with no `hold_time` to tune. **(untested)**

**H8. Hold the point.** *Zone (`count_player`) or a weight plate + Delay (`on_delay` 4) + a door or a powered catwalk layer, with something hostile nearby.* The output rises only if the input stays on the whole delay, so standing in the zone for 4 s opens the route, and stepping out (or being knocked out) cancels the wait and starts it again. A zone's `hold_time` is a delay on leaving, which cannot do this. Put the zone where standing still is uncomfortable: in an arc turret's cycle (F6), within a grunt's reach, on a see-saw. A Delay shows no progress, so a second Delay with a shorter `on_delay` driving a powered gem marks the halfway point. *Twists:* the input is an angle sensor on a see-saw, so a plank that bobs under a hop never opens it and only a steady counterweight does (C1); a melting ice block on a plate (about 2 s under a laser, A4) cannot pay a 3 s delay, so the player needs metal or has to shield the ice; `off_delay` keeps the route open for a while after the player leaves; `count_enemies` makes it a grunt parked in the zone for the duration (G7), which a wandering one does not pay. **(untested)**

**H9. Exclusive choice.** *Two pads + two Latches, each with the other in its `reset_sources` + two routes + one shared reset.* Whichever pad is pressed first latches its route and locks the other out: while Latch A is on, B's reset is on, and a reset wins over a set, so B cannot take. The player commits to one route, say the bonus gems (H2) or the short way to the finish (H3). A reset in both `reset_sources` frees them. The Inspector warns of a loop on both nodes; here it is intended. A restore keeps the choice (Latches are saved), so put the checkpoint before the pads. *Twists:* the reset pad is behind the chosen route, so the choice is permanent for that attempt; a Delay (`on_delay` 0.3) on each pad's input so a kicked block that merely brushes a pad does not commit; the routes are bridges (E8) that the other choice leaves out. What two pads pressed in the same physics frame do is untested. **(untested)**

**H10. Fuel gauge.** *A boilable pool with a slow or no faucet + a basin sensor (FILL) + powered gems + a boil turret on a switch + a steam lift (B11, A3).* A laser boil drains the pool 1 volume per second (more with a `boil_volume_scale` above 1), so a lift has a fixed number of seconds in it, and unlike a Delay node the fuel is a visible quantity: the water level is the gauge. A basin sensor on the same pool, with powered gems at its thresholds, puts a number on screen, and the sensor's hysteresis keeps the gems from chattering. The player spends fuel on a ride (B11) or a raised shadow (A3), and a faucet on a button refills it, so the room asks how much water is left for the climb and whether the faucet fills it faster than the beam drains it. A pool with no inflow is a hard budget: once it is empty the steam stops and the block falls wherever it is. *Twists:* a TIMED faucet is a refuel that has to be paid for with a trip; a second pool chained after the first (an overflow) catches what is spilled and is a second tank; slag is never boiled, so a slag pool is no fuel. **(untested)**

### I. Full-room recipes (stacks of the above)

**I1. The Foundry.** Metal block on a ledge, laser aimed at it (A3). Block melts and slag pours into a basin (B2). Water from a faucet (button) crusts it, making a bridge and steam. The player crosses the crust, and the steam carries a surfer to the upper ledge (D6) with a speed trap on the final line. Checkpoint before the pour.

**I2. The Clockwork.** Motorized see-saw (C6) with an angle sensor (clock), a crusher (F1) and a mirror that advances on each sensor rising edge (A2). **I3. The Reservoir.** A basin chain: faucet → basin 1 (spill) → basin 2. An ice block floats up in basin 1 (B1) to reach a laser, which melts it and the water spills to basin 2 (a basin sensor opens a door at 50%). A rail bot sparks basin 2 on a timer (B8), so the swim to the door is timed.

**I4. The Gauntlet.** A surf run: downhill, a wet section (B7) to hit 800, a barrier at `break_speed` 700 (D3), a speed trap (D2) whose door is 12 tiles on, a launch to a swing (C4), and a release onto a crust bridge. Gems along the arc as guides. Grunts in the lane for a chain.

**I5. The Lock Box.** Three receivers in AND (laser, arc, bullet target) around one vault door. Laser via a mirror relay (A1), the arc via ricochet (A6) and the cannon bullet via a counter mirror (F4). Each is solvable on its own and then interacts: the arc and the laser share a mirror, so turning it for one breaks the other.

**I6. The Moat.** A spike field fills the floor between two ledges, with a catwalk above it. The no-tool route is the underside hang across the catwalk (E1): always safe and slow. The fast route retracts the field: an ice block on a plate under a laser (A4) gives a melt-timed window, and powered gems wired to the same source appear only while the field is open (H1), so the fast line is marked exactly when it is safe. **I7. The Kennel.** A shut-in room (E7) whose entry door closes behind the player. Inside are two grunts and a dormant mine. **I8. The Causeway.** A slag lake crossed in three beats, with the checkpoint on ground before the first button (H4). A TIMED button summons a bridge across the first half, ghost on (E8, H6). The far half is a pair of flip-flop stones (E10) on a see-saw clock. The landing is a short shaft whose ladder layer has two sources in OR (E8): a TIMED button on the last stone, and a CRUST sensor on the lake (B2), so a faucet that crusts the slag keeps the ladder out for the way back. Each beat has its own tell: the bridge has the ghost and the blink, the stones have the handoff window, and the ladder has the climb against `duration`. **(untested)**

**I9. The Signal Tower.** One laser and two toggle-mode mirrors in series (A11). The first is driven by an angle sensor on a see-saw (C1): weight on the plank (the player or a metal block) tips it past the threshold and the mirror swings to aim 1, and when the plank tilts back the sensor drops and it swings back. The second is driven by a TOGGLE button across a gap, which the player can instead *kick* into place from the near ledge. Three endpoints: a receiver on the exit door, a receiver that summons a bridge to the bonus gems (E8, H2) and a decoy that drives a spike layer on the stairs. Only one pair of states opens the door, and where the beam lands tells the player which mirror is off (A8). Checkpoint at the foot of the tower (H4): each mirror restores on its saved aim, the plank and button restore their state, and the beam is solved live. **(untested)**

**I10. The Vault.** A shut-in room (E7) with an alarm (F7). A zone just past the entry sets the alarm Latch through a Delay (`on_delay` 2), so a player who crosses the hall quickly never trips it, and a cannon fires down the hall while it is on. Inside are three MOMENTARY pads that have to be pressed in order (H7) and a decoy pad on the way: a Counter on the decoy (`count_to` 3) is three strikes and sets the same alarm Latch through an OR. The last Latch opens the vault door through the camera cue's pass-through (H5), so the door starts moving as the camera arrives. One reset pad in an alcove is in the `reset_sources` of the alarm, the chain and the Counter, so it clears everything at once and is safe to stand on. Every logic node has a visible consequence (a powered gem per stage, the cannon, the door), because none of them shows anything itself (A2). Checkpoint just before the entry (H4): the Latches, the count and both zones come back as saved, with the cannon harmless. **(untested)**

**I11. The Mint.** A metal dispenser beside a button (C9) drops a block on a plate that opens the first door. In the second hall a laser keeps the exit shut through an inverted receiver, and the shield that opens it (A3) has to come from the same dispenser, which has `max_alive` 1: making the shield takes back the plate's block. So the player has to be through the first door before spending the block, and the shield's window is the run down the hall. Checkpoint (H4) after the first door: the dispenser's blocks come back as saved and a restore makes none, so the player presses again for the shield. A camera cue on the first door's source (H5) shows the second hall opening. **(untested)**

**I12. The Trebuchet.** A metal dispenser (C9) above the low end of a launching plank (C10), and a button out of reach of the plank's own platform. The player stands on the raised end, the button drops the block and the throw lands them on a ledge. An angle sensor on the plank (C10) holds a door open for as long as the block stays on the low end, and the door is the way on. Beyond it, an ice block sits on the raised end of a second plank (C11) and a laser at the far side of a pit has to melt it to open the exit. The first block is also the hammer for the second plank, so the player carries it there and gives up the first door to do it, and has to be through it before it shuts. A checkpoint on the ledge saves both planks' angles and the blocks (H4). **(untested)**

**I13. The Cold Store.** A pool between two ledges, a rail bot above it and a freeze turret on a toggle mirror. A button swings the mirror so the beam rests on the pool for its dwell (B10), and the pool becomes a slippery slab. The rail bot's sparks electrify the slab on its drip (B8), so the player hops across between strikes and has to brake on the far ledge. A freeze receiver (A12) beside the far door needs the same beam held on it from a second mirror, and the grunt that patrols the far ledge cuts that beam on every lap, so the player lets it pass before the dwell. A laser behind a glass wall can thaw the slab (the way back is the swim or the hop, the player's choice), and a checkpoint (H4) before the button comes back with the pool liquid and the receiver empty. **(untested)**

**I14. The Porter.** A permanent block carried through a room, one stage at a time. A carbon fiber dispenser (`max_alive` 1) and its button sit at the start (E12). The first lane has an ice plug in it that a laser melts (E13), so the block waits behind the melt. The second is a crusher corridor (F2) whose window the push has to catch with the player a beat behind. The third is a short slag trench: the block is kicked across onto a shelf (B4) and the player takes a catwalk hang (E1) over the same slag. The block ends on a plate that the player's weight completes, and a zone sensor beside it that counts the block regardless of weight; the door follows an AND of the two (C2, D4). A cannon along the last stretch has a metal block parked in its lane as cover (F8), which the player has to kick forward a tile at a time, so the carbon fiber block is brought through *after* the cover. The checkpoint (H4) goes after the crusher: the dispenser's block is restored where it was, no new one is made, and the ice plug and the crusher come back as saved. Each stage uses a different rule of the block that lasts, so the player learns it in one room: it is pushed, not carried, it is light, it does not melt, and it breaks. **(untested)**

**I15. The Dumbwaiter.** A block taken up a shaft in three beats. A carbon fiber dispenser (`max_alive` 1) and its button sit at the foot (E12), with the spawn point over a catwalk shelf above a boilable pool. A TIMED button fires the boil turret and the block rises through the shelf and a deck above it, and the player does not ride it (a rider slows it, and two ground it, B11). When the steam stops it settles on the deck. The deck is a pushed-block room (E12): a plate at threshold 2 is the 2×2 block's weight (C2), a zone sensor beside it counts it whatever it weighs (D4), and a metal block parked in a cannon's lane gives cover for the push (F8). A basin sensor on the pool drives a row of powered gems, the fuel (H10): the shaft is climbed only a few times before the pool is dry, so a wasted lift is a real cost, and a faucet on a far button refills it.
