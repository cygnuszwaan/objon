# Puzzle primitives and concepts

The small reusable patterns (logic, spatial, timing, resource) that the current objects can build, and a catalogue of puzzle concepts that combine them. **The concepts are design proposals derived from the documented rules, not playtested**; one that leans on behaviour the docs only imply is marked **(untested)**, and the mark is removed once it has been tried in a level. What each object does, its knobs and its limits are in `level-design.md`; read `objon.md` (the core: setup, layers and groups, contracts, invariants, docs index) first.

Covers: Puzzle primitives (Logic; Spatial; Timing; Resource); Puzzle concepts (A. Light, mirrors and receivers; B. Water, slag and steam; C. Weight, balance and swinging; D. Momentum and the surf; E. Traversal and spatial; F. Timing, rhythm and traps; G. Enemies as mechanisms; H. Information, routing and pacing; I. Full-room recipes)

## Puzzle primitives

The atoms. Each can be built from the objects in the Object catalogue (`level-design.md`).

**Logic**
- **AND**: several sources on one target with `all_required`. Receivers and plates are best (steady). For arcs/bullets use TIMED latch.
- **OR**: several sources, default. Gives alternate solutions for free.
- **NOT**: `invert` on the device or target. A door that closes when you press the button; a floor that vanishes when you press it (an inverted catwalk layer).
- **Latch / memory**: TOGGLE button, TOGGLE bullet/arc target, a crust (never thins), a jam (latched until power cycle).
- **Timer**: TIMED button / speed trap `hold_time` / receiver `hold_time` / melting ice on a plate / a zone sensor's `hold_time`, which keeps it on for that long after the last body leaves (a delay on leaving, where a TIMED button is a delay after a press).
- **Presence and absence**: a zone sensor is on while `min_count` bodies of the enabled kinds (player, enemies, blocks) are inside it, so `invert` makes it absence ("the room is empty", "nobody is in the shelter"). It sums across kinds, so "the player and a block together" is `min_count` 2 with both on, and it is the one source an enemy can power.
- **Counter / sequence**: a step-mode mirror turning one step per rising edge. Aim at the receiver only at step N.
- **Selector (two-way route)**: a toggle-mode mirror sends one beam to one of two places by the *state* of a source (powered = aim 1, unpowered = aim 0), so exactly one of two receivers is lit and the other is dark (A10). Mirrors in series make a truth table out of geometry: the beam arrives only when every mirror is on the right aim (A13). Unlike the counter it needs no clicks to be counted, because the mirror's angle always shows the source's state.
- **Pulse**: MOMENTARY target; a cannon firing faster than `pulse_length` holds it on.
- **Analogue threshold with hysteresis**: angle sensor, basin sensor, weight plate. Can't chatter.
- **Clock**: crusher/arc/cannon/rail bot cycles with `phase_offset`; a patrolling crawler or rail bot crossing a zone sensor (G8); a motorized see-saw read by an angle sensor produces a square wave **(untested)**.

**Spatial**
- **Gate**: door, breakable barrier, crust, mirror-shielded beam, a retractable spike field (a gate that hurts instead of blocking, so it also works on a surf route), a missing bridge or ladder (a gate that opens by *creating* a route).
- **Gap crossing**: catwalk hang, crust bridge, ice raft, slag raft, swing, rail slide, ramp-and-skim, pad, a summoned bridge.
- **Summoned route**: a powered catwalk or ladder layer is terrain that exists only while a source says so. It extends at once and harmlessly, and the event is the *retract*: whatever stands on it drops, a hanger is released, a climber falls. `invert` turns it round: a floor a source takes away.
- **Foreshadowed terrain**: `show_ghost` draws a retracted layer faintly where it will be, so the player can plan a route through terrain that is not there yet. It says *where*, never *when*. With it off the same layer is a hidden route, a secret rather than a promise.
- **Alternation**: two layers on one source, one of them inverted, are never both out except during the blink: the one that is powering up extends at once and the other holds for `warn_time`. That second is a handoff window in both directions; with `warn_time` 0 it is a hard swap.
- **Keyhole**: a corridor only a crouch/sneak fits, a catwalk only a hang passes, a door you can't hang from.
- **Trip zone**: an invisible rectangle that reacts to a body being somewhere (a sealed room, an ambush, a camera look-ahead, a tripwire). Make it deeper than the fastest body moves in a frame (10 to 13 px for a surf), and give the player something visible to read, since the zone itself is neither drawn nor heard.
- **Weighted lever**: see-saw with a threshold sensor.
- **Aiming**: mirror steps, a toggle-mode mirror's two aims (a sweep the player starts, A12), a tilted mirror see-saw, a moving platform with a mirror, liquid poured as a variable load.

**Timing**
- **Telegraphed cycle**: crusher (dwell windows), arc turret glow, rail bot glow, grunt windup.
- **Time-to-live**: ice (2 s in a beam), metal-on-slag (8 s), flipped crawler (4 s), drop platform (1 s), TIMED hold, a powered bridge (TIMED `duration` plus the layer's `warn_time`).
- **Race**: a TIMED button and a door/route you have to reach before it resets.
- **Warned retract**: a catwalk or ladder layer blinks at 5 Hz for `warn_time` (1 s) before it goes, still solid and still climbable, and a power return during the blink cancels it. A TIMED button pulses in its own last second as well, so a bridge has two tells in a row. The spike layer has no such phase.
- **Unannounced edge**: a powered spike layer extends with no warning phase, so the telegraph has to be built around it: the player's own action that causes it, a visible bullet or beam on its way, a camera cue, or a place to stand that is never spiked. A zone sensor adds an edge the player cannot see at all, so a zone-driven trap uses one of the same telegraphs and puts the zone's edge well before the hazard.

**Resource**
- **A block as tool**: shield, weight, bridge, plug, battery of mass.
- **A liquid as tool**: load, crust-maker, steam-maker, lift, aim.
- **An enemy as tool**: a key (parked at a ledge, standing in a zone), a clock (a fixed patrol that crosses a zone every lap), a counter (the zone stays occupied until the last one is dead), or a stepping stone (a flipped crawler). Killing it is then a choice with a cost.
- **Momentum as tool**: surf speed that is *banked* over a stretch and *spent* on a barrier, trap or launch.

## Puzzle concepts

Each concept lists **ingredients** and how it plays, then **twists** to scale difficulty. They're grouped by what the player has to *think about*. Many combine across groups; the last section has full-room recipes.

### A. Light, mirrors and receivers

**A1. Mirror relay.** *Laser turret + 2 to 4 mirrors + laser receiver + door.* The player kicks mirrors (45° steps) until the beam reaches the receiver. *Twists:* a mirror on a **moving platform** sweeps the beam, so the receiver only lights during a window (needs `hold_time` to bridge); a mirror the player can't reach is turned by a **button's rising edge** (step mode) or aimed by its state (toggle mode, A10) instead; set `kick_rotates` false on switch-owned mirrors; the beam crosses glass and catwalks so the solution can be visible from afar.

**A2. Combination lock.** *Mirror on power + a momentary button + receiver.* Each *click* (crouch while standing on the pad cycles it) advances the mirror one step; the receiver is lit only at the right step. The player has to count clicks from the layout. *Twists:* two mirrors on two buttons, both must align (AND of two receivers); a toggle button instead teaches that only rising edges count; a mirror past the receiver position forces going all the way around (8 steps).

**A3. Disposable shield.** *Laser + ice or metal block + a thing that must not be lit.* A block in a beam is a shield with a lifespan, and melting spawns liquid at its position. Ice gives about 2 s then a puddle (and refreezes when shaded). Metal gives about 3 s, then pours slag. *Twists:* the shield stands on a see-saw (the melt shifts the load); metal melts onto a lower basin and crusts it; the player must *carry* the shield forward by kicking it.

**A4. Beam-and-block race.** *Laser aimed at a block that holds a button/plate down.* The block melts and the plate releases, so a timer is built from the physics. *Twists:* a mirror lets the player re-aim the beam to buy time (shade it, let it cool); two blocks on one plate are required together.

**A5. Window receiver.** *Moving platform with a mirror, or a Stingbot crossing the beam.* The receiver lights briefly each pass. With a short `hold_time` the player has to be at the door when it's lit; with a longer one it's an easy gate. Stingbots flicker receivers behind them by design (their `hold_time` covers it). Used on purpose, a stingbot is a *clock*. **(untested)**

**A6. Arc ricochet.** *Arc turret + mirrors + arc receiver.* The arc is slow (220 px/s) and has finite range (480 px), so the path length matters. It passes enemies and the player's layer, so it can run through a room unseen. *Twists:* two arc receivers on TIMED latches (AND); `phase_offset` to stagger turrets; an arc that fizzles because the path is too long teaches range; water on the route electrifies the pool.

**A7. Shoot-through-a-wall.** *Glass wall + laser receiver on the far side.* Light is visible but unreachable until a door opens or a mirror is repositioned. Same for arcs. Reads well as a puzzle statement ("the receiver is right there").

**A8. Window floor.** *Moving platform with a mirror + laser receiver + inverted spike layer.* The beam sweeps the receiver only as the platform passes (A5), and `hold_time` stretches each pass into a window; the spike field on the route is retracted only while the receiver is lit. The player waits for the platform to come round and crosses before the beam moves on. *Twists:* a longer `hold_time` makes it forgiving; two receivers on two staggered platforms in AND make the window rarer; a glass wall lets the player see the receiver they are waiting on (A7). **(untested)**

**A9. Sweep bridge.** *Moving platform with a mirror + laser + laser receiver + powered catwalk layer over a pit.* A8 with a bridge instead of a spike field. The beam sweeps the receiver as the platform passes, and the bridge is out for the lit time plus the receiver's `hold_time` plus the layer's `warn_time`, so the blink is a telegraph that A8's spike field lacks: the player crosses while it is solid and hurries when it blinks. A laser passes catwalks, so the bridge never shades its own beam and the beam can run along the span in plain sight. *Twists:* a second receiver on a staggered platform in AND makes the window rare; `warn_time` 0 takes the telegraph away; `show_ghost` on teaches where the bridge will be, off makes the player read it from the beam. **(untested)**

**A10. Beam switch.** *Laser turret + toggle-mode mirror + two laser receivers + a TOGGLE button + two doors.* Aim 0 sends the beam to receiver A, aim 1 to receiver B, and the button picks. The mirror follows the button's state, so its angle always shows that state and there are no clicks to count (the contrast with A2). One beam lights one receiver at a time, so doors A and B are never both open: an either/or gate for free. *Twists:* each receiver's `hold_time` (0.3 s) keeps its door open a beat after the beam leaves, a short overlap for a player who flips and runs (0 removes it); `invert` swaps which state means A; with `kick_rotates` on, a kick sets the aim until the button next changes, so one mirror is aimed by kick or by switch; a third receiver on the sweep between the aims lights as the beam passes, as a blip at `hold_time` 0 and a window at the default. **(untested)**

**A11. Hold the beam.** *MOMENTARY button + toggle-mode mirror + laser receiver + a door + an ice or metal block.* The beam reaches the receiver only while the button is down, and the player cannot stand on the pad and be at the door, so a block has to hold it: kick one onto the pad (a block as tool). The crouch-cycle that advances a step-mode mirror (A2) does nothing to a toggle-mode one, so the pad has to stay held. *Twists:* the block is ice under a second laser (A4), so it melts and releases the pad after about 2 s and the mirror swinging back is the visible countdown on a route that closes itself again; a metal block lasts longer but has to be got to the pad; two pads on two mirrors in series (A13) need two blocks. **(untested)**

**A12. Scanner.** *Toggle-mode mirror with a long `rotate_time` (1 to 2 s) + laser + a laser receiver between the two aims + a button + a door.* Pressing the button swings the beam across the room and over the receiver, and the door is open for the pass plus `hold_time`. Where A5 and A8 wait for a platform, the player starts the sweep and can run it again by stepping off and on. Design around three details: the turn eases in and out, so the beam is slowest near its aims and fastest mid-sweep (a receiver mid-sweep needs a longer `hold_time`); a power change mid-sweep turns the beam back from where it is, so releasing early either sends it back over the receiver (a second window) or stops it short of one near the far aim; and the sweep runs the numeric way, so 0 to 270 scans the long way round and 0 to -90 the short way. *Twists:* two receivers at different angles on one door in AND, so one sweep must pass both inside the hold. **(untested)**

**A13. Optical gate.** *Laser + two toggle-mode mirrors in series + two sources (buttons, plates or sensors) + one receiver.* The first mirror sends the beam on to the second only at its aim 1, and the second sends it to the receiver only at its aim 1, so the receiver is lit only with both sources on: an AND made of geometry, steady and visible, with one receiver and no latch. The other three states end at a dead wall, the mirror's back or a decoy. The player reads the beam to see which mirror is wrong, a two-by-two truth table with exactly one answer where A2 needed counting. *Twists:* `invert` on one wire turns it into "press A, release B"; put a decoy receiver at each wrong endpoint, wired to something that bites (a spike layer, a crusher); a third mirror makes eight states and one answer; leave `kick_rotates` on and a mirror whose button is across a gap can be kicked into place instead (it stays until that button next changes). **(untested)**

### B. Water, slag and steam

**B1. Float up.** *Faucet + basin + ice block + a ledge above.* Fill the basin to float the block up to the ledge. Ice floats at about 80% submerged and follows the rising surface (even with riders). *Twists:* a metal block sinks, so it must be carried another way; `rest_fraction` past 1.0 means too heavy (a big block with riders sinks); a basin sensor opens a door at 50% fill and a drain brings the level back; riders are limited by `rider_load_tiles`.

**B2. Crust bridge.** *Slag basin + water faucet.* Pouring water on slag crusts it into a walkable slab and makes steam. The player has to pour enough, in the right place. Pre-filled slag you can't cross becomes a floor. *Twists:* water has to come from a melting ice block (laser); the crust makes steam that is a **surf lift** column for the next section; the steam rises into a ceiling gap and stops.

**B3. Under the crust.** *Crust + a drain + the crust underside hang.* Crust, then drain the liquid under it to open a void, then **hang and brachiate under the crust** across a hazard. The slag lake above is safe (crust), and the void below is the route. *Twists:* the void only exists while the drain is open; a basin sensor on CRUST powers the door that gives you access; a second basin chained after the first receives the overflow.

**B4. Slag raft.** *Slag basin + metal block + a gap.* Metal floats on slag but lasts about 8 s. Kick it across and ride. A tight timeline. *Twists:* ice dropped on the slag instead bursts to water and crusts instead (the other solution); the raft is needed to *carry a weight* across onto a plate; a tread crawler flipped as a stepping stone is a free third option.

**B5. Evaporation.** *Laser (boils_water) + boilable basin + basin sensor.* Boiling drains the pool, so a pool that blocks the way becomes passable. The sensor releases a door when it's empty. The faucet refills it, and the pool *settles where the beam meets the surface*. *Twists:* a faucet-refilled pool that never empties unless the beam stays on; steam from boiling lifts a surfer; slag is never boiled, so this only works on water.

**B6. Hydraulic scale.** *Water or slag poured onto a see-saw plank + angle sensor.* Liquid is load: water 1 per tile of volume, slag 3. A faucet pouring on one end tips the plank until the sensor trips. *Twists:* the plank has `mirror_surface` so the pour *aims a beam*; a laser melts an ice block on the plank so the load turns into water and keeps changing **(untested)**.

**B7. Wet run-up.** *Melting ice on a slope + a surf.* Melt ice at the top of a slope: the water flows down and wets the floor, raising surf top speed from 600 to 800 and reducing friction. A barrier with `break_speed` 700 can only be broken on wet ground. *Twists:* the laser has to be redirected to start the melt; the water has to reach the floor or it dries (decays 0.4 s after leaving).

**B8. Safe water.** *Rail bot spark or arc turret over a pool.* The pool is electrified for 1 s after each strike. The swim is possible only in the gaps between strikes, and the telegraph glow tells you when. *Twists:* the pool can't be skimmed while electrified, so a surf over it fails; `conducts_sparks` off on a decoy basin; the strike comes from a ricocheting arc and electrifies a pool far from the turret.

**B9. Relaxation oscillator.** *Basin + steady faucet + basin sensor FILL + drain faucet wired to the sensor.* The pool fills to 50%, the sensor opens the drain, it falls below the threshold minus hysteresis, the drain closes, and it refills. The result is a slow periodic signal driving doors, crushers, or a plate **(untested; the docs only guarantee one swing without inflow)**.

**B10. Floating key.** *Faucet + basin + ice block + a zone high in a shaft (`count_player` and `count_blocks`, `min_count` 2) + door.* The zone sits where only a raised block can put the player: fill the basin so the ice block floats up with the player riding it (B1; a floating block follows the surface even with riders), and the block and the player are both in the zone at once. Either alone is a count of 1, and a metal block sinks, so the ice is the only lift. Shape the shaft so the player cannot jump into the zone from a ledge. A drain brings the level back, and the door closes `hold_time` after they drop out of the zone. *Twists:* `count_player` off makes the block a plug rather than a lift, so the player only has to get it up there; a basin sensor on the same basin in AND adds a fill condition; `hold_time` is the margin for hopping off at the top. **(untested)**

**B11. Crust latch.** *Slag basin + water faucet + basin sensor (CRUST) + a powered ladder or catwalk layer.* A crust never thins, so a CRUST sensor is a permanent latch, and a layer wired to it stays out for good once the pour has happened. One pour then gives two routes: the crust is a floor and a steam column for a surfer (B2, D6), and a ladder appears for a walker. Crust fields are saved, so a restore keeps it. *Twists:* `invert` makes the same pour take a route away (the bridge you came by is gone once the slag is crusted), a point of no return the player chooses; the ladder is hidden until the pour (`show_ghost` off) so the player has to guess what the faucet does; a second basin chained after the first receives the overflow and has its own sensor in AND. **(untested)**

### C. Weight, balance and swinging

**C1. Counterweight.** *See-saw + metal block (weight 4 per tile) + angle sensor + door.* The player alone (weight 2) can't tip the plank, so the block must be positioned first and the player completes the load. *Twists:* the door only holds while the plank stays tipped, so something must be left on it; the plank's motor fights the player's weight and you only win while standing on the high end; the block is on the plank *and* the plate.

**C2. Plate budgeting.** *Weight plate with a threshold (e.g. 6) + blocks of several weights.* Weights: player 2, ice 1×1 = 1, metal 1×1 = 4 (a 2×1 metal is 8). The player needs to find a combination (metal block + player on top counts as stacked weight; 4 + 2 = 6). *Twists:* a plate under a melting ice block releases on its own (a timer); two plates wired AND; a plate that also *tips a see-saw* so the weight is spent twice.

**C3. Pump the swing.** *Swing + angle sensor on it + door.* The player grabs the bar and pumps with left/right until the arc passes the threshold. *Twists:* the sensor is on the *far* side so the player must release at the right moment; the arc crosses a spike pit; `free_rotation` for a full loop (the sensor is powered through that sector once per turn, so use a limited swing for a sensor); a moving platform under the arc.

**C4. Momentum into the swing.** *Surf ramp + swing.* A surfer in the air grabs the bar and passes the **whole surf speed** into the swing (400 px/s on a 64 px rod is about 6 rad/s), then releases to launch far past where a jump could go. *Twists:* the ±80° limit pins a fast catch and swings it back (use `free_rotation` for a big arc); a gem trail marks the release point.

**C5. Plank-as-ramp.** *See-saw on a surf route.* A tilted plank is a ramp from the low end, but the raised end is a lip that stops a fast surf like a wall. The player must arrive on the low side, or hit it when the plank is level, or make the plank level by standing a counterweight on it. Flat plank ends sit flush at extremes.

**C6. Motor and brake.** *Motorized see-saw + riders.* A heavy enough rider on the high side holds it against the stop. A see-saw that oscillates becomes a moving ramp/bridge the player times, and an angle sensor on it is a clock.

**C7. Plug the plate.** *Weight plate (threshold above the player's own weight) + a block + an inverted spike layer.* A source that is on retracts an inverted layer, so a block left on the plate holds the field open; kicking it off re-arms the spikes on that frame. The player has to bring the block to the plate, then cross. *Twists:* an ice block under a laser makes the window the melt time (A4: about 2 s lit), so the player has to run; metal lasts about 3 s and then pours slag onto the plate; a second plate in AND needs a second block. **(untested)**

**C8. Headcount.** *Zone (`count_blocks`, `min_count` 3) + blocks of mixed weights + door.* The zone counts bodies, not weight (C2 counts weight), so a 1×1 ice block and a 1×1 metal block are worth the same. The puzzle is getting enough blocks into an area, and what is easy to place differs: an ice block slides about 4 tiles on a kick, a metal block about 1. *Twists:* a laser melts one of the three once it is in place (a melted block frees itself and leaves the count after `hold_time`), so it has to be shielded or timed (A3); the zone is over a basin, so floating ice counts but a sunk metal block below the zone does not; `count_player` as well, so the player has to stand in the zone with the stack. **(untested)**

### D. Momentum and the surf

**D1. Banked speed.** *Downhill + flat + speed trap or barrier.* The level is designed so the *only* way to reach 450+ is a particular line (downhill matched, wet floor, a clean landing). *Twists:* a belt against the surfer is a speed tax (or a speed assist); a tilt-mismatched section silently drains speed; a pad on the line launches without a tilt penalty.

**D2. Speed trap door.** *Speed trap + door.* The trap reads a *surfer's* ground speed as they cross its line, and powers the door for `hold_time` seconds (5 default). The door is far, so the player must surf *and then run* before it closes. *Twists:* `hold_time` 0 = permanent; two traps in an AND with different thresholds; the line is across a pool, so skim at 180+ speed *and* arrive at 450+.

**D3. Barrier gauntlet.** *Breakable barriers with different `break_speed`s along a run.* The player must still be fast after paying friction. Mix `break_from` LEFT/RIGHT so you must approach from the right side. Barriers don't pay points, but enemies bowled between them do. *Twists:* the barrier blocks a laser beam, so smashing it opens the line of fire (and *lights a receiver* through the hole); the run continues into a launch.

**D4. Ice shove.** *Surf + ice block + weight plate far away.* The surf is a long-range kick: shove an ice block at speed and it slides up to about 18 tiles on its own to a distant plate, or into a mine cluster. *Twists:* a metal block moves much less per shove, a ledge dumps the block, a wall in front of the block refuses the shove (the surfer stops like at a wall), a stack of blocks.

**D5. Skim crossing.** *Settled water pool + a surf run-up.* The pool is a road if you arrive at 180+. If you drop below, you swim (and you'd end up in an electrified or slag pool if it changes). *Twists:* crust over the far half makes it a plain floor; an arc/spark strikes while you're crossing; the basin's rim is a natural ramp out.

**D6. Steam launch.** *Steam column (crust formation, a boiled basin) + surf.* The board rises through the column (up to 120 px/s) to a ledge or an upper route. It only works while surfing, and ceilings stop it. A non-surfing player can't use it at all, so it is a surf-only shortcut. *Twists:* a second route for non-surfers that is longer; the column only exists while the laser boils.

**D7. Bowling lane.** *Grunts, tread crawlers and drillbots (from behind only) along a fast lane.* A chain pays 5/10/20 points within a 2 s window. Drillbots punish approaching from the drill side; mines stop the surf and arm; use a barrier to break the lane at a precise speed. *Twists:* a bowled-over grunt that is mid-windup is still bowled; a lane that bends so one enemy faces you.

**D8. Belt and brake.** *Conveyor running against the surfer + stall gate.* If belt speed ≈ the board's speed, the surf stalls. A powered belt lets a button *change what the run does*: belt off = easy, belt on = need more speed. A speed trap *reads* the surf plus belt ground speed.

**D9. Speed gate.** *Speed trap + inverted spike layer across a surf run.* Crossing the trap's line at 450 or more retracts the field for `hold_time` (5 s); a slower pass leaves the spikes out, and a surfer who rides into them is knocked off the board (a surf ends through `take_hit()`). Where D2 stops a slow player with a door, this punishes them. *Twists:* `require_surfing` so a walker cannot trip it; a `hold_time` short enough that the player has to keep the speed down a long field; a second trap with a higher threshold in AND. **(untested)**

### E. Traversal and spatial

**E1. Underside route.** *Catwalk (own layer) over spikes or slag.* The player hangs by crouch+jump from standing on the catwalk (or jumps up into it), brachiates across, and mantles at the end. A ladder cell in the path hands off to a climb. *Twists:* the catwalk ends and the player must swing/jump; terrain under the catwalk blocks brachiating; a crust underside as the continuation; the catwalk is a powered layer, so it lets go of the hanger when it retracts (E8).

**E2. Hang from things that move.** *Doors, blocks, see-saw ends, moving and drop platforms are all hangable.* A door that opens releases a hanger (a shaft with a door the player is hanging from), a drop platform releases at its drop, a floating block bobs. These are timed vertical routes. *Twists:* a block that melts under the player; the see-saw end is only hangable within 15° and slowly.

**E3. Rail ride.** *Steep and flat rail segments + rail bot.* Slide down the steep part (speed builds), brachiate along the flat part (friction brings you to a stop), and jump past a bot (contact hurts, and knocks you off). Catching a slope keeps a share of your speed. Jumping off carries the slide's velocity. *Twists:* the rail passes over a pool or slag; sparks drip down on the route below; a laser kills the bot (the usual solution) or a mine blast does.

**E4. Crouch gap + sneak.** *Low corridor* that only a crouched player fits, a long one needs sneak at 50 px/s while a Grunt or timing cycle pushes pressure. Standing back up is blocked by a real overlap check under a ceiling, so you creep out. *Twists:* crouching while a crusher cycles; a button *inside* the corridor requires a crouch-press (the crouch itself is the click).

**E5. Ladders and landings.** A ladder through a catwalk is a clean route up; you can brachiate onto it from beneath. *Twists:* a spike tile in a ladder cell breaks climbing (don't do that); a ladder that ends at a door; a ladder that is not there until a button summons it (E10).

**E6. Pad chains.** *Jump pads aimed by rotation + a moving platform or swing as the landing.* Pads keep the surf's speed. Powered pads are *live only when a source says so*, so a pad route can be a timed window. Blocks also get launched (lighter ones go higher).

**E7. Shut-in.** *Zone (`count_player`) covering a room + a door on `invert` at its entry + a puzzle inside whose exit is gated another way.* Put the zone just past the doorway. The inverted door starts open, closes behind the player as they step in, and stays shut while they are inside. A door never crushes and waits if the player stops in the doorway, so the trap cannot hurt anyone. Leaving by the exit empties the zone and the entry reopens `hold_time` later. A checkpoint inside the room restores it sealed, because the zone and the door's frame are both saved, so a retry never finds the door open. *Twists:* a `hold_time` of several seconds is a lock-out timer on the entry; the exit door is wired to the room's own puzzle, so the zone and the puzzle together are the only way out; two rooms share a zone for a sealed pair. **(untested)**

**E8. Summoned bridge.** *TIMED button + powered catwalk layer over a pit (spikes or slag).* The bridge extends the moment the button is pressed and goes `duration` plus `warn_time` later: the button pulses in its last second and the layer then blinks for `warn_time`. The crossing budget is that sum, and a player standing on it when it goes simply drops (the collision is gone), so size it for a real crossing with margin, as in F7. The first bridge in a level can have `show_ghost` on, so the player sees what the button is for. *Twists:* a second TIMED button midway on a long span restarts the countdown (two buttons are OR, so it stays out while either is on); the way back is gone, so the far side needs its own button or the underside route (E1); a player hanging under the bridge is released as it retracts, with the regrab lockout, so the hang route has the same clock; `warn_time` 0 for a strict version. **(untested)**

**E9. Trapdoor.** *Catwalk layer on `invert` + a button + a load standing on it + a destination below.* The floor is out while the source is off, so pressing the button retracts it (after the blink) and whatever stands on it drops: a metal block onto a weight plate or into slag (C2, B4), an ice block into a basin or into a laser line (A3), a block onto a see-saw end (C1). It delivers a block to a place a kick cannot reach. At the default `warn_time` the blink is a second of slack to kick the load off again or to change one's mind; at 0 it is instant. A catwalk lets water through, so a melting ice block on the trapdoor pours straight through it. *Twists:* the trapdoor sits over the same basin the block is meant for, so the player has to choose when; two trapdoors on one button drop two blocks together; the player stands on it too and has to jump clear when it blinks. **(untested)**

**E10. Race the ladder.** *TIMED button at the foot of a shaft + powered ladder layer, optionally a powered catwalk deck at the top on the same source.* The ladder stays climbable through the blink and then goes, dropping anyone on it on the next frame, so the shaft height against `duration` plus `warn_time` is the difficulty. A deck with a ladder through it is a two-layer lift on one source. *Twists:* a ledge or button halfway up restarts the countdown for a tall shaft; an underside route (E1) ends at a powered ladder that takes the hanger by up/down, so the hand-off has the same clock; the ladder layer sits at the same position as the static `Ladders` layer so a static lower half and a summoned upper half join; a checkpoint at the foot, not on the deck (H4). Climb speed is not in the docs, so time a real climb before sizing `duration`. **(untested)**

**E11. Flip-flop stepping stones.** *Two catwalk layers on one source, one with `invert`, laid as alternate stones across a pit.* Whichever set has just been powered extends at once and the other blinks for `warn_time` before it goes, so for that second both sets exist and the player can hop across; the window opens in both directions. The source can be the player's own TOGGLE button, or a clock (a motorized see-saw's angle sensor, C6, or a patrol through a zone, G8). *Twists:* `warn_time` 0 makes it a hard swap, and a stone of the wrong set drops its rider, so the player has to read the rhythm elsewhere; the clock is faster than the player can cross in one go, so a static catwalk stone midway (on its own layer, kept off the powered cells) gives somewhere to wait for the next window; a ladder layer on the same source joins the far end; the pit is slag, so the margin is a fall into it. **(untested)**

### F. Timing, rhythm and traps

**F1. Phase corridor.** *Crushers, sawwheels or cannons with `phase_offset`.* The spike is the dangerous end and the arm (side) is solid; dwell windows are the safe beats. Stagger so the player has to learn a rhythm. *Twists:* a powered crusher parks *safe* when off, so a button is a pause; a TIMED button pauses it just long enough.

**F2. Jam it.** *Crusher + metal block.* Kick metal under the extending spike; the crusher jams, parks and stays harmless. The corridor is permanently safe, but the block is stuck there (and wired crushers reset on a power cycle, which makes a trap-reset you can trigger by accident). *Twists:* **ice is the wrong tool** (it shatters into water, which might be wanted: water onto slag crusts it); the metal must first be melted off a laser line, then moved.

**F3. Shatter delivery.** *Crusher above a basin + ice block.* An ice block shattered by the crusher bursts into water **at the crusher**. Water onto slag below crusts it. A way to *deliver water on a timer*. **(untested)**

**F4. Bullet counter.** *Cannon + mirror with `bullet_rotates` + bullet targets.* Each bullet turns the mirror a step and is consumed. The bullet target gets hit only when the mirror aims right. A cannon firing every second advances it without the player. *Twists:* the player kicks it too (race); a TOGGLE bullet target latches the result.

**F5. Cannon pulse trains.** *Cannon + MOMENTARY bullet target.* Fire rate shorter than `pulse_length` keeps it powered; longer lets it flicker. Choose the cannon's rate to decide whether a door is steady or blinks.

**F6. Telegraph reading.** *Arc turret and rail bot glows (0.45 s), grunt windup (amber).* Teaching language: amber = coming, red = live. Use it consistently.

**F7. Safe window.** *TIMED button + inverted spike layer + a corridor.* Pressing the button retracts the field for `duration`; the player crosses before it ends. The spikes extend with no warning and hurt a player still in them at once, so `duration` is the only clock: size it for a real crossing with margin. *Twists:* a second TIMED button midway restarts the countdown for a long corridor (pressing a button again restarts its timer; two buttons are OR, so the field stays open while either is on); a button inside a crouch gap (E4) so the press is the crouch; a TOGGLE button instead lets the player latch it open and puts the question on what else the same source drives. **(untested)**

**F8. Pulse floor.** *Cannon + MOMENTARY bullet target + spike layer.* Each shot holds the target on for its pulse (0.3 s by default), and a `fire_rate` longer than that lets it flicker (F5), so the field is out for a blink every shot and retracted otherwise. The bullet is visible in flight, so it is the telegraph: the spikes extend as it lands. Two cannons on different `fire_rate`s feeding two layers drift in and out of phase, which makes a rhythm the player has to learn (F1). *Twists:* an inverted layer is retracted for a blink and dangerous the rest of the time, which only suits a very short gap; a mirror or barrier in the bullet's path lets the player stop the pulses. **(untested)**

**F9. Tripwire cannon.** *Zone (`count_player`) + cannon + a corridor.* A powered cannon fires one bullet at once on a rising edge and then every `fire_rate`, so entering the zone starts the barrage. Set the zone back from the muzzle so a bullet is in flight, and visible, before it can reach the player: the bullet is the telegraph. *Twists:* `hold_time` keeps the cannon firing for that long after the player leaves; `invert` gives a cannon that fires except while the player stands in a shelter zone, and `hold_time` is the grace before it resumes; `count_blocks` instead of the player lets the player kick a block into the zone to start it from a distance, and a bullet target at the far end turns that into a remote switch (F5). **(untested)**

### G. Enemies as mechanisms

**G1. Boot the mine.** *Mine + grunts.* Kick a mine into a grunt group (a mine that slides faster than 40 into a wall or any enemy detonates; a blast hits everything in 40 px and chains to other mines). *Twists:* a laser arms a mine from far away (arm, never damage); a rail bot overhead killed by a mine blast; mines on both sides of a door.

**G2. Crawler stepping stones.** *Tread crawler between two safe spots over spikes or slag.* Stomp it flipped (you bounce), then stand on it. It stays pinned while you stand there. After the top is clear it rights itself after 4 s. *Twists:* one stone, two players of time; the crawler flips on the far side of a pit because its patrol ends there.

**G3. Lure.** *Grunt + laser.* A chasing grunt stops at a ledge and walks into a beam; the laser does 1 damage per 0.5 s, so two beams or a long exposure kill it. *Twists:* an enemy in the beam *shields* a receiver behind it (a Stingbot crossing it flickers it); a grunt chased onto a plate does nothing (enemies weigh nothing), but one parked in a zone sensor does (G7).

**G4. Stingbot windows.** *Stingbot landing for 2 s every 8 s.* The recharge is a safe window. Use it as a timed door-opening or a way to cross a hazard *under* its flight path. It doesn't avoid obstacles, so a wall can *trap* it. **(untested)**

**G5. Drillbot lane.** *Drillbot patrolling a narrow ledge.* Bowl it from behind (surf), or kick it three times *from behind* (its body is harmless), or cross over the top. *Twists:* the free-patrol (`patrol_distance` 0) walks to ledges, so it patrols the whole platform.

**G6. Clear the room.** *Zone over an arena (`count_enemies`) + a door on `invert` + grunts, a crawler and a mine.* The zone is powered while any enemy is inside, so the inverted door stays shut until the last one is dead (an enemy leaves the group as it starts to die, so the door opens about `hold_time` after the last kill). A dormant mine and a flipped crawler still count, so a mine has to be booted into the others (G1) and a crawler has to be killed, not just stomped. A Stingbot flies, so the zone must cover its airspace, and it has no pathing, so wall off anywhere it could stick or the room soft-locks. *Twists:* two arenas in AND; `min_count` 2 opens the door when only one enemy is left, which the player can then choose to leave alone; a checkpoint inside restores the enemies and the zone together. **(untested)**

**G7. Park the lure.** *Grunt + a ledge + a zone at the ledge's edge (`count_enemies`, `count_player` off) + door, with the player across a gap.* A chasing grunt stops at a ledge (G3), so it parks in a predictable place: put the zone there, and the door is held open for as long as it stands in it. G3 kills the lure; here it has to stay alive, and the player has to get through while keeping it interested. Leaving its aggro range may let it wander off and shut the door, which is the part to test. *Twists:* two grunts on two ledges in AND, with the player between them; a mine kicked beside the parked grunt would blast it, so the player's own tool becomes the failure state; the door is a spike layer on `invert`, so a grunt that wanders off re-arms the route. **(untested)**

**G8. Patrol clock.** *Tread crawler (or a rail bot on its rail) on a fixed patrol + a zone over part of it (`count_enemies`) + a door or spike layer.* Each pass through the zone powers the source for the pass plus `hold_time`, a window that repeats every lap, and the crawler's turn pause at each end is part of the period. The player times a crossing to the lap. A rail bot gives a ceiling clock whose sparks also drip on the route below (E3). *Twists:* a mirror that turns one step per rising edge counts laps for free, so the player waits N laps (A2 without the button); flip the crawler and stand on it inside the zone (G2) and the pinned enemy holds the source on for as long as the player stays; kill the clock and the source stays off, so give the route a second way (OR) or the player can soft-lock. **(untested)**

**G9. Cut the span.** *Catwalk layer on `invert` (the bridge exists while the source is off) + a momentary button on the far side + a chasing grunt.* A grunt chases the player across the bridge, the player steps onto the button, and the bridge blinks and retracts under the grunt. Where it lands is the design: a pit it cannot leave, or a spot that matters. A block kicked onto the button holds the cut after the player steps off (otherwise the bridge returns, since the button is momentary). Because the grunt only stops at a ledge when one is ahead of it, it keeps walking during the blink, so the player has to be on the button while the grunt is still on the span. What a grunt does after the fall is not documented, so test it. *Twists:* the grunt is parked at the far end by a zone (G7) and the cut is what keeps it there; two bridges and two grunts. **(untested)**

### H. Information, routing and pacing

**H1. Gem trail on a switch.** *Powered gems + a source.* The gems of a trail appear only while powered. A timed source shows the route briefly; a latched one shows it permanently. Trails double as **route hints** for launch arcs. Powered gems are free to place in quantity. *Note:* use 5 or 10 point values (popups exist for 5, 10, 20, 50).

**H2. Bonus route.** *Bonus gems (50 pts, not part of the unlock %)* guard risky detours. Pair with a medkit at the end of the risky path.

**H3. Locked finish.** *Finish with `require_all`* gems + a button, or OR for two routes. A level where 100% gems are impossible without the button, and a level where the button is *optional*, change the pacing.

**H4. Checkpoint-aware design.** *Place checkpoints before expensive resets.* Blocks, doors, basins (fill and crust) and enemies are restored; liquid particles, arcs, bullets and sparks aren't, so avoid designs where success depends on an in-flight particle. A restore always sets dangerous things harmless (mines dormant, grunts recovering, drips restarted). A powered bridge or ladder is re-derived from its source: with the source off it gets one blink and retracts, so a checkpoint on a bridge drops the respawned player. Put the checkpoint on ground, or on a bridge whose source a restore brings back on (a TOGGLE button, a crust).

**H5. Cue the opening.** *Camera cue + inverted spike layer on the same source.* The player presses a button; the field in a far shaft retracts and the camera pans to it, so the new route is visible without a hint sign. The cue plays on the rising edge of its own power and the layer is inverted, so both fire on the same press. Keep the hold short, because the world keeps running and input is not locked. *Twists:* `once` so the pan does not repeat on a TOGGLE button; a cue with `invert` on the same source shows the spikes coming back out, which teaches what the button did; a `delay` lines the pan up with the player leaving a calm spot; the same cue on a bridge's source shows the span extending (H7). **(untested)**

**H6. Look-ahead trigger.** *Zone (`count_player`) + camera cue (`once`) + a far door or route.* Walking to a spot pans the camera to what has just changed, with no button to press (H5 is the deliberate-act version). The cue plays only on a rising edge, never at startup or after a restore, and `once` is saved, so a retry does not replay it. Put the zone on a calm stretch of the route, because input is not locked while the camera is away, and use `delay` to line the pan up with a door opening. *Twists:* the zone sits on the far side of a hazard, so the pan is a reward and not a distraction; the same zone also drives the door itself, so one trigger opens it and shows it. **(untested)**

**H7. The telegraph ladder.** *Powered bridge + `show_ghost` + a camera cue on the same source.* Summoned terrain has three tells and they say different things: the **ghost** says where it will be, the **cue** (H5) says that it has appeared, the **blink** says when it ends. Teach in that order: the first bridge in a level has the ghost on and a cue, the second has the cue only, the third has neither and relies on the player expecting a button to do something. A ghost is a promise and a hidden layer is a secret, so turn it off only where the player is meant to hunt. The spike layer has no blink, so a ghost and a cue are its only tells. *Twists:* a lower `ghost_opacity` for a dark room; the ghost is on for the layer the player will need later and off for the one they need now. **(untested)**

### I. Full-room recipes (stacks of the above)

**I1. The Foundry.** Metal block on a ledge, laser aimed at it (A3). Block melts and slag pours into a basin (B2). Water from a faucet (button) crusts it, making a bridge and steam. The player crosses the crust, and the steam carries a surfer to the upper ledge (D6) with a speed trap on the final line. Checkpoint before the pour.

**I2. The Clockwork.** Motorized see-saw (C6) with an angle sensor (clock), a crusher (F1) and a mirror that advances on each sensor rising edge (A2). The receiver on the exit lights only at one step, so the player has to *wait for the right beat and be at the door*. A second receiver needs the player to jam the crusher with a metal block (F2).

**I3. The Reservoir.** A basin chain: faucet → basin 1 (spill) → basin 2. An ice block floats up in basin 1 (B1) to reach a laser, which melts it and the water spills to basin 2 (a basin sensor opens a door at 50%). A rail bot sparks basin 2 on a timer (B8), so the swim to the door is timed.

**I4. The Gauntlet.** A surf run: downhill, a wet section (B7) to hit 800, a barrier at `break_speed` 700 (D3), a speed trap (D2) whose door is 12 tiles on, a launch to a swing (C4), and a release onto a crust bridge. Gems along the arc as guides. Grunts in the lane for a chain.

**I5. The Lock Box.** Three receivers in AND (laser, arc, bullet target) around one vault door. Laser via a mirror relay (A1), the arc via ricochet (A6) and the cannon bullet via a counter mirror (F4). Each is solvable on its own and then interacts: the arc and the laser share a mirror, so turning it for one breaks the other.

**I6. The Moat.** A spike field fills the floor between two ledges, with a catwalk above it. The no-tool route is the underside hang across the catwalk (E1): always safe and slow. The fast route retracts the field: an ice block on a plate under a laser (C7, A4) gives a melt-timed window, and powered gems wired to the same source appear only while the field is open (H1), so the fast line is marked exactly when it is safe. A speed trap at the far end (D9) can make the run a surf instead. Checkpoint before the plate (H4): the block, plate and sources are restored, and the field follows them.

**I7. The Kennel.** A shut-in room (E7) whose entry door closes behind the player. Inside are two grunts and a dormant mine. A second zone over the room counts enemies and drives an inverted exit door (G6), which opens only once the room is clear. The mine is the player's tool (G1) and the grunts' windups are the telegraph (F6). A camera cue on the exit's source (H5) pans to it opening. Checkpoint just before the entry (H4): the doors, both zones and the enemies come back as saved, with the mine dormant. **(untested)**

**I8. The Causeway.** A slag lake crossed in three beats, with the checkpoint on ground before the first button (H4). A TIMED button summons a bridge across the first half, ghost on (E8, H7). The far half is a pair of flip-flop stones (E11) on a see-saw clock. The landing is a short shaft whose ladder layer has two sources in OR (E10): a TIMED button on the last stone, and a CRUST sensor on the lake (B11), so a faucet that crusts the slag keeps the ladder out for the way back. Each beat has its own tell: the bridge has the ghost and the blink, the stones have the handoff window, and the ladder has the climb against `duration`. **(untested)**

**I9. The Signal Tower.** One laser and two toggle-mode mirrors in series (A13). The first is driven by an angle sensor on a see-saw (C1): weight on the plank (the player or a metal block) tips it past the threshold and the mirror swings to aim 1, and when the plank tilts back the sensor drops and it swings back. The second is driven by a TOGGLE button across a gap, which the player can instead *kick* into place from the near ledge. Three endpoints: a receiver on the exit door, a receiver that summons a bridge to the bonus gems (E8, H2) and a decoy that drives a spike layer on the stairs. Only one pair of states opens the door, and where the beam lands tells the player which mirror is off (A10). Checkpoint at the foot of the tower (H4): each mirror restores on its saved aim, the plank and button restore their state, and the beam is solved live. **(untested)**
