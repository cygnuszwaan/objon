# Power: sources, devices and conductors

The power contract (`PowerTarget`), the powered devices, every power source (button, speed trap, laser and arc receivers, bullet target, weight plate, angle sensor, basin sensor), the conductor layers and the door. Read `objon.md` (the core: setup, layers and groups, contracts, invariants, docs index) first.

Covers: Powered devices; Power contract; Button; Speed trap; Laser receiver; Arc receiver; Bullet target; Weight plate; Angle sensor; Basin sensor; Conductors (Connections; Circuits; Terminals are loose ends; Lit/dim; Editor overlay); Door; Camera cue

- **Powered devices** (each scene has a `PowerTarget` child and an `invert` export on its root; unwired they behave as authored): **laser turret** powered = beam on (`set_enabled`); **arc turret** powered = firing (a rising edge fires one arc at once, see Arc turret); **conveyor** powered = running (`set_enabled`; move the `PowerTarget` along the belt to choose where a wire attaches); **faucet** powered = open (`is_open`; `PowerTarget` at the tile centre); **moving platform** powered = running, unpowered = the tween is paused where it is; **cannon** powered = firing (its `Timer` stops/starts; switching on fires one bullet immediately, via a deferred call, then the `Timer` runs, so the next shot is a full `fire_rate` later; a cannon already powered or unwired at load keeps its ordinary first shot after `fire_rate`); **crusher** powered = cycling, unpowered = it finishes its current leg and parks fully retracted at the start of the retracted dwell (never frozen mid-stroke), resuming from there (a power cycle, off then on, also clears a metal jam: see Sawwheel / Crusher hazards); **sawwheel** powered = spinning and dangerous, unpowered = blade paused, hum stopped, no damage; **jump pad** powered = live, unpowered = inert and dimmed (`modulate`; a pad that becomes live under a player launches on the next entry); **mirror** each rising edge of the final power state turns it one step (`rotate_step(1)`), falling edges do nothing, the startup state is not an edge, and an edge during a turn is ignored like a kick (set `kick_rotates` false on a switch-owned mirror); **see-saw** powered = motorized, unpowered = free (see Rotating bodies); **door** as below; **camera cue** a rising edge pans the camera to it (see Camera cue); **powered spike layer** powered = spikes out (dangerous), unpowered = hidden and harmless, unwired = out (see Powered spike layer in `hazards-traps.md`). All user-verified in-editor.
- **Power contract** (`res://scenes/interactive/power_target.gd`, `class_name PowerTarget`, `@tool` `Node2D`): duck-typed like `take_hit()`/`get_conveyor_speed()`. A **source** emits `powered_changed(on)` and answers `is_powered()` (`Button` and conductor circuits); a **device** implements `set_powered(on: bool)` and optionally `set_powered_initial(on)`. `PowerTarget` sits between them as a child of the device (`debug_power_target.gd` extends it as a disposable test device to delete once real devices exist): `@export var sources: Array[Node]` (typed node-picker), `invert`, `all_required` (default OR). **Invert** may also be exported on the parent device's root script (`@export var invert: bool`), which puts the toggle in the device's own Inspector; `PowerTarget` reads it from its parent (either one inverts, both set = inverted). `sources` and `all_required` stay on the `PowerTarget` child (Editable Children). **No valid sources = not driven**: the device keeps its authored state, so every device scene carries a `PowerTarget` child and behaves as before until something is wired to it. The first recompute is deferred until every `_ready()` (and the conductor layers' own deferred build) has run, so a device is never driven before it is ready; that first apply, if within `STARTUP_FRAMES` (3) of load, goes to the parent's `set_powered_initial(on)` if it has one (door snaps to its state; mirror records it without counting an edge), else `set_powered(on)`. After that `_apply_power(on)` runs once per actual change (default: forwards to the parent's `set_powered()`; subclasses override it), then `power_changed(on)` is emitted. Joins group `"power_target"`; `add_source(node)` attaches a source at runtime (duplicates ignored). `debug_toggle_key` (default `KEY_NONE`) flips the final state from the keyboard so any device can be triggered remotely while testing; it XORs with the computed state, and an unlinked device starts from unpowered. A `draw_links` editor overlay draws a line from the target to each source, orange when inverted. The cell a conductor wire attaches to is the cell the `PowerTarget` node itself sits in, so inside a multi-tile device the child is moved to the tile the wire should end at.
- **Button** (`res://scenes/interactive/button.gd`): `Area2D` power source, joins group `"power_source"`; node origin is the centre of its 16×16 tile. `sprites/button1_<colour>.png` (searched in `sprites/interactive/` then `sprites/`), 16×96 = 6 frames drawn by one `Sprite2D`; frame 5 is the dark resting pad, frame 0 the bright active one, animated at 30 fps and turning around from wherever the frame is. `color` (blue/red/yellow/green) only swaps the art. `latch_mode`: `MOMENTARY` (active while pressed; the player *starting* to crouch while standing on it **cycles** it — off then on again within the same physics frame, so targets see a falling and a rising edge, the pad replays its press animation and both sounds play) `TOGGLE` (flips on the rising edge of "pressed", and also when the player starts crouching while standing on it) or `TIMED` (active for `duration` seconds after a press, `duration` shown in the Inspector only in this mode; a press is the step-on edge or the player starting a crouch on it; pressing again while active restarts the countdown with no `powered_changed`, replaying the press sound and sinking the pad again; standing on it doesn't extend it, and once it runs out a player still standing there needs to step off and on, or crouch, to press it again; the pad's frame sweeps from 0 (bright, sunk) to 5 (dark, risen) across the duration, driven from the remaining fraction through the same 30 fps travel cap as the other modes, so it sinks quickly on a press and then rises slowly, and the pedestal collider follows the frame; a glow strip on the pad pulses at 1.5 Hz and at 5 Hz over the last second, fading out over the last half second). The crouch rules are one action per crouch, and arriving already crouched doesn't count; read through the player's `is_crouching`/`is_on_floor()`, so `player.gd` needed no change. Pressed by the player and any `MeltableBlock`, not enemies or liquid; the sensor strip is polled every physics frame like Sawwheel/Crusher rather than tracking `body_entered`/`exited`, so a block that melts on the pad can't leave it stuck. A child `StaticBody2D` pedestal (layer 2, joins `"water_solid"`, deliberately not `"ground"`/`"catwalk"` so it can't be ledge-grabbed) has a collider whose top follows the middle of the art's oval per frame (`SURFACE_TOP_PX`), resized on a per-instance shape. Sounds `interactive/button_press`/`interactive/button_release` follow `active`.
- **Speed trap** (`res://scenes/interactive/speed_trap.gd` + `speed_trap.tscn`, `@tool`): a speed camera as a power **source** in the same contract as `Button` (`Area2D`, group `"power_source"`, `is_powered()`, `powered_changed(on)`, so a `PowerTarget` picks it in `sources` and a conductor wire attaches to it by adjacency). The node origin is the centre of a 16×16 tile (the cell a conductor files it under); the sensor strip is the vertical line at the origin's x, from that tile's bottom edge (the floor) up `strip_height` px (default 32), with a camera on a post beside it (`post_side`, left or right) and a three-digit screen. The `Area2D` has no layers and monitoring off; its `CollisionShape2D` (resized per instance from `strip_height`) only shows the strip in the editor, because a 600 px/s surf moves 10 px a frame and would step over a thin overlap. **Crossing**: each physics frame it takes the sign of the player's x relative to the line; a change of sign between two frames, with the player's collider overlapping the strip's height and a step of at most `MAX_CROSS_STEP` (48 px, so a teleport or respawn isn't a crossing), is a read, in either direction; standing in the strip does nothing, and a frame exactly on the line waits for the next. **Read**: `require_surfing` (default on) reads a surfing player only, through `get_surf_speed()` (the board's ground speed, or the horizontal speed it carries in the air), and ignores any other crossing entirely (no flash, no reading), so a pad launch or a swing release can't trigger it; with it off the reading is the player's `|velocity.x|`. A dead or dancing player is ignored. The reading is `floor(speed * display_scale)` (default 0.1, clamped to 999) and the pass test is `speed >= threshold_speed` (default 450): pick a threshold whose product with `display_scale` is whole, so a passing speed never displays below the limit. A pass powers the trap for `hold_time` seconds (default 5; 0 = latched until the level is reset), restarts the countdown if already powered (no new edge), and shows the reading in green; a failed pass shows its reading in red and changes nothing else. The flash is a brief expanding ring and glow at the lens (`FLASH_TIME` 0.18 s), not a full-screen effect. Placeholder art is drawn in code (seven-segment digits with faint unlit segments, dashes before any reading, a green screen edge and a one-pixel drain bar while powered with a timed hold, a status LED; in the editor the screen previews the threshold); the node must not be rotated. Saveable: `powered`, `time_left`, `reading`, `passed`; `load_state()` snaps silently, resets the crossing memory and emits `powered_changed` only if `powered` changed. Sounds `interactive/speed_trap_flash` (every read), `interactive/speed_trap_pass` and `interactive/speed_trap_fail` are in `Audio.PLANNED`. Place it before the Player in the tree so the player draws in front.
- **Laser receiver** (`res://scenes/interactive/laser_receiver.gd` + `laser_receiver.tscn`, `@tool`): a power **source** in the same contract as `Button` (group `"power_source"`, `is_powered()`, `powered_changed(on)`), lit by a laser beam. A `StaticBody2D` on layer 2, mask 0 (16×16, origin at the tile centre like `Button`), group `"water_solid"` and not `"ground"`/`"catwalk"`; not kickable (layer 1 is not set). `LaserTurret` calls its `on_laser_hit(point, direction)` every physics frame the beam ends on it; the receiver only stamps `Engine.get_physics_frames()` and counts as lit when the last hit is within `LIT_FRAMES` (2) frames (the `MeltableBlock` rule, so turret order doesn't matter). It powers while lit and stays powered `hold_time` seconds after the beam leaves (default 0.3, 0 = exactly as long as lit), which bridges a flickering beam or one swept across it by a turning mirror. It is opaque like any wall, so anything solid in front shields it. AND of two receivers: wire both to one `PowerTarget` with `all_required`. Saveable: `powered` and `time_left`; `load_state()` snaps silently and emits `powered_changed` only on a change, then the live beam takes over. (The state is derived from the beam but is still saved: a fresh receiver would otherwise rise on the first physics frame after a restore, and a rising edge turns a wired mirror.) Placeholder art is drawn in code (a housing with a lens that glows while powered); sounds `interactive/laser_receiver_on` and `interactive/laser_receiver_off` are in `Audio.PLANNED` (not played within `QUIET_FRAMES` of load). Place it before the Player in the tree so the player draws in front.
- **Arc receiver** (`res://scenes/interactive/arc_receiver.gd` + `arc_receiver.tscn`, `@tool`): the bullet target's cousin for arcs, a power **source** in the same contract as `Button` (group `"power_source"`, `is_powered()`, `powered_changed(on)`), struck by arcs. A `StaticBody2D` on layer 2, mask 0 (16×16, origin at the tile centre like `Button`), group `"water_solid"` and not `"ground"`/`"catwalk"`; solid like a wall, not kickable. An `Arc` that ends on it calls `on_arc_hit(point, direction)` once (a beam is continuous; an arc is one event), and each hit acts at once. `latch_mode` (`MOMENTARY`, `TOGGLE`, `TIMED`, as on the bullet target; `pulse_length` shows only in `MOMENTARY`, `duration` only in `TIMED`): `MOMENTARY` powers for `pulse_length` (default 2.5 s, a little over an `ArcTurret`'s default 2 s `fire_interval`, so a turret aimed straight at it holds it on) after each hit, `TIMED` for `duration` (default 5 s), and a hit while powered restarts the countdown with no new edge; `TOGGLE` flips on every hit. Only arcs light it (a laser, bullet or spark does nothing), and an arc that crosses a catwalk or glass first still counts. An AND of two arc receivers needs `TIMED` or a long pulse, since two arcs rarely arrive together. Saveable per mode: `TOGGLE` saves `powered`; `MOMENTARY` and `TIMED` save `powered` and `time_left` (unlike the bullet target's momentary mode, because the pulse is seconds long and arcs in flight are not saved, so a restore mid-pulse would drop the power at once); `load_state()` snaps silently and emits `powered_changed` only on a change, then the countdown runs out. Placeholder art is drawn in code (a housing with a coil ring and a lens that glows while powered, a shrinking arc while a countdown runs, a flash ring per hit); sounds `interactive/arc_receiver_on` and `interactive/arc_receiver_off` (on a change of power, not within `QUIET_FRAMES` of load) are in `Audio.PLANNED`. Place it before the Player in the tree so the player draws in front.
- **Bullet target** (`res://scenes/interactive/bullet_target.gd` + `bullet_target.tscn`, `@tool`): a power **source** in the same contract as `Button` (group `"power_source"`, `is_powered()`, `powered_changed(on)`), struck by cannon bullets. A `StaticBody2D` on layer 2, mask 0 (16×16, origin at the tile centre like `Button`), group `"water_solid"` and not `"ground"`/`"catwalk"`; solid like a wall, not kickable. It implements `on_bullet_hit(point, direction) -> bool` (always true: the bullet is consumed and explodes on it). `latch_mode` (`MOMENTARY`, `TOGGLE`, `TIMED`, as on `Button`; `pulse_length` shows only in `MOMENTARY`, `duration` only in `TIMED`): `MOMENTARY` powers for `pulse_length` (default 0.3 s) after each hit, `TIMED` for `duration` (default 5 s), and a hit while powered restarts the countdown with no new edge, so a cannon firing faster than the pulse length holds it on; `TOGGLE` flips on every hit. Saveable per mode: `TOGGLE` saves `powered`, `TIMED` saves `powered` and `time_left`, `MOMENTARY` saves nothing (a restore mid-pulse comes back off); `load_state()` snaps silently and emits `powered_changed` only on a change. Placeholder art is drawn in code (rings, a lit centre while powered, a flash ring per hit, a shrinking arc while a pulse runs); sound `interactive/bullet_target_hit` (every hit) is in `Audio.PLANNED`. Place it before the Player in the tree so the player draws in front.
- **Weight plate** (`res://scenes/interactive/weight_plate.gd` + `weight_plate.tscn`, `@tool`, `Area2D`): a floor plate that is a power **source** in the same contract as `Button` (group `"power_source"`, `is_powered()`, `powered_changed(on)`), powered while the summed weight resting on it reaches `threshold` (default 2, the player alone). Weight is in the see-saw's units: a `MeltableBlock`'s engine `mass` (metal 4, ice 1 per 16px tile) and `player_weight` (default 2, an export matching `RotatingBody.player_weight`); enemies and liquid weigh nothing. Same layout as `Button`: an `Area2D` (layer 0, mask 1) with a sensor strip straddling the top of the pedestal, polled every physics frame, and a child `Solid` `StaticBody2D` (layer 2, `"water_solid"`, not `"ground"`/`"catwalk"`) that sinks 2px while powered. Each body overlapping the strip counts once; the player counts only while `is_on_floor()`; blocks and the player resting on a block that is on the plate count too (up to `MAX_STACK_DEPTH` 3, a band above each of its shapes like the see-saw's load scan, and a candidate must be centred above the block under it, so a block beside it doesn't count). Rises the frame the reading reaches `threshold`; falls once it has stayed below it for `hold_time` (default 0.15 s), the shared rule for an analogue source, which also absorbs a block bobbing on the plate. A melting ice block lowers the reading, so a plate can release by itself (a timer). `show_weight` (runtime only) draws the live `weight/threshold` above it for tuning. Saveable: `powered` and `time_left`; `load_state()` snaps silently, emits `powered_changed` only on a change, and keeps power for `RESTORE_GRACE_FRAMES` (3) physics frames even if the sensor reads empty (a restored block only overlaps the strip once the physics server has stepped, as in `Button`). Placeholder art is drawn in code (a slab, green while powered, with a fill bar for weight against threshold); sounds `interactive/weight_plate_press` and `interactive/weight_plate_release` are in `Audio.PLANNED`. Origin at the tile centre like `Button`. Not handled: a block merely falling through the strip counts for the frames it overlaps. Place it before the Player in the tree so the player draws in front.
- **Angle sensor** (`res://scenes/interactive/angle_sensor.gd` + `angle_sensor.tscn`, `@tool`, `Node2D`): a power **source** in the same contract as `Button` (group `"power_source"`, `is_powered()`, `powered_changed(on)`) that reads a `RotatingBody`'s tilt, so a see-saw tipped to one end powers a door and a swing pumped past an angle gives pumping a goal. Not physical (no collision, so it can sit anywhere and is wired like any source); `body` is a `NodePath` to the `RotatingBody` (a warning appears in the editor if it is unset, not a `RotatingBody`, or if `threshold_degrees` is beyond the body's limit on that side). It reads `RotatingBody.get_angle()` in degrees, positive = clockwise on screen; `side` (`CLOCKWISE`, `COUNTER_CLOCKWISE`) picks the direction that counts, so `threshold_degrees` (default 15) is always positive. Powers once the tilt toward `side` reaches the threshold and releases only below `threshold_degrees - hysteresis_degrees` (default 3, clamped to the threshold), the shared rule for an analogue source; it is what keeps a sensor on a body its own power drives from chattering at the threshold. With `free_rotation` the angle wraps to ±180 and the sensor is powered through that sector once per turn, so use a limited body. Derived from the body (which restores its own angle) and read directly with no physics overlap, so it needs no restore grace; it still saves `powered` per the shared rule, `load_state()` snaps silently and emits `powered_changed` only on a change, and an angle inside the hysteresis band keeps the restored state. Placeholder art is drawn in code (a dial with a needle following the angle, a tick at the threshold and a lamp lit while powered); sounds `interactive/angle_sensor_on` and `interactive/angle_sensor_off` are in `Audio.PLANNED` (not played within `QUIET_FRAMES` of load). Origin at the tile centre like `Button`.
- **Basin sensor** (`res://scenes/interactive/basin_sensor.gd` + `basin_sensor.tscn`, `@tool`, `Node2D`): a power **source** in the same contract as `Button` (group `"power_source"`, `is_powered()`, `powered_changed(on)`) that reads a `WaterBasin`, so a faucet filling a basin can open a door and a drain emptying it can close one. Not physical (no collision, wired like any source); `basin` is a `NodePath` to the `WaterBasin` (a warning appears in the editor if it is unset, has no interior, or `column` is beyond its interior columns). `mode`: `FILL` powers at or above `fill_fraction` (default 0.5) of `WaterBasin.get_fill_fraction()` (settled liquid plus crust over the capacity at the spill point) and releases below `fill_fraction - fill_hysteresis` (default 0.05); `CRUST` powers while `has_crust` (a crust never thins, so it ends only with a reload or a restore from before it formed); `COLUMN` powers while still liquid at least `column_depth_tiles` (default 0.25) deep lies over one interior column (`column`, 0 = just inside the left wall; crust and a void read as no liquid) and releases below `column_depth_tiles - depth_hysteresis_tiles` (default 0.15). The Inspector shows only the active mode's fields. `liquid_filter` (`ANY`, `WATER`, `SLAG`) adds a condition to every mode: the basin's **runtime** `liquid_type` must match (an empty basin is claimed by whichever liquid arrives first; a basin crusted over slag still reads slag); the sensor never reads the authored export. Rises the physics frame the reading is met; falls once it has stayed unmet for `hold_time` (default 0.15 s), the shared rule for an analogue source (hysteresis plus hold), so a rippling surface or arriving particles can't chatter it, and a sensor whose power opens a drain on its own basin swings once between its two thresholds instead of flickering at one level. It reads the basin through `get_fill_fraction()`, `has_crust`, `liquid_type` and two read-only basin accessors, `get_interior_column_count()` and `get_liquid_depth_at_interior_column(index)`, and writes nothing on the basin. Derived from the basin (which restores its own state) and read directly, so a restore needs no grace period; it saves `powered` and `time_left` per the shared rule, `load_state()` snaps silently and emits `powered_changed` only on a change, and a reading inside the hysteresis band keeps the restored state. A basin authored pre-filled past the threshold powers on the first physics frame, which the `PowerTarget` startup apply absorbs. Placeholder art is drawn in code (a level tube coloured by the liquid, a tick at the threshold, a lamp lit while powered); sounds `interactive/basin_sensor_on` and `interactive/basin_sensor_off` are in `Audio.PLANNED` (not played within `QUIET_FRAMES` of load). Origin at the tile centre like `Button`.
- **Conductors** (`scenes/levels/conductor_layer.gd`, `class_name ConductorLayer`, a `@tool` `TileMapLayer`): purely visual, no physics. `scenes/levels/conductor_layers.tscn` is a `Node2D` holding four pre-wired layers named Red, Yellow, Green and Blue (one per colour, so differently coloured lines can cross in one cell) on `res://resources/conductor_tileset.tres`. Instance it once per level into `Tilemaps` right after `Ladders`, at the same position as `Ground` (`(-16, 0)` in `test_level`) so cells line up; it draws above the ground tiles and below actors later in the tree. Atlas `res://sprites/conductors/conductors_atlas.png`, 15 columns × 8 rows of 16px: rows 0–3 lit (red, yellow, green, blue), rows 4–7 dim; column = piece: 4 corner W–S, 5 straight N–S, 6 corner E–S, 7 corner N–W, 8 straight E–W, 9 corner N–E, 10 T (N,S,W), 11 T (E,S,W), 12 T (N,S,E), 13 T (N,E,W), 14 four-way. Columns 0–3 (the old terminal stubs) are unused: `WIRE_MASK` gives them mask 0, so a painted one is ignored with a warning. Colour is art only; a level may hold any number of circuits, including several of one colour. No TileSet custom data: the `WIRE_MASK` table (column → open edges) decodes the pieces.
  - **Connections**: two neighbouring cells in one layer connect only if both have an open edge facing each other. Layers never connect to each other, so colours cross freely; a same-layer four-way is a junction. Circuits are found by per-layer flood fill and built **once at load** — tiles painted at runtime don't rewire.
  - **Circuits**: each is a `Node` that emits `powered_changed(on)` and answers `is_powered()`, so it plugs into `PowerTarget` exactly as a button does. It is lit while any attached button is active; a button already active at load lights its circuit immediately, and targets are attached only after every circuit has read its buttons, so a target's first apply already sees the true state.
  - **Terminals are loose ends**: any open edge that doesn't meet a matching open edge in the neighbouring cell is a wire end, and a device in the cell beyond it is attached — no dedicated terminal tile, so any piece (straight, corner, T, four-way) can end a wire next to its device. A button (group `"power_source"`) there becomes a source of the circuit; a `PowerTarget` (group `"power_target"`) there gets the circuit added as one of its sources, so `invert` and `all_required` still work. A device reached from several loose ends attaches to each circuit (a button lights all of them; a target has all as sources). A wire passing beside a device with no open edge facing it attaches nothing. A device counts as being in the cell its `PowerTarget` node (for a target) or its own origin (a button, which sits at tile centre) is in: a multi-tile device gets its wire cell by moving its `PowerTarget` child.
  - **Lit/dim**: all cells of a circuit swap between rows `y` and `y+4`, keeping colour and column; every cell is normalised to one state at load.
  - **Editor overlay**: decodes the painted tiles through the same table — a line from each cell centre to its open edges, coloured per circuit, with a label per circuit (`#n x src y tgt`), and a dot on every loose end's open edge (green if a device is attached, red if not, so a wire stopping short of its device or butting against a piece that doesn't open back shows red). Ignored legacy terminal tiles get an orange cross. If the lines don't follow the drawn wire, `WIRE_MASK` is wrong for the art.
- **Door** (`res://scenes/interactive/door.gd` + `door.tscn`): `AnimatableBody2D`, layer 2, mask 0, joins `"ground"` (ledge-hangable) and `"water_solid"`. Scene: `Sprite2D` (`sprites/interactive/door1.png`, 16×144 = 9 frames, `vframes` 9), `CollisionShape2D`, and a `PowerTarget` child whose `sources` are picked per instance (or left empty for a conductor circuit to attach by adjacency). `invert` is exported on the door itself, so an inverted door starts open. `door.gd` implements `set_powered(on)` (powered = open) and `set_powered_initial(on)` (snaps to the startup state with no animation); a door with nothing wired to it stays closed. Node origin is the centre of its 16×16 tile (the cell `ConductorLayer` files it under); rotate the instance in 90° steps for a horizontal door. Frame 0 is fully closed (an 8px-wide post filling the tile, bottom-anchored), frame 8 fully open; the top edge drops 2px per frame and frames 7 and 8 both keep a 2px foot, so visible height is `max(2, 16 - 2·frame)`. The per-instance `RectangleShape2D` is resized on every frame change so collision matches the art, except that the collider is **disabled whenever the visible height is down to the 2px foot**: an open door is recessed into the floor and the foot is decoration only (a solid 2px lip stops the player dead, since there is no step-up); `open_fps` (16) sets the pace. **Closing never crushes**: before each step that would grow the collider above the foot, `intersect_shape` over the new size (mask layer 1, inset 0.5px per side) looks for a player, enemy or block; if one is in the way the door holds at its current frame and retries every physics frame. Opening shrinks the collider, so it can't be blocked; a player hanging from the door (`hang_platform == self`) is released as it starts to open, since the hang is pinned in world space (same as `drop_platform.gd`). A player standing on a half-open door blocks it from closing further. Sounds `interactive/door_open`/`interactive/door_close` play when the door actually starts moving that way (not while a blocked close waits). Verified in-editor: opening/closing under buttons and circuits, `invert`, both orientations, ledge grab (including release of a hanging player when it opens), the blocked-close wait/retry onto a player and onto a block, water resting on it, laser blocking.
- **Camera cue** (`res://scenes/interactive/camera_cue.gd` + `camera_cue.tscn`, `class_name CameraCue`, `@tool`, `Node2D`): a powered device that shows the player something off-screen: a rising edge pans the camera to it, holds, and eases back. The node origin is the point to look at; it has no collision and no art at run time. The scene carries a `PowerTarget` child (move it to the tile a conductor wire should end at); `sources` and `invert` are exported on the root, so an instance is wired in its own Inspector without Editable Children. `sources` is forwarded to the child (as a copy, so a conductor's `add_source()` never writes the export) when the node enters the tree, which is before the child's deferred first recompute, and whenever the list is edited; left empty it leaves the child's own `sources` alone, and `all_required` stays on the child. **Edges**: only a rising edge of the final power state plays it; a falling edge does nothing; the startup state goes through `set_powered_initial()`, which only records it, so a level that loads with the source already on, and a checkpoint restore, never cue. Exports: `pan_time` (0.6 s), `hold_time` (1.5), `return_time` (0.6), `delay` (seconds from the edge to the start of the pan, so a door's opening can be lined up with the camera arriving; 0), `once` (play only the first time), `cooldown` (ignore edges for this long after a cue ends, whether it finished, was replaced or was cut short by a hit; 0), `ease_curve` (`LINEAR`, `SINE` default, `CUBIC`; used for the pan and the return) and `show_view_rect`. `play()` is public and does what an edge does (it goes through `delay`, `once` and `cooldown`). An edge while a `delay` is already pending is ignored; an edge while the cue is playing restarts it from the camera's current offset, and a second cue shown over a first replaces it the same way (cues are never queued). `delay` and `cooldown` are plain countdowns in `_physics_process()`, so pausing freezes them (there is no `create_timer()`). The camera work belongs to the player (`show_cue()`, see Camera in `player.md`); the cue passes itself as the target, so its `global_position` is read every frame and a cue parented to a moving platform tracks it, and it listens to the player's `cue_ended(source)` to start its cooldown. **No input is locked**: the world keeps running, so the player can move and be hit while the camera is elsewhere; a damaging hit or the win dance ends the cue early (the camera eases back), a jump pad launch or a stomp bounce does not. Keep holds short and trigger cues from calm moments (a button press, not a speed trap); a lock would need an input wrapper in `player.gd` first and is not built. Nothing plays if the player is dead or dancing. Saveable: `fired` only (what `once` needs, so a restore from a checkpoint after the cue does not replay it); the cue itself is runtime only, and `load_state()` is silent and clears a pending delay and any cooldown. Authoring: `show_view_rect` draws the 512x288 view rectangle centred on the cue point while `authoring_overlay` is set (a plain variable the editor sets in `_ready()`, not an `Engine.is_editor_hint()` test, so a runtime level editor can set it too); `get_warnings()` is a plain method that `_get_configuration_warnings()` calls (no `PowerTarget` child; no sources on the cue or on its `PowerTarget`, which is fine only when a conductor wire ends at it). Sound `interactive/camera_cue_pan` (when the pan starts) is in `Audio.PLANNED`. Place it anywhere in the level.

Reads with: `lasers-arcs-mirrors.md` (laser and arc turrets, the beams that light the receivers), `rotating-bodies.md` (the see-saw motor, the angle sensor), `liquid-system.md` (the faucet, the basin sensor), `platforms-conveyors.md` (moving platform, conveyor and jump pad are powered), `hazards-traps.md` (cannon, crusher and sawwheel are powered), `enemies.md` (the rail bot is a powered device), `saving-levels-ui.md` (the finish, saved and restored power state).
