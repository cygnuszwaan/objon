# Power: sources, devices and conductors

The power contract (`PowerTarget`), the powered devices, every power source (button, speed trap, laser and arc receivers, bullet target, weight plate, angle sensor, basin sensor, zone sensor, the logic nodes), the conductor layers and the door. Read `objon.md` (the core: setup, layers and groups, contracts, invariants, docs index) first.

Covers: Powered devices; Power contract; Button; Speed trap; Laser receiver; Freeze receiver; Arc receiver; Bullet target; Weight plate; Angle sensor; Basin sensor; Zone sensor; Logic nodes (Delay; Latch; Counter; Pulse); Conductors (Connections; Circuits; Terminals are loose ends; Lit/dim; Editor overlay); Door; Camera cue; Camera cue stop

- **Powered devices** (each scene has a `PowerTarget` child and an `invert` export on its root; unwired they behave as authored): **laser turret** powered = beam on (`set_enabled`); **arc turret** powered = firing (a rising edge fires one arc at once, see Arc turret); **conveyor** powered = running (`set_enabled`; move the `PowerTarget` along the belt to choose where a wire attaches); **faucet** powered = open (`is_open`; `PowerTarget` at the tile centre); **moving platform** powered = running, unpowered = the tween is paused where it is; **cannon** powered = firing (its `Timer` stops/starts; switching on fires one bullet immediately, via a deferred call, then the `Timer` runs, so the next shot is a full `fire_rate` later; a cannon already powered or unwired at load keeps its ordinary first shot after `fire_rate`); **crusher** powered = cycling, unpowered = it finishes its current leg and parks fully retracted at the start of the retracted dwell (never frozen mid-stroke), resuming from there (a power cycle, off then on, also clears a metal jam: see Sawwheel / Crusher hazards); **sawwheel** powered = spinning and dangerous, unpowered = blade paused, hum stopped, no damage; **jump pad** powered = live, unpowered = inert and dimmed (`modulate`; a pad that becomes live under a player launches on the next entry); **mirror** by control mode (see Mirror in `lasers-arcs-mirrors.md`): in step mode each rising edge of the final power state turns it one step (`rotate_step(1)`), falling edges do nothing, the startup state is not an edge, and an edge during a turn is ignored like a kick; in toggle mode powered = aim 1 and unpowered = aim 0 on both edges, a change during a turn turns it back, and the startup state snaps to its aim (set `kick_rotates` false on a switch-owned mirror); **see-saw** powered = motorized, unpowered = free (see Rotating bodies); **door** as below; **camera cue** a rising edge pans the camera to it (see Camera cue); **powered spike layer** powered = spikes out (dangerous), unpowered = hidden and harmless, unwired = out (see Powered spike layer in `hazards-traps.md`). **powered catwalk layer** powered = bridge present (one-way, hangable, in group `"catwalk"`), unpowered = gone after a `warn_time` blink, unwired = present (see Powered catwalk layer in `hazards-traps.md`); **powered ladder layer** the same for ladder tiles (see Powered ladder layer). **block dispenser** powered = a rising edge spawns one ice or metal block at its spawn point, a falling edge does nothing, the startup state is not an edge, and an unwired dispenser never spawns (see Block dispenser in `meltable-blocks.md`). All user-verified in-editor.
- **Power contract** (`res://scenes/interactive/power_target.gd`, `class_name PowerTarget`, `@tool` `Node2D`): duck-typed like `take_hit()`/`get_conveyor_speed()`. A **source** emits `powered_changed(on)` and answers `is_powered()` (`Button` and conductor circuits); a **device** implements `set_powered(on: bool)` and optionally `set_powered_initial(on)`. `PowerTarget` sits between them as a child of the device (`debug_power_target.gd` extends it as a disposable test device to delete once real devices exist): `@export var sources: Array[Node]` (typed node-picker), `invert`, `all_required` (default OR). **Invert** may also be exported on the parent device's root script (`@export var invert: bool`), which puts the toggle in the device's own Inspector; `PowerTarget` reads it from its parent (either one inverts, both set = inverted). `sources` and `all_required` stay on the `PowerTarget` child (Editable Children). **No valid sources = not driven**: the device keeps its authored state, so every device scene carries a `PowerTarget` child and behaves as before until something is wired to it. The first recompute is deferred until every `_ready()` (and the conductor layers' own deferred build) has run, so a device is never driven before it is ready; that first apply, if within `STARTUP_FRAMES` (3) of load, goes to the parent's `set_powered_initial(on)` if it has one (door snaps to its state; a step-mode mirror records it without counting an edge, a toggle-mode one snaps to its aim), else `set_powered(on)`. After that `_apply_power(on)` runs once per actual change (default: forwards to the parent's `set_powered()`; subclasses override it), then `power_changed(on)` is emitted. Joins group `"power_target"`; `add_source(node)` attaches a source at runtime (duplicates ignored). `evaluate() -> bool` is the read-only final state a recompute would apply right now (false while nothing is wired): a device that is itself a source uses it to answer `is_powered()` before its own first apply (the camera cue's pass-through). **A source that is not a wire object** (the logic nodes, the camera cue's pass-through) answers `is_powered()` and emits `powered_changed` but stays out of group `"power_source"`, so a `ConductorLayer` never attaches to it: a device picks it in its `sources`. `debug_toggle_key` (default `KEY_NONE`) flips the final state from the keyboard so any device can be triggered remotely while testing; it XORs with the computed state, and an unlinked device starts from unpowered. A `draw_links` editor overlay draws a line from the target to each source, orange when inverted. The cell a conductor wire attaches to is the cell the `PowerTarget` node itself sits in, so inside a multi-tile device the child is moved to the tile the wire should end at.
- **Button** (`res://scenes/interactive/button.gd`): `Area2D` power source, joins group `"power_source"`; node origin is the centre of its 16×16 tile. `sprites/button1_<colour>.png` (searched in `sprites/interactive/` then `sprites/`), 16×96 = 6 frames drawn by one `Sprite2D`; frame 5 is the dark resting pad, frame 0 the bright active one, animated at 30 fps and turning around from wherever the frame is. `color` (blue/red/yellow/green) only swaps the art. `latch_mode`: `MOMENTARY` (active while pressed; the player *starting* to crouch while standing on it **cycles** it — off then on again within the same physics frame, so targets see a falling and a rising edge, the pad replays its press animation and both sounds play) `TOGGLE` (flips on the rising edge of "pressed", and also when the player starts crouching while standing on it) or `TIMED` (active for `duration` seconds after a press, `duration` shown in the Inspector only in this mode; a press is the step-on edge or the player starting a crouch on it; pressing again while active restarts the countdown with no `powered_changed`, replaying the press sound and sinking the pad again; standing on it doesn't extend it, and once it runs out a player still standing there needs to step off and on, or crouch, to press it again; the pad's frame sweeps from 0 (bright, sunk) to 5 (dark, risen) across the duration, driven from the remaining fraction through the same 30 fps travel cap as the other modes, so it sinks quickly on a press and then rises slowly, and the pedestal collider follows the frame; a glow strip on the pad pulses at 1.5 Hz and at 5 Hz over the last second, fading out over the last half second). The crouch rules are one action per crouch, and arriving already crouched doesn't count; read through the player's `is_crouching`/`is_on_floor()`, so `player.gd` needed no change. Pressed by the player and any `MeltableBlock`, not enemies or liquid; the sensor strip is polled every physics frame like Sawwheel/Crusher rather than tracking `body_entered`/`exited`, so a block that melts on the pad can't leave it stuck. A child `StaticBody2D` pedestal (layer 2, joins `"water_solid"`, deliberately not `"ground"`/`"catwalk"` so it can't be ledge-grabbed) has a collider whose top follows the middle of the art's oval per frame (`SURFACE_TOP_PX`), resized on a per-instance shape. Sounds `interactive/button_press`/`interactive/button_release` follow `active`.
- **Speed trap** (`res://scenes/interactive/speed_trap.gd` + `speed_trap.tscn`, `@tool`): a speed camera as a power **source** in the same contract as `Button` (`Area2D`, group `"power_source"`, `is_powered()`, `powered_changed(on)`, so a `PowerTarget` picks it in `sources` and a conductor wire attaches to it by adjacency). The node origin is the centre of a 16×16 tile (the cell a conductor files it under); the sensor strip is the vertical line at the origin's x, from that tile's bottom edge (the floor) up `strip_height` px (default 32), with a camera on a post beside it (`post_side`, left or right) and a three-digit screen. The `Area2D` has no layers and monitoring off; its `CollisionShape2D` (resized per instance from `strip_height`) only shows the strip in the editor, because a 600 px/s surf moves 10 px a frame and would step over a thin overlap. **Crossing**: each physics frame it takes the sign of the player's x relative to the line; a change of sign between two frames, with the player's collider overlapping the strip's height and a step of at most `MAX_CROSS_STEP` (48 px, so a teleport or respawn isn't a crossing), is a read, in either direction; standing in the strip does nothing, and a frame exactly on the line waits for the next. **Read**: `require_surfing` (default on) reads a surfing player only, through `get_surf_speed()` (the board's ground speed, or the horizontal speed it carries in the air), and ignores any other crossing entirely (no flash, no reading), so a pad launch or a swing release can't trigger it; with it off the reading is the player's `|velocity.x|`. A dead or dancing player is ignored. The reading is `floor(speed * display_scale)` (default 0.1, clamped to 999) and the pass test is `speed >= threshold_speed` (default 450): pick a threshold whose product with `display_scale` is whole, so a passing speed never displays below the limit. A pass powers the trap for `hold_time` seconds (default 5; 0 = latched until the level is reset), restarts the countdown if already powered (no new edge), and shows the reading in green; a failed pass shows its reading in red and changes nothing else. The flash is a brief expanding ring and glow at the lens (`FLASH_TIME` 0.18 s), not a full-screen effect. Placeholder art is drawn in code (seven-segment digits with faint unlit segments, dashes before any reading, a green screen edge and a one-pixel drain bar while powered with a timed hold, a status LED; in the editor the screen previews the threshold); the node must not be rotated. Saveable: `powered`, `time_left`, `reading`, `passed`; `load_state()` snaps silently, resets the crossing memory and emits `powered_changed` only if `powered` changed. Sounds `interactive/speed_trap_flash` (every read), `interactive/speed_trap_pass` and `interactive/speed_trap_fail` are in `Audio.PLANNED`. Place it before the Player in the tree so the player draws in front.
- **Laser receiver** (`res://scenes/interactive/laser_receiver.gd` + `laser_receiver.tscn`, `@tool`): a power **source** in the same contract as `Button` (group `"power_source"`, `is_powered()`, `powered_changed(on)`), lit by a laser beam. A `StaticBody2D` on layer 2, mask 0 (16×16, origin at the tile centre like `Button`), group `"water_solid"` and not `"ground"`/`"catwalk"`; not kickable (layer 1 is not set). `LaserTurret` calls its `on_laser_hit(point, direction)` every physics frame the beam ends on it; the receiver only stamps `Engine.get_physics_frames()` and counts as lit when the last hit is within `LIT_FRAMES` (2) frames (the `MeltableBlock` rule, so turret order doesn't matter). It powers while lit and stays powered `hold_time` seconds after the beam leaves (default 0.3, 0 = exactly as long as lit), which bridges a flickering beam or one swept across it by a turning mirror. It is opaque like any wall, so anything solid in front shields it. AND of two receivers: wire both to one `PowerTarget` with `all_required`. Saveable: `powered` and `time_left`; `load_state()` snaps silently and emits `powered_changed` only on a change, then the live beam takes over. (The state is derived from the beam but is still saved: a fresh receiver would otherwise rise on the first physics frame after a restore, and a rising edge turns a wired mirror.) Placeholder art is drawn in code (a housing with a lens that glows while powered); sounds `interactive/laser_receiver_on` and `interactive/laser_receiver_off` are in `Audio.PLANNED` (not played within `QUIET_FRAMES` of load). Place it before the Player in the tree so the player draws in front. It has a `BeamTarget` child like the freeze receiver (a 16×16 Area2D by default, group `beam_target_laser`; `get_beam_target()`), so shrinking its shape makes the beam land on a smaller spot; the body is otherwise the solid it always was.
- **Freeze receiver** (`res://scenes/interactive/freeze_receiver.gd` + `freeze_receiver.tscn`, `@tool`; roadmap N3.2): the freeze ray's counterpart to the laser receiver, in the same contract (group `"power_source"`, `is_powered()`, `powered_changed(on)`; a `StaticBody2D` on layer 2, mask 0, 16×16, origin at the tile centre, group `"water_solid"`, not `"ground"`/`"catwalk"`, opaque, hit on any face; the body is the solid the player and everything else meets, while the freeze beam is stopped by a separate **`BeamTarget`** child, an `Area2D` on layer 4 (mask 0, monitoring off) with its own shape (8×8 at the centre by default), so the beam is drawn into the receiver and ends at the target; `get_beam_target()` returns it; a freeze beam that hits the body but misses the target stops on the body and does not light the receiver; the art `Sprite2D` has `z_index` -1 so the beam, drawn by the turret at 0, is in front of it). `FreezeTurret` calls its `on_freeze_hit(point, direction)` every physics frame the beam ends on it; like the laser receiver it only stamps the physics frame and counts as lit within `LIT_FRAMES` (2). Unlike the laser receiver the beam has to **stay on it**: while lit and not yet powered it builds frost, one second per second of beam, and powers once the frost reaches `dwell_time` (default 1 s; 0 = at once, as a laser receiver does). Frost decays at one second per second with no beam (`FROST_DECAY_PER_SEC`, the same rate as a basin's), so a beam swept across does nothing and one that leaves and returns keeps what is left. Once powered, a lit receiver holds full frost, stays powered while lit and for `hold_time` seconds after (default 0.3, 0 = exactly as long as lit). Holding a beam steady on it while mirrors turn is the puzzle. Saveable: `powered` and `time_left` (frost is derived: a restore comes back full if powered, else empty, and the live beam takes over; silent, `powered_changed` only on a change). Placeholder art is drawn in code (the lens brightens as the frost builds); sounds `interactive/freeze_receiver_on` and `interactive/freeze_receiver_off` are in `Audio.PLANNED` (not played within `QUIET_FRAMES` of load).
- **Arc receiver** (`res://scenes/interactive/arc_receiver.gd` + `arc_receiver.tscn`, `@tool`): the bullet target's cousin for arcs, a power **source** in the same contract as `Button` (group `"power_source"`, `is_powered()`, `powered_changed(on)`), struck by arcs. A `StaticBody2D` on layer 2, mask 0 (16×16, origin at the tile centre like `Button`), group `"water_solid"` and not `"ground"`/`"catwalk"`; solid like a wall, not kickable. An `Arc` that ends on it calls `on_arc_hit(point, direction)` once (a beam is continuous; an arc is one event), and each hit acts at once. `latch_mode` (`MOMENTARY`, `TOGGLE`, `TIMED`, as on the bullet target; `pulse_length` shows only in `MOMENTARY`, `duration` only in `TIMED`): `MOMENTARY` powers for `pulse_length` (default 2.5 s, a little over an `ArcTurret`'s default 2 s `fire_interval`, so a turret aimed straight at it holds it on) after each hit, `TIMED` for `duration` (default 5 s), and a hit while powered restarts the countdown with no new edge; `TOGGLE` flips on every hit. Only arcs light it (a laser, bullet or spark does nothing), and an arc that crosses a catwalk or glass first still counts. An AND of two arc receivers needs `TIMED` or a long pulse, since two arcs rarely arrive together. Saveable per mode: `TOGGLE` saves `powered`; `MOMENTARY` and `TIMED` save `powered` and `time_left` (unlike the bullet target's momentary mode, because the pulse is seconds long and arcs in flight are not saved, so a restore mid-pulse would drop the power at once); `load_state()` snaps silently and emits `powered_changed` only on a change, then the countdown runs out. Placeholder art is drawn in code (a housing with a coil ring and a lens that glows while powered, a shrinking arc while a countdown runs, a flash ring per hit); sounds `interactive/arc_receiver_on` and `interactive/arc_receiver_off` (on a change of power, not within `QUIET_FRAMES` of load) are in `Audio.PLANNED`. Place it before the Player in the tree so the player draws in front. It has a `BeamTarget` child like the freeze receiver (a 16×16 Area2D by default, group `beam_target_arc`; `get_beam_target()`), so shrinking its shape makes the beam land on a smaller spot; the body is otherwise the solid it always was.
- **Bullet target** (`res://scenes/interactive/bullet_target.gd` + `bullet_target.tscn`, `@tool`): a power **source** in the same contract as `Button` (group `"power_source"`, `is_powered()`, `powered_changed(on)`), struck by cannon bullets. A `StaticBody2D` on layer 2, mask 0 (16×16, origin at the tile centre like `Button`), group `"water_solid"` and not `"ground"`/`"catwalk"`; solid like a wall, not kickable. It implements `on_bullet_hit(point, direction) -> bool` (always true: the bullet is consumed and explodes on it). `latch_mode` (`MOMENTARY`, `TOGGLE`, `TIMED`, as on `Button`; `pulse_length` shows only in `MOMENTARY`, `duration` only in `TIMED`): `MOMENTARY` powers for `pulse_length` (default 0.3 s) after each hit, `TIMED` for `duration` (default 5 s), and a hit while powered restarts the countdown with no new edge, so a cannon firing faster than the pulse length holds it on; `TOGGLE` flips on every hit. Saveable per mode: `TOGGLE` saves `powered`, `TIMED` saves `powered` and `time_left`, `MOMENTARY` saves nothing (a restore mid-pulse comes back off); `load_state()` snaps silently and emits `powered_changed` only on a change. Placeholder art is drawn in code (rings, a lit centre while powered, a flash ring per hit, a shrinking arc while a pulse runs); sound `interactive/bullet_target_hit` (every hit) is in `Audio.PLANNED`. Place it before the Player in the tree so the player draws in front.
- **Weight plate** (`res://scenes/interactive/weight_plate.gd` + `weight_plate.tscn`, `@tool`, `Area2D`): a floor plate, a flush sensor ribbon with no collider, that is a power **source** in the same contract as `Button` (group `"power_source"`, `is_powered()`, `powered_changed(on)`), powered while the summed weight resting on it reaches `threshold` (default 2, the player alone). Weight is in the see-saw's units: a `MeltableBlock`'s engine `mass` (metal 4, ice 1 per 16px tile) `player_weight` (default 2, an export matching `RotatingBody.player_weight`) and an enemy's `get_load_weight()` (see Enemy weight in `enemies.md`: its `load_weight` while alive and on the floor, else 0) times the plate's `enemy_weight_scale` (default 1; 0 = this plate ignores enemies); liquid weighs nothing. **It is a ribbon, not a solid**: an `Area2D` (layer 0, mask 1) whose only shape is a 16×4 sensor strip centred on the **top edge** of its tile (2 px above it, 2 px into it), polled every physics frame, with no body under it, nothing to step up onto and no recess to catch on, and it does not sink. Place it in the cell that is the top row of the floor, over a ground tile (or whatever else the actors stand on there): that tile carries the weight and a body resting on it overlaps the ribbon. Each body overlapping the strip counts once; the player counts only while `is_on_floor()` and an enemy only through its `get_load_weight()` (so a dying one, one in the air and a rail bot count nothing); blocks, enemies and the player resting on a block or an enemy that is on the plate count too (up to `MAX_STACK_DEPTH` 3, a band above each of its shapes like the see-saw's load scan, and a candidate must be centred above the block under it, so a block beside it doesn't count). Rises the frame the reading reaches `threshold`; falls once it has stayed below it for `hold_time` (default 0.15 s), the shared rule for an analogue source, which also absorbs a block bobbing on the plate. A melting ice block lowers the reading, so a plate can release by itself (a timer). `show_weight` (runtime only) draws the live `weight/threshold` above it for tuning. Saveable: `powered` and `time_left`; `load_state()` snaps silently, emits `powered_changed` only on a change, and keeps power for `RESTORE_GRACE_FRAMES` (3) physics frames even if the sensor reads empty (a restored block only overlaps the strip once the physics server has stepped, as in `Button`). Placeholder art is drawn in code (a 3 px strip along the tile's top edge: dark, an amber bar filling left to right with the weight against the threshold, green while powered); sounds `interactive/weight_plate_press` and `interactive/weight_plate_release` are in `Audio.PLANNED`. Origin at the tile centre like `Button`. Not handled: a block merely falling through the ribbon counts for the frames it overlaps. Place it before the Player in the tree so the player draws in front.
- **Angle sensor** (`res://scenes/interactive/angle_sensor.gd` + `angle_sensor.tscn`, `@tool`, `Node2D`): a power **source** in the same contract as `Button` (group `"power_source"`, `is_powered()`, `powered_changed(on)`) that reads a `RotatingBody`'s tilt, so a see-saw tipped to one end powers a door and a swing pumped past an angle gives pumping a goal. Not physical (no collision, so it can sit anywhere and is wired like any source); `body` is a `NodePath` to the `RotatingBody` (a warning appears in the editor if it is unset, not a `RotatingBody`, or if `threshold_degrees` is beyond the body's limit on that side). It reads `RotatingBody.get_angle()` in degrees, positive = clockwise on screen; `side` (`CLOCKWISE`, `COUNTER_CLOCKWISE`) picks the direction that counts, so `threshold_degrees` (default 15) is always positive. Powers once the tilt toward `side` reaches the threshold and releases only below `threshold_degrees - hysteresis_degrees` (default 3, clamped to the threshold), the shared rule for an analogue source; it is what keeps a sensor on a body its own power drives from chattering at the threshold. With `free_rotation` the angle wraps to ±180 and the sensor is powered through that sector once per turn, so use a limited body. Derived from the body (which restores its own angle) and read directly with no physics overlap, so it needs no restore grace; it still saves `powered` per the shared rule, `load_state()` snaps silently and emits `powered_changed` only on a change, and an angle inside the hysteresis band keeps the restored state. Placeholder art is drawn in code (a dial with a needle following the angle, a tick at the threshold and a lamp lit while powered); sounds `interactive/angle_sensor_on` and `interactive/angle_sensor_off` are in `Audio.PLANNED` (not played within `QUIET_FRAMES` of load). Origin at the tile centre like `Button`.
- **Basin sensor** (`res://scenes/interactive/basin_sensor.gd` + `basin_sensor.tscn`, `@tool`, `Node2D`): a power **source** in the same contract as `Button` (group `"power_source"`, `is_powered()`, `powered_changed(on)`) that reads a `WaterBasin`, so a faucet filling a basin can open a door and a drain emptying it can close one. Not physical (no collision, wired like any source); `basin` is a `NodePath` to the `WaterBasin` (a warning appears in the editor if it is unset, has no interior, or `column` is beyond its interior columns). `mode`: `FILL` powers at or above `fill_fraction` (default 0.5) of `WaterBasin.get_fill_fraction()` (settled liquid plus crust over the capacity at the spill point) and releases below `fill_fraction - fill_hysteresis` (default 0.05); `CRUST` powers while `has_crust` (a reaction crust never thins, so it ends only with a reload or a restore from before it formed; a frozen crust also ends when a laser thaws it); `COLUMN` powers while still liquid at least `column_depth_tiles` (default 0.25) deep lies over one interior column (`column`, 0 = just inside the left wall; crust and a void read as no liquid) and releases below `column_depth_tiles - depth_hysteresis_tiles` (default 0.15). The Inspector shows only the active mode's fields. `liquid_filter` (`ANY`, `WATER`, `SLAG`) adds a condition to every mode: the basin's **runtime** `liquid_type` must match (an empty basin is claimed by whichever liquid arrives first; a basin crusted over slag still reads slag); the sensor never reads the authored export. Rises the physics frame the reading is met; falls once it has stayed unmet for `hold_time` (default 0.15 s), the shared rule for an analogue source (hysteresis plus hold), so a rippling surface or arriving particles can't chatter it, and a sensor whose power opens a drain on its own basin swings once between its two thresholds instead of flickering at one level. It reads the basin through `get_fill_fraction()`, `has_crust`, `liquid_type` and two read-only basin accessors, `get_interior_column_count()` and `get_liquid_depth_at_interior_column(index)`, and writes nothing on the basin. Derived from the basin (which restores its own state) and read directly, so a restore needs no grace period; it saves `powered` and `time_left` per the shared rule, `load_state()` snaps silently and emits `powered_changed` only on a change, and a reading inside the hysteresis band keeps the restored state. A basin authored pre-filled past the threshold powers on the first physics frame, which the `PowerTarget` startup apply absorbs. Placeholder art is drawn in code (a level tube coloured by the liquid, a tick at the threshold, a lamp lit while powered); sounds `interactive/basin_sensor_on` and `interactive/basin_sensor_off` are in `Audio.PLANNED` (not played within `QUIET_FRAMES` of load). Origin at the tile centre like `Button`.
- **Zone sensor** (`res://scenes/interactive/zone_sensor.gd` + `zone_sensor.tscn`, `@tool`, `Area2D`): a power **source** in the same contract as `Button` (group `"power_source"`, `is_powered()`, `powered_changed(on)`) that is powered while enough bodies are inside a rectangle, for a door that shuts behind the player, an ambush trigger, or a lure puzzle where an enemy has to stand in the zone. Not physical (layer 0, mask 1, it blocks nothing, so it is wired like any source) and **invisible and silent in play by default**. `size` (px, default 48×48) is the zone's width and height, centred on the node origin, on a per-instance `RectangleShape2D` (the scene's shape is replaced in `_ready()`, so instances never share one); rotate and scale stay at their defaults, since the zone is axis-aligned (a configuration warning says so). The cell a conductor files it under is the cell the origin is in, so a large zone is wired by `sources`, or placed so its origin cell is where a wire ends. **What counts**: `count_player` (default on), `count_enemies` and `count_blocks` (both default off). The player is the group-`"player"` body, never while `is_dead` or `is_dancing`; an enemy is any body in group `"enemy"` (which bodies are in it is in `enemies.md`: a dead one leaves the group at once, or is already queued for deletion); a block is any `MeltableBlock`. Every body overlapping the area counts once, in any state (a player on a ladder, hanging or swimming counts, unlike the weight plate's on-the-floor rule), the count is the sum across the enabled kinds, and the sensor powers at `min_count` (default 1) or more. "Nobody in the zone" is `invert` on the `PowerTarget`. **Power**: the area is polled every physics frame through `get_overlapping_bodies()` (never `body_entered`/`exited`, so a body that melts, dies or is freed inside cannot leave it stuck); it rises the physics frame the count reaches `min_count` and falls once the count has stayed below it for `hold_time` seconds (default 0.15, 0 = the same frame), the shared rule for an analogue source, so a body crossing the edge can't chatter it. A body is seen from the physics step after it appears, as with any `Area2D`. Saveable: `powered` and `time_left`; `load_state()` snaps silently, emits `powered_changed` only on a change, and keeps power for `RESTORE_GRACE_FRAMES` (3) physics frames even if the zone reads empty (a restored body only overlaps once the physics server has stepped, as in `Button` and the weight plate). `show_zone` draws the zone and the live `count/min_count` at run time (the outline is always drawn in the editor, through the plain variable `authoring_overlay` that the editor sets in `_ready()`, not an `Engine.is_editor_hint()` test); `silent` (default on) suppresses the sounds, and with it off `interactive/zone_sensor_on` and `interactive/zone_sensor_off` play on a change of `powered`, never within `QUIET_FRAMES` (3) of load; both ids are in `Audio.PLANNED`. `get_warnings()` is a plain method that `_get_configuration_warnings()` calls (no kind counted; rotation or scale set).
- **Logic nodes** (`res://scenes/interactive/logic_node.gd`, `class_name LogicNode`, the base; `logic_delay.gd` `LogicDelay`, `logic_latch.gd` `LogicLatch`, `logic_counter.gd` `LogicCounter`, `logic_pulse.gd` `LogicPulse`, each with a one-node `.tscn`; all `@tool` `Node2D`): small power **sources** that read other sources and give back a state of their own, for the things a bare `PowerTarget` (OR, AND, `invert`) cannot do: wait, remember, count, make a pulse. A logic node is **not a wire object**: it is not a `PowerTarget` and not in group `"power_source"`, so a conductor never attaches to it. Its inputs are the exported `sources` list (a typed node-picker; any node with `is_powered()`: buttons, receivers, plates, sensors, a camera cue's pass-through, other logic nodes) and its output is picked as a source by the device it should drive (`is_powered()`, `powered_changed(on)`), like a Button. A conductor circuit is generated at run time and cannot be picked, so to bring a wire into a logic node put a source (a button, a sensor) on that wire. No collision, art or sound at run time (the devices it drives make the noise; the editor draws a tag and a line to each source, orange for a reset list; `draw_links`, `authoring_overlay`). **Input** (shared): `sources` combine like a `PowerTarget`'s (any one lights it; `all_required` needs every one), then `invert_input` flips the level; no valid source reads as off, never as inverted-on. **Startup**: the first state is derived from the inputs at once and is never an edge (nothing counts, pulses or waits); `is_powered()` derives it on demand, so a device whose own startup apply runs first still reads the right level and snaps through `set_powered_initial()`, and the deferred call from `_ready()` re-derives it from sources that were not ready yet. **Loops**: logic nodes may read each other; a loop that cannot settle (A inverts B, B follows A) is cut by a shared depth guard (`MAX_DEPTH` 16) with one warning, and `get_warnings()` names a node that can reach itself. **Save**: `"saveable"`; `save_state()` is the output, the last input level and what the subclass adds, `load_state()` snaps silently, emits `powered_changed` only on a change, and re-reads the inputs once (deferred) without counting that as an edge, so a source restored after the node cannot fake one. Timers are plain countdowns in `_physics_process()`, so pausing freezes them. A subclass never overrides `_ready()` or `_physics_process()` without calling `super()` (it overrides the virtuals instead). Add a source at run time by assigning `sources` (the setter reconnects); there is no `add_source()`.
  - **Delay** (`on_delay` 1 s, `off_delay` 0): the output follows the input late. A rise switches it on after `on_delay`, a fall off after `off_delay`, each only if the input has stayed there the whole time, so a flicker shorter than the delay never passes (an on-delay filters short presses, an off-delay stretches them). Zero is instant in that direction; both zero is a plain pass-through (a warning). Saves the pending target and the seconds left.
  - **Latch** (`reset_sources`, `start_on`): the output goes on while a `sources` input is on and stays on after it lets go, until a reset input switches it off. Both are **levels** and **reset dominates**: while any reset source is on the output is off and a set cannot take; when the reset lets go, a set that is still on latches again at once. `reset_sources` are any-one; `sources` use `all_required`/`invert_input`. Startup: `(start_on or set) and not reset`. Saves the reset level.
  - **Counter** (`count_to` 3, `reset_sources`, `auto_reset_time` 0): on after `count_to` rising edges. **Each source counts on its own** (a second source rising while the first is held is a new edge), so three buttons once each and one button three times both reach 3; `invert_input` counts falling edges; `all_required` does not apply and is hidden. Edges past `count_to` are ignored. While any reset source is on the count is held at 0, the output is off and edges are ignored (not saved up). `auto_reset_time` above 0 clears the count that many seconds after the output goes on (a timed unlock); 0 keeps it on until a reset. `count` is readable. Startup: count 0 and a level already on at load is not counted. Saves the count, each source's last level (in `sources` order), the reset level and the auto-reset time left.
  - **Pulse** (`duration` 1 s): a rising edge of the combined input switches the output on for `duration`, whatever the input does next. A new rising edge while on restarts the time with no new `powered_changed`; falling edges do nothing (`invert_input` makes the fall the trigger). Startup: off. Saves the time left.
  - Not built: a cap on how many edges a counter holds beyond `count_to`, a counting window (reset if no edge arrives within a time), a per-source `all_required` for the counter, `add_source()`.
- **Conductors** (`scenes/levels/conductor_layer.gd`, `class_name ConductorLayer`, a `@tool` `TileMapLayer`): purely visual, no physics. `scenes/levels/conductor_layers.tscn` is a `Node2D` holding four pre-wired layers named Red, Yellow, Green and Blue (one per colour, so differently coloured lines can cross in one cell) on `res://resources/conductor_tileset.tres`. Instance it once per level into `Tilemaps` right after `Ladders`, at the same position as `Ground` (`(-16, 0)` in `test_level`) so cells line up; it draws above the ground tiles and below actors later in the tree. Atlas `res://sprites/conductors/conductors_atlas.png`, 15 columns × 8 rows of 16px: rows 0–3 lit (red, yellow, green, blue), rows 4–7 dim; column = piece: 4 corner W–S, 5 straight N–S, 6 corner E–S, 7 corner N–W, 8 straight E–W, 9 corner N–E, 10 T (N,S,W), 11 T (E,S,W), 12 T (N,S,E), 13 T (N,E,W), 14 four-way. Columns 0–3 (the old terminal stubs) are unused: `WIRE_MASK` gives them mask 0, so a painted one is ignored with a warning. Colour is art only; a level may hold any number of circuits, including several of one colour. No TileSet custom data: the `WIRE_MASK` table (column → open edges) decodes the pieces.
  - **Connections**: two neighbouring cells in one layer connect only if both have an open edge facing each other. Layers never connect to each other, so colours cross freely; a same-layer four-way is a junction. Circuits are found by per-layer flood fill and built **once at load** — tiles painted at runtime don't rewire.
  - **Circuits**: each is a `Node` that emits `powered_changed(on)` and answers `is_powered()`, so it plugs into `PowerTarget` exactly as a button does. It is lit while any attached button is active; a button already active at load lights its circuit immediately, and targets are attached only after every circuit has read its buttons, so a target's first apply already sees the true state.
  - **Terminals are loose ends**: any open edge that doesn't meet a matching open edge in the neighbouring cell is a wire end, and a device in the cell beyond it is attached — no dedicated terminal tile, so any piece (straight, corner, T, four-way) can end a wire next to its device. A button (group `"power_source"`) there becomes a source of the circuit; a `PowerTarget` (group `"power_target"`) there gets the circuit added as one of its sources, so `invert` and `all_required` still work. A device reached from several loose ends attaches to each circuit (a button lights all of them; a target has all as sources). A wire passing beside a device with no open edge facing it attaches nothing. A device counts as being in the cell its `PowerTarget` node (for a target) or its own origin (a button, which sits at tile centre) is in: a multi-tile device gets its wire cell by moving its `PowerTarget` child.
  - **Lit/dim**: all cells of a circuit swap between rows `y` and `y+4`, keeping colour and column; every cell is normalised to one state at load.
  - **Editor overlay**: decodes the painted tiles through the same table — a line from each cell centre to its open edges, coloured per circuit, with a label per circuit (`#n x src y tgt`), and a dot on every loose end's open edge (green if a device is attached, red if not, so a wire stopping short of its device or butting against a piece that doesn't open back shows red). Ignored legacy terminal tiles get an orange cross. If the lines don't follow the drawn wire, `WIRE_MASK` is wrong for the art.
- **Door** (`res://scenes/interactive/door.gd` + `door.tscn`): `AnimatableBody2D`, layer 2, mask 0, joins `"ground"` (ledge-hangable) and `"water_solid"`. Scene: `Sprite2D` (`sprites/interactive/door1.png`, 16×144 = 9 frames, `vframes` 9), `CollisionShape2D`, and a `PowerTarget` child whose `sources` are picked per instance (or left empty for a conductor circuit to attach by adjacency). `invert` is exported on the door itself, so an inverted door starts open. `door.gd` implements `set_powered(on)` (powered = open) and `set_powered_initial(on)` (snaps to the startup state with no animation); a door with nothing wired to it stays closed. Node origin is the centre of its 16×16 tile (the cell `ConductorLayer` files it under); rotate the instance in 90° steps for a horizontal door. Frame 0 is fully closed (an 8px-wide post filling the tile, bottom-anchored), frame 8 fully open; the top edge drops 2px per frame and frames 7 and 8 both keep a 2px foot, so visible height is `max(2, 16 - 2·frame)`. The per-instance `RectangleShape2D` is resized on every frame change so collision matches the art, except that the collider is **disabled whenever the visible height is down to the 2px foot**: an open door is recessed into the floor and the foot is decoration only (a solid 2px lip stops the player dead, since there is no step-up); `open_fps` (16) sets the pace. **Closing never crushes**: before each step that would grow the collider above the foot, `intersect_shape` over the new size (mask layer 1, inset 0.5px per side) looks for a player, enemy or block; if one is in the way the door holds at its current frame and retries every physics frame. Opening shrinks the collider, so it can't be blocked; a player hanging from the door (`hang_platform == self`) is released as it starts to open, since the hang is pinned in world space (same as `drop_platform.gd`). A player standing on a half-open door blocks it from closing further. Sounds `interactive/door_open`/`interactive/door_close` play when the door actually starts moving that way (not while a blocked close waits). Verified in-editor: opening/closing under buttons and circuits, `invert`, both orientations, ledge grab (including release of a hanging player when it opens), the blocked-close wait/retry onto a player and onto a block, water resting on it, laser blocking.
- **Camera cue** (`res://scenes/interactive/camera_cue.gd` + `camera_cue.tscn`, `class_name CameraCue`, `@tool`, `Node2D`): a powered device that shows the player something off-screen: a rising edge pans the camera to it, holds, and eases back. The node origin is the point to look at; it has no collision and no art at run time. The scene carries a `PowerTarget` child (move it to the tile a conductor wire should end at); `sources` and `invert` are exported on the root, so an instance is wired in its own Inspector without Editable Children. `sources` is forwarded to the child (as a copy, so a conductor's `add_source()` never writes the export) when the node enters the tree, which is before the child's deferred first recompute, and whenever the list is edited; left empty it leaves the child's own `sources` alone, and `all_required` stays on the child. **Edges**: only a rising edge of the final power state plays it; a falling edge does nothing; the startup state goes through `set_powered_initial()`, which only records it, so a level that loads with the source already on, and a checkpoint restore, never cue. Exports: `pan_time` (0.6 s), `hold_time` (1.5), `return_time` (0.6), `delay` (seconds from the edge to the start of the pan, so a door's opening can be lined up with the camera arriving; 0), `once` (play only the first time), `cooldown` (ignore edges for this long after a cue ends, whether it finished, was replaced or was cut short by a hit; 0), `ease_curve` (`LINEAR`, `SINE` default, `CUBIC`; used for the pan and the return) and `show_view_rect`. `play()` is public and does what an edge does (it goes through `delay`, `once` and `cooldown`). An edge while a `delay` is already pending is ignored; an edge while the cue is playing restarts it from the camera's current offset, and a second cue shown over a first replaces it the same way (cues are never queued). `delay` and `cooldown` are plain countdowns in `_physics_process()`, so pausing freezes them (there is no `create_timer()`). The camera work belongs to the player (`show_cue()`, see Camera in `player.md`); the cue passes itself as the target, so its `global_position` is read every frame and a cue parented to a moving platform tracks it, and it listens to the player's `cue_ended(source)` to start its cooldown. **No input is locked**: the world keeps running, so the player can move and be hit while the camera is elsewhere; a damaging hit or the win dance ends the cue early (the camera eases back), a jump pad launch or a stomp bounce does not. Keep holds short; a lock would need an input wrapper in `player.gd` first and is not built. Nothing plays if the player is dead or dancing. **Pass-through**: the cue is also a power **source** (`is_powered()`, `powered_changed(on)`, not in group `"power_source"`; a device picks it in its `sources`) that mirrors the cue's final state (after `invert`) with one difference: a **rising edge is held back until the camera is in position**, `delay` plus `pan_time` seconds, so what it drives (a door, a bridge) starts once the player can see it. A falling edge passes at once and cancels a wait. When no cue will play for an edge (`once` already spent, a cooldown running, no player, a dead or dancing player) the edge passes with **no wait**, so the pass-through stays open on later triggers even with `once` set. An edge that joins a pending `delay` waits out what is left of it plus `pan_time`. The wait is a plain countdown in `_physics_process()`, unaffected by a hit or by the cue ending early. The startup state passes at once and `is_powered()` answers it (through `PowerTarget.evaluate()`) before the cue's own first apply, so a device wired to it snaps instead of counting an edge. **Stops**: `CameraCueStop` children (see Camera cue stop) make the cue a sequence: the camera pans to each in tree order, then returns; with none, it looks at its own origin as before. With stops the cue's own `pan_time`, `hold_time`, `zoom` and `frame_player` are unused; `return_time`, `ease_curve`, `delay`, `once`, `cooldown` and `invert` still apply. **Pass-through with stops**: a rising edge is released when the camera arrives at the release stop (the first stop with `releases_pass_through`, else the first), not after a countdown; it is also released when the cue ends for any reason (a hit, the dance, a respawn, a replacing cue). **Time**: a stop's `time_scale` below 1 slows the whole game (`Engine.time_scale`) from the start of its pan until the next stop's pan starts; after the last stop it eases back to 1 over `return_time`. The cue's own clocks (pan, hold, return) run in real time; every game clock (`delay`, `cooldown`, every timer) slows, so at 0.1 a second takes ten on the wall: release a pass-through by a stop, never by seconds. A hit, the dance, a respawn and a level swap restore time at once. Audio is not slowed; the pause menu opens over slow motion. Saveable: `fired` only (what `once` needs, so a restore from a checkpoint after the cue does not replay it); the cue itself and the pass-through are runtime only, and `load_state()` is silent and clears a pending delay, a pending pass and any cooldown (the first apply re-derives the pass-through). Authoring: `show_view_rect` draws the 512x288 view rectangle centred on the cue point while `authoring_overlay` is set (a plain variable the editor sets in `_ready()`, not an `Engine.is_editor_hint()` test, so a runtime level editor can set it too); `get_warnings()` is a plain method that `_get_configuration_warnings()` calls (no `PowerTarget` child; no sources on the cue or on its `PowerTarget`, which is fine only when a conductor wire ends at it). **Zoom**: `zoom` (0.1 to 2, default 1) is the camera zoom multiplier while the cue holds (1 leaves it alone, 0.5 shows twice as much). `frame_player` (default off) centres the view between this node and the player and zooms out just far enough to hold both, every frame, with `zoom` as the most zoomed-in it may be, `frame_padding` (48 px) kept clear and `min_zoom` (0.35) as the widest it will go; use it when the cue shows something far from a moving player (a speed trap's door). The overlay draws the view at `zoom`, and in frame mode a fainter rectangle at `min_zoom`. Sound `interactive/camera_cue_pan` (when the pan starts) is in `Audio.PLANNED`. Place it anywhere in the level.
- **Camera cue stop** (`res://scenes/interactive/camera_cue_stop.gd`, `class_name CameraCueStop`, `@tool`, `Node2D`, no scene): one stop of a `CameraCue`, a plain child node of the cue (`type="Node2D"` plus the script) whose origin is the point to look at; stops play in tree order. Exports: `pan_time` (0.6 s, from wherever the camera is), `hold_time` (1.0), `zoom` (0.1 to 4, a multiplier), `time_scale` (0.05 to 1: the speed of the world from the start of this pan until the next stop's pan) and `releases_pass_through`. Overlay: the numbered view rectangle (orange when it releases) and a dashed path drawn by the cue. Nothing is saved. Sound `interactive/camera_cue_slow` (a cue with a slowing stop starting) is in `Audio.PLANNED`.

## Status

Receiver `BeamTarget`s (laser, arc, freeze) are built but not yet confirmed in-editor: each receiver owns an `Area2D` (layer 4, group by kind) that its own kind of beam must reach, with `get_beam_target()`; the beam is drawn into the receiver and a hit on the body outside the target does not light it. Default shapes: 16×16 for the laser and arc receivers (no change in behaviour until shrunk), 8×8 for the freeze receiver. The laser and arc receivers' root has `z_index` -1 and the freeze receiver's art sprite does, so the beam draws in front. The freeze receiver's target has `stop_at_centre` on, so the beam is drawn in to the receiver's centre whatever the shape's size; the laser and arc receivers' targets keep it off (their beam ends at the shape's edge) and every target has `extra_depth` (see BeamTarget in `lasers-arcs-mirrors.md`). The target's size and position are tuned in the editor (see Beam targets in the Laser beam solver bullet of `lasers-arcs-mirrors.md`).

Camera cue stops, `time_scale` and the stop-released pass-through are built but not yet confirmed in-editor. Headless on 2-5: the pass-through releases on the camera's arrival with the player 96 px short of the spikes, and every early exit restores time; the feel, shimmer at high zoom and interpolation at low scales are unchecked.

Camera cue `zoom` and `frame_player` are built but not yet confirmed in-editor (headless run on 2-3: the player stayed in view for the whole cue, the zoom eased out to about 2.1 of 2.5 and back, `respawn_at()` reset it; pixel shimmer during the zoom and the parallax backdrop's behaviour under a changing zoom are unchecked).
