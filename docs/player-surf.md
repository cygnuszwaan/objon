# Player: surf

The surf state (`State.SURF`): ground speed, tilt, launch and landing, bowling and shoving, surface friction, water and steam, conveyors, jump pads and the swing. Read `objon.md` (the core: setup, layers and groups, contracts, invariants, docs index) first.

Covers: Surf (Ground speed; Tilt; Leaving the ground; Landing; Bowling; Shoving blocks; Surface friction; Water; Conveyors, jump pads and the swing; Drop platforms and the see-saw; Ends without keeping momentum; Shared air carry; Board and audio)

- **Surf** (`is_surfing`, `State.SURF`; its deferred polish is under Roadmap: before level design and level loading, its standing risks under Surf risks): a held state that owns the crouch collider (`wants_crouch` includes `is_surfing`, so it is kept in the air too; up/down are zeroed so ladders and the ladder mount are suppressed). **Mount**: kick while crouching on the floor (`_start_surf()`, before the ordinary kick block); **dismount**: kick again (`_end_surf(true)`: on the ground the player stops and the crouch logic stands up; in the air the speed goes to a steering air carry and the player stands at once via `_stand_up_instantly()`, because the UNCROUCHING one-shot would cancel the carry). Facing is locked at mounting.
  - **Ground speed**: its own `_surf_speed` along the travel direction (not `_slide_vx`), starting at `surf_start_speed` (240), changing each step by `gravity * t.y * surf_slope_gain` (1.25; t is the floor tangent in the travel direction, `t.y` < 0 uphill, so downhill gains and uphill loses fast) minus the friction (`surf_friction` 320 dry, about 5 tiles of coasting on the flat from the start speed, eased toward `surf_friction_wet` 80 by the wetness, see Water, then multiplied by the floor's friction scale, see Surface friction) minus the tilt-mismatch drag (and `surf_skim_drag` while skimming), clamped to 0..the top speed (`surf_top_speed` 600 dry, eased toward `surf_top_speed_wet` 800 by the wetness). It ends below `surf_min_speed` (40); a wall zeroes it, so a wall ends the surf the same way. The velocity is `t * _surf_speed` minus `SURF_FLOOR_PRESS` (60 px/s) along the floor normal: a velocity exactly along an uphill slope never touches it, so `is_on_floor()` flickered false (and the floor snap is off while moving up) without the press. Floor normals within `SURF_FLAT_NORMAL_X` (about 3°) of up are treated as exactly flat (contact normals are noisy, and a tiny upward tangent also cancelled the snap). The press is not part of `_surf_prev_velocity`, which stores the pure tangent velocity a launch or landing should read. Because `_surf_speed` is kept across floor changes, joints between slopes and flat lose no speed; only air landings cost (below). The conveyor and water pushes still add on top of `velocity.x` and don't change `_surf_speed` (the water push is multiplied by `surf_water_push_scale` while surfing; for the belt, see Conveyors, jump pads and the swing below).
  - **Tilt** (left/right, relative to the facing; up/down unused): input toward the travel direction is nose down, against it is nose up, none is level (`_surf_tilt_sign`: +1 up, -1 down, 0 level; deadzone 0.3). Match is by sign: ground sign is +1 uphill, -1 downhill, 0 flat (floor angle under `surf_flat_threshold_degrees`, 8°). A matching tilt costs nothing; a level board on a slope adds `surf_drag_level` (150); any other mismatch adds `surf_drag_wrong` (400), including nose up/down on the flat, so pulling the nose up on the flat is a hard brake (about 35px of coasting against about 82). The drawn board eases (`surf_tilt_ease_degrees_per_second`, 720) to `surf_tilt_down_degrees` (26.57, 2:1) or `surf_tilt_up_degrees` (45, 1:1) by rotating the `SurfBoard` node (`rotation = -angle * facing`); that is looks only, the match uses the target at once.
  - **Leaving the ground** (a ledge, a ramp lip or a jump): `_surf_move()` detects it on the first airborne frame (or the jump consumed by `_surf_jump_pending`), takes `_surf_prev_velocity`, rotates it by the tilt (`_surf_launch_bias()`: nose up `surf_launch_pop_degrees` 12° upward, nose down `surf_launch_dive_degrees` 10° downward, magnitude kept; skipped for a coyote jump already in the air), adds `jump_velocity * surf_jump_boost` to the vertical part on a jump (so a slope's vertical tangent is kept), and hands it to the shared air carry with steering off. A ramp lip needs no code: the upward tangent suppresses the floor snap, so the board simply leaves. Checked: a 1:1 ramp launches along 45°, nose up launches higher and shorter, nose down flatter. A 1:1 slope costs about 1,100 px/s² of speed uphill, so a ramp 2.5 tiles high needs roughly 500 px/s to launch, a 1-tile ramp about 320; downhill 1:1 matched reaches the 600 cap quickly.
  - **Landing** (carry active and the floor reached): `_surf_landing_speed()` takes the arrival velocity (`_surf_prev_velocity`) along the new floor's tangent (what is into the floor is lost), multiplied by `surf_landing_level` (0.85) for a level board on a slope or `surf_landing_wrong` (0.5) for any other tilt/ground sign mismatch, capped at the current top speed (the wet one when the floor reached is wet); a clean landing on a matching slope keeps it all. Checked: the same ramp jump landing on the flat keeps about twice the speed level as with the nose still up.
  - **Bowling** (stage 3): from `surf_bowl_speed` (300 px/s: the ground speed, or the horizontal carried speed in the air; `get_surf_speed()` is the public getter, 0 when not surfing) `_surf_bowl_sweep()` runs each physics frame just before `move_and_slide()`. It is a swept shape query, not a `KickHitbox`-style `Area2D`: a rectangle covering this frame's motion (start and end boxes, so slopes and jumps are covered) plus `SURF_BOWL_LEAD` (8 px) ahead on layer 1 (actors), calling the optional `bowled_over(direction, surf_speed) -> bool` on each body that has it, `direction` being the locked facing. An Area's overlap would only be seen a step late, after the player had already stopped against a solid target. A body that returns true gets `add_collision_exception_with()` on the player at once, because a bowled body only disables its own shape with `set_deferred()` (or `queue_free()`), which is too late to stop this frame's move; the exception is left behind, which is harmless (stale RIDs). The surf keeps all its speed after a bowl. A body without the method, or one that returns false, is left alone and stays solid, so the surf stops against it (a wall). Checked: a fast surfer bowls a grunt and keeps going; below the threshold the grunt stops the surf; a non-opt-in solid body stops it even when fast; a Drillbot facing the way the surfer travels is bowled and one facing the surfer is not; a motionless grunt floating at jump height is bowled in the air. The same query serves every `bowled_over()` implementer (grunt, Drillbot, the breakable barrier). Sound `player/surf_bowl` is in `Audio.PLANNED`.
  - **Shoving blocks** (the surf as a long-range kick): the same sweep also reads a second optional contract, `surf_shoved(direction, surf_speed) -> float`, from bodies with no (or a false) `bowled_over()`; `MeltableBlock` implements it (see its bullet). A negative return refuses and the body stays solid, so the surf stops against it. Otherwise the return is the surf speed the shove cost, taken off `_surf_speed` (or the carried air speed) from the next frame, and the player takes a collision exception with the block and tracks it in `_surf_shoved_bodies` (instance id → body, `touched`, `age`): the block is solid and can't leave in one frame, so the surfer passes through it, and `_update_surf_shove_exceptions(delta)` (top of `_physics_process()`, every state) drops the exception only after the player's collider, grown by 1 px, has overlapped the block and then stopped (`intersect_shape()` ignores exceptions, so it sees the overlap they hide), or after `SURF_SHOVE_GIVE_UP` (0.6 s) without ever touching it. The exception is taken while the block is still up to a frame's motion plus `SURF_BOWL_LEAD` ahead of the board, so it must not be released on "not overlapping" alone: a released body is shoved and charged again, every frame of the approach. A body still in the list is skipped by the sweep. Only a block in front counts: `_surf_shove_reaches()` repeats the query with the player's own height, the bottom 3 px trimmed and no vertical motion, so a block the board stands on or lands on is a floor and is never shoved.
  - **Surface friction**: the floor under the board scales the ground friction (after the wet blend; slope gain, the mismatch drag and the top speed are untouched), so a material changes the coast distance without touching anything else. `_surf_floor_friction_scale()` finds the floor with a short downward `test_move()` (a normal within 60° of up) and reads it from either of two optional sources, 1 if neither says anything, clamped to 0.05..10: a body's duck-typed `get_surf_friction_scale() -> float` (below 1 coasts further, above 1 stops sooner), or a tile of the world tilemap whose TileSet has a float custom-data layer named `surf_friction` (0 or unset = 1; the layer is on the glass tile in `ground_tileset.tres`, set per tile in the TileSet editor; a missing layer is skipped). `IceBlock` implements the method with its `surf_friction_scale` export (0.25: about four times the dry coasting distance, 80 px/s² against 320, so about 22 tiles against 5.6 from the start speed). A new material (mud, gravel) is a body with the same one method, or a tile value above 1; a basin's skim floor has no method, so a skim uses the plain wet friction. The scale applies on top of wetness, so a wet ice block is slicker still and wet mud still drags.
  - **Water** (stage 4; soft gate: dry ground works, water is better). Three surfaces. *Dry ground*: the values above. *Running film* (flowing particles over the floor: streams, runoff, a melted ice block's water): `_update_surf_wetness()` reads `WaterManager.get_floor_wetness(feet, half_width)` while the board is on the floor, a 0..1 coverage of the footprint by non-hazardous particles in a thin band above the feet that are running (above `WET_MIN_SPEED`, not falling or bouncing, so a waterfall overhead or settled liquid doesn't count; slag is a hazard, not a surface, and counts nothing). `_surf_wetness` rises at once and falls linearly over `surf_wet_decay_time` (0.4 s), so the noisy reading can't flicker the friction; it decays in the air too, so a short hop keeps it. The ground friction eases from `surf_friction` to `surf_friction_wet` and the top speed from `surf_top_speed` to `surf_top_speed_wet` by it, so leaving a stream eases the speed back to the dry cap rather than cutting it; the flow's own push (`get_player_push()`, added to `velocity.x` as always) is scaled by `surf_water_push_scale`, so with the current is faster and against it slower. *Settled basin* (skim): at or above `surf_skim_min_speed` (180), `_update_skim_floor()` (called each frame just before `move_and_slide()`) asks `WaterManager.find_skim_basin()` for the basin under the board (liquid over that column that is not hazardous right now, so not slag and not water a spark has electrified, no crust, surface at or below the feet or up to `SKIM_CATCH_DEPTH` 6 px above them, feet not below the seabed; with basins stacked in a column the nearest surface below wins) and raises that basin's skim floor (`WaterBasin.set_skim_floor()`): a thin one-way `StaticBody2D` on layer 3 laid flat on the flat liquid line across the interior columns (plus 2 px into the walls), its one-way margin set to the catch depth. Because it is a real floor, landing, riding, flat-floor tilt matching (level is the match), jumping and launching off the surface all use the ordinary surf code with no special case, and the swim state never starts while it holds (the swim probe stays above the surface). On the surface the floor reads fully wet (`_surf_on_skim`) and `surf_skim_drag` (160) adds to the friction, so a skim is short without speed. The floor is re-placed on the current liquid line every frame it is up and dropped when the surf ends, the player leaves the basin's columns, or the basin changes. Below the minimum speed no floor is raised and the player falls into the ordinary swim (`_update_swim_state()` ends the surf); a surfer riding the surface that drops below it ends the surf, stops and sinks the same way. A dismount on the surface also sinks. Slag is never skimmed, and neither is electrified water while it lasts (no floor is raised; the hazard and swim entry apply), a crusted basin is just solid ground, and particle water is not skimmable. Cosmetic only: a splash and `player/surf_splash` on first touching the surface (`SWIM_SPLASH_IMPULSE` times the arrival vertical speed) and a wake behind the board each frame (`SKIM_WAKE_IMPULSE` times the speed) through `WaterBasin.disturb()`; nothing touches fill volume. A basin's far wall is an ordinary wall: below the rim it ends the surf, at the rim (a full basin) the surface continues straight onto the wall top. The water chain for level design: melted ice runs down a slope and makes a wet route, a skim crosses a pool, a crusted slag lake is just a floor, and a basin's rim or sloped floor tiles are natural ramps. The player's mask includes layer 3 (added in `_ready()`). **Steam lift**: only while surfing, `_surf_move()` smooths `WaterManager.get_steam_lift()` (see the Liquid system) over the collider's rect into `_surf_steam_lift` (0..1; passing its previous value and the frame time, so the manager does the smoothing, and 0 whenever the surf isn't running). In the air, `_apply_surf_steam_lift()` accelerates the board upward by `surf_steam_lift` (1500 px/s², on top of gravity, so full steam nets 600 up) times the lift, never beyond `surf_steam_max_rise` (120 px/s) and never slowing a faster upward speed (a jump pad's). On the floor, once `surf_steam_lift` × the lift exceeds `gravity` (900) the board leaves it: that frame departs like a ramp lip, without the tilt's launch bias and keeping only the upward part of the floor-tangent velocity (downhill it would point into the floor and the snap would hold the board down), and the ground branch is skipped until it lands again. Below that threshold steam does nothing on the floor. Lift only writes the velocity, so a solid ceiling still stops the board; the walking player, a swimmer and blocks never read steam. The two exports and the decay time in the manager are placeholders to tune against the column's height and density. A reading doesn't cut out the instant a surfer leaves the column: it decays over `STEAM_LIFT_DECAY_TIME`, so the board keeps rising briefly past the column's top.
  - **Conveyors, jump pads and the swing**: *Conveyors*: a belt is a moving floor. `_surf_move()` reads `_conveyor_push_speed()` into `_surf_belt_vx` each grounded frame (0 in the air, off belts and when the surf starts or ends); friction, slope gain, tilt drag and the top speed stay relative to the board's own `_surf_speed`, the shared conveyor line in `_physics_process()` adds the belt to `velocity.x`, and the speed over the ground along the travel direction is `_surf_speed + _surf_belt_vx * facing`. `get_surf_speed()` returns that on the ground (so the bowl threshold and the speed trap read the true ground speed; in the air it is the carried horizontal speed as before), `_surf_prev_velocity` includes the belt so a launch off a belt's end carries it (the landing doesn't subtract it again, so a belt gives a small net boost), and the **stall gate** ends the surf (stop, stand up) when that ground speed falls below `surf_min_speed`: a belt as fast as the board against it stalls it at once, a slower one only slows the ground speed. *Jump pads*: `launch()` doesn't tear anything down while surfing; it buffers the vector with `_pending_launch_keeps_surf` and `_apply_surf_launch()` (last in `_physics_process()`, like every launch) replaces the vertical velocity with the pad's and adds the pad's horizontal part to the board's horizontal velocity (`_air_carry_vx` if already carrying, else `velocity.x`), hands the result to the shared non-steering air carry and `_surf_prev_velocity`, and applies no tilt bias, so the board leaves the pad with the speed it had, lands back into the surf and keeps the speed (a straight-up pad at 445 px/s took off at 445 and landed at 445); if the surf ended before the buffered launch is applied it falls back to a plain velocity write. A non-surfing player is launched as before, through `_interrupt_current_action()`. *Swing*: the swing grab gate has no surf exclusion, so a surfing player in the air grabs a bar like any other airborne player: `_start_swing_hang()` reads the surf velocity into `catch_momentum()` before `_end_surf(false)`, the board vanishes and the swing takes the whole speed (400 px/s on the 64px default rod is about 6.2 rad/s). A swing with angle limits (±80° in `swing.tscn`) pins at the limit and zeroes the angular velocity there, so a fast catch swings to the stop and back; a swing meant to take a big arc or a loop needs `free_rotation` (it then swung past 100° in the check), and `catch_transfer` tunes the share.
  - **Drop platforms and the see-saw** (no surf code): a drop platform's countdown latches and can't be cancelled, so a fast surf across a long one beats it. A level see-saw plank is crossed at speed with no loss beyond the ordinary friction; a tilted plank is a ramp when approached from its low end, but its raised end is a lip of about `half length × sin(angle)` px that stops a fast surf like a wall, so a see-saw on a surf route needs the surf to arrive on the low side (or a plank end that sits flush at its extremes).
  - **Ends without keeping momentum** (all via `_end_surf(false)`): `_interrupt_current_action()` (a hit, a dance; a jump pad launch doesn't come through here while surfing), grabbing a swing (`catch_momentum()` is given the surf velocity first), and entering water with no skim floor up (too slow, or slag; see Water). Ledge and catwalk grabs and the catwalk climb-down are gated off while surfing.
  - **Shared air carry**: the swing's carry was generalized: `_air_carry_active/_vx/_steers`, `_begin_air_carry(vx, steers)`, `carry_air_accel`; steering carries (swing release, dismount) let left/right steer toward `input * speed`, non-steering ones (surf) don't; it ends on the floor, a grab, a one-shot, an interrupt, and a wall contact zeroes it.
  - **Board and audio**: a separate `SurfBoard` `Node2D` in `player.tscn` (script `surf_board.gd`, class `SurfBoardArt`, placeholder drawn in code facing right, origin on the board's top surface) placed under the feet each frame and mirrored by `scale.x`, hidden unless surfing; the `fall` animation is the placeholder pose. `player/surf_start`, a `player/surf_loop` loop (off in the air and while skimming, pitch follows speed), a `player/surf_water_loop` loop (only while skimming, same pitch) and `player/surf_splash` (touching a basin's surface, positional) and a `player/surf_steam_loop` loop (volume follows the steam lift, silent without it) are in `Audio.PLANNED`. `get_surf_speed()` is the public speed getter (the bowl threshold and the speed trap read it).
