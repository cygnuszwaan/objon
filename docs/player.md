# Player (`player.gd`)

The player's states and mechanics: movement, floors, crouch, kick, climb, ledge, catwalk and swing hangs, swim, health, gems and winning, launch and interrupts. The surf is in `player-surf.md`. Read `objon.md` (the core: setup, layers and groups, contracts, invariants, docs index) first.

Covers: Movement; Slippery floors; Rising rigid floors; Crouch; Kick; Climb (ladder); Ledge hang + mantle; Catwalk underside hang + brachiate; Swing hang; Rail hang; Swim; Health / Hurt / Dead; Gems / win; Jump pads; `_interrupt_current_action()`; Facing; Hard teleports; Camera; A tile-boundary rounding gotcha

State is a single `enum State` + `match`, decided each frame from physics reality (`is_on_floor()`, `velocity.y` sign, input) rather than tracked as an independent flag — deliberately simple rather than a node-per-state architecture. One-shot animations (kick, uncrouch, mantle, climb-down, hurt) share one `enum OneShotAction`; held states (`is_crouching`, `is_climbing`, `is_hanging`) are separate bools with their own lifetimes, not folded into the enum.

- **Movement**: horizontal from `move_left`/`move_right` axis; gravity + jump with coyote time and jump buffering (independently counting-down floats, consumed together). `floor_snap_length` is raised to `slope_snap_length` (8px, not the 1px default) — walking down a slope moves horizontally first and can leave the floor several px below the body within one physics tick, which the 1px default can't snap across, causing `is_on_floor()` to flicker false and the player to visibly hop; snapping is skipped automatically while `velocity.y < 0`, so jumps are unaffected. Side effect: any step down smaller than the snap length is walked down rather than fallen off (no coyote window for those).
- **Slippery floors**: duck-typed like `get_conveyor_speed()` — `_floor_is_slippery()` does a short downward `test_move()` (not last frame's slide collisions: grounded, `velocity.y` is 0, so the slide often records no floor contact at all) and asks whatever it's standing on for `is_slippery() -> bool`. On a slippery floor the horizontal block builds toward `input_dir * speed` at `ice_accel` and decays toward 0 at `ice_friction` (also while crouching/kicking) instead of snapping. `_slide_vx` holds the player's *own* horizontal velocity, separate from `velocity.x`, because `velocity.x` also gets the conveyor and water pushes added each frame; every non-climb/hang/swim branch refreshes it (before the pushes), so stepping onto ice from normal ground carries momentum, capped at walk speed when re-entering the slide. It's zeroed on running into a wall and on entering the hurt/dead branch. Only `IceBlock` implements `is_slippery()` so far; the surf reads its own, separate contract for the same surfaces (`get_surf_friction_scale()`, see Surf).
- **Rising rigid floors**: `_lead_rising_rigid_floor(delta)` runs right after `move_and_slide()`. A `RigidBody2D` floor (an ice or metal block on a rising level) moves after the player's own step, so the engine's carry plus the floor snap would put the player back on the surface it just left and the block would rise into a rider that stayed put. When the player is on the floor, the platform velocity is rising faster than `RISING_FLOOR_MIN_SPEED` (1 px/s) and a 2 px downward `test_move()` finds a `RigidBody2D` rising at that speed, the player moves up by the floor's rise for the tick with `move_and_collide()` (stopping at a ceiling), so it sits where the surface will be. Other moving floors (platforms, belts, planks) are untouched. Standing on a floating block while the level rises needs no swim-state special case: the block keeps its rest fraction, so the swim probe never reaches the water.
- **Crouch**: a runtime-derived shorter `CollisionShape2D`, built once in `_ready()` from whatever shape type is present (`RectangleShape2D`: half height; `CapsuleShape2D`: half height clamped to two radii, since a capsule can't be shorter than its own caps — note Godot 4's `CapsuleShape2D.height` is the *full* height including caps, not the cylinder segment only). Standing back up is blocked by a real `intersect_shape` physics query, not `is_on_ceiling()`. **Any resource shared across scene instances (a `Shape2D` on a `CollisionShape2D`) must be `.duplicate()`d before being mutated at runtime**, or every instance of that scene fights over one shared resource — this bit both the player's crouch shape and the Crusher's resized arm shape. **Sneak** (`State.SNEAK`, the `sneak` clip): a crouched player holding left/right creeps at `sneak_speed` (50 px/s, walk is 150) instead of being rooted. `_can_sneak()` gates it (crouched, on the floor, no one-shot running, not surfing, and the crouch-down animation on its last frame or the `sneak` clip already playing), so movement and the state decision share one test and nothing creeps while the crouch-down plays. It is derived each frame from `is_crouching`, input and the sprite, not a held state, so `_interrupt_current_action()` needs no entry for it. The `sneak` clip's ping-pong is baked into its frame list (atlas frames 0 1 2 3 4 3 2 1, Loop on); `sprite.frame` is the index into that 8-entry list, which is also what `FRAME_SOUNDS` keys on (`player/sneak_step`). Letting go of left/right returns to `State.CROUCH` by holding the crouch clip's last frame (`_hold_crouch_pose()`), not by replaying the crouch-down. Standing up from the `sneak` clip reverses `crouch` from its end (`play("crouch", -1.0, true)`): a reverse started from another clip begins at frame 0 and finishes at once. On ice the sneak speed is a target approached at `ice_accel` / `ice_friction`. Sneaking off an edge stands the player up in the air as before, but the stand-up keeps the creep velocity (`_slide_vx` clamped to `sneak_speed`) instead of stopping dead; a crouch-jump from a standstill still has none. Held crouched under a low ceiling with down released, left/right creeps out (before, the player was rooted there). Kick while sneaking mounts the surf as for any crouch.
- **Kick**: a one-shot action ending via `animation_finished`, gated against crouch. `KickHitbox.body_entered` has two independent duck-typed contracts: `take_damage(amount) -> int` (enemies, `Mirror`) and `kicked(direction: float)` (−1 left / +1 right, from `sprite.flip_h`; `IceBlock` uses it to know which way to slide). There is no dash and no dodge: the only immunity is the post-hit `hurt_invincibility_timer`. **Air kick**: a kick pressed while airborne (not crouching, climbing, hanging, swimming or surfing, and not the same press that mounted or dismounted the board) starts the same one-shot through `_start_air_kick()`, with the same animation, sound and hitbox. It differs from the ground kick in two ways: it does not root the player (`_air_kick` is true, so the KICKING branch of the horizontal movement is skipped, and a carried swing or dismount momentum survives it), and with `air_kick_halts_ascent` (default true) it sets `velocity.y = maxf(velocity.y, 0.0)`, cancelling the rise. It never slows a fall, so spamming it cannot hover. While the animation plays no ledge, catwalk or swing grab can start (they need `one_shot_action == NONE`). Landing mid-kick just lets the animation finish. Uses the ground kick art for now.
- **Climb (ladder)**: held state, not a `OneShotAction`. Ladder membership is **collider overlap**, sampled along the current collision shape's centerline (so it respects crouching) — not origin-cell membership — so a ladder whose top is flush with a floor stays grabbed until the feet actually clear it. Mounting from *above* (standing on a surface a ladder continues through) is a separate downward probe gated on down-input only. Mounting *upward* from a floor lifts the feet to just above that floor's surface when they already sit inside the landing catch zone (`landing_catch`, measured from the tile's cell top): a tile whose collider top is lowered below its cell top leaves them there, and the landing would otherwise end the climb on the next frame. Landings (both directions, on any floor a ladder passes through) stop the player there rather than carrying them through; `climb_up_latched`/`climb_down_latched` stop a still-held key from instantly re-triggering a grab/crouch right after a landing. Vertical movement overrides gravity and locks horizontal, x-snapped to the ladder cell's center column. **Several ladder layers**: the player reads every layer in group `"ladder_tilemap"` (`_ladder_layers()`, read each call): the level's static `Ladders` layer and any powered ladder layer while it is out (see Powered ladder layer in `hazards-traps.md`). `_ladder_cell_overlapping()` and `_ladder_cell_under_feet()` remember the layer a hit was on (`_ladder_hit_layer`, `_ladder_mount_layer`) for the x-snap on mount. A ladder that retracts under a climbing player ends the climb through the ordinary not-on-a-ladder rule, so they fall.
- **Ledge hang + mantle**: grab detection runs every physics frame while airborne, on-wall, and descending (not collision-enter-only, so a jump alongside a wall can grab a ledge above the first contact point). Two raycast probes (forward clearance, headroom) gate the grab; a downward ray finds the ledge top, and the hang keeps the player's x (the grab only runs in contact with the wall, so the player is already flush; `hang_grip_inset` and `hang_hand_local_offset` are tuned against that). The wall face is never read from a ray: a hand-height ray hits or misses depending on whether the hand is a hair above or below the ledge top at the jump apex, and a hit would shove the hang 8 px off the wall. `_is_hangable_surface()` accepts tiles `SurfaceTypes.is_hangable()` approves (`"ground"`/`"glass"`/`"catwalk"`) or group membership (`"ground"` or `"catwalk"`); a body that also has `is_hangable()` (`MeltableBlock`, `RotatingBody`) is asked at the grab and every frame of the hang. Mantling up (`climb_up` pressed while hanging, or, for a ledge hang only, a fresh press of left/right toward the wall; a direction held through the grab does not trigger it) tweens position to a computed target over half the `climb_up` animation's actual duration.
- **Catwalk underside hang + brachiate**: a second, parallel hang path (`hang_style: HangStyle` — `LEDGE` or `CATWALK_UNDER`) for grabbing a one-way catwalk's underside — no wall check at all, since a catwalk's one-way collision produces no side contact. Detection is a single straight-up raycast (raycasts are assumed to ignore `one_way_collision`, unlike body collision resolution). Reachable by an airborne jump that doesn't clear the catwalk, or by crouch+jump while standing on one (animated via a reversed `climb_up` clip). Once hanging in this style, holding left/right brachiates hand-over-hand along the underside (`catwalk_brachiate_speed`), playing the `"brachiate"` animation only on frames that actually displace the player and freezing it otherwise (including when blocked by the catwalk's end — travel is clamped there, not dropped). Pressing up/down while brachiating over a ladder cell hands off directly into the Climb state. **Jump** (`_jump_up_through_underside()`) jumps up through the catwalk or crust, as a rail hang's jump leaves the rail: `velocity` becomes `(0, jump_velocity)`, `_skip_buffered_jump` is set, there is no regrab lockout and the jump sound plays. The grab only takes a falling body (`catwalk_hang_min_fall_speed`), so the rise cannot re-catch, and a jump that falls short (a low ceiling above) catches the underside again on the way down. A catwalk is one-way, so the body rises through it by itself. Crust is solid, so the jump adds a collision exception with that crust's body (`_crust_pass_body`) which `_update_crust_pass()` ends, every physics frame in every state, once the feet are above the crust's collision top (the crust is solid again and a landing on it works), the head is below its underside, or the body is no longer over any crust of it; `_unembed_from_crust()` leaves the exempted crust alone meanwhile, and `respawn_at()` clears it. A hit mid-pass does not clear it, so a body knocked about inside the crust is never left embedded in a solid it collides with. **Crouch** is the plain let-go. Mantling up through a catwalk uses the same mantle machinery as a ledge mantle, but with its own `catwalk_mantle_offset` (x-symmetric — there's no wall corner to offset around). **Crust** (roadmap 6.1): a basin's crust body joins group `"crust"` (never `"catwalk"`, so lasers, water and steam still stop on it) and the same hang accepts its underside: the up-probe's `is_catwalk` key is also true for a crust hit (`_is_free_crust_hit()`), but only if `WaterBasin.is_underside_free(x, top, bottom)` says no liquid lies anywhere in the span the hanging body would fill, so it only works over a void left by a drain and is refused while liquid fills the space. Brachiating re-tests that at each candidate x (it stops where the crust ends or liquid begins), and a hang lets go if liquid rises into the body (`_hang_crust_blocked()`). **Brachiating is also blocked by terrain** (`_brachiate_blocked_into()`, for catwalk and crust alike): a hang skips `move_and_slide()`, so without it a crust whose underside follows a floor that rises to meet it (the crust is clipped at the seabed) carries the body into the slope. A shape query tests the region from just below the grip surface down to the feet (raised by `catwalk_brachiate_floor_tolerance`), so the crust or catwalk the hand is in never counts; crust, the platform hung from, anything in group `"catwalk"` and `CharacterBody2D`s are not blockers, and a hang already overlapping terrain can still move (it is never pinned). Tiles of the world tilemap block unless every tile the region touches is a catwalk, so keep catwalks on their own `"catwalk"` layer. A mantle up onto crust ends at the crust's own collision top (`_crust_mantle_target()`: the catwalk formula applied to `get_crust_collision_top_world_y()`), so the feet land on it with no drop. The crust's walkable collision starts `CRUST_COLLISION_TOP_INSET` (1 px) below its top face, because the art's first row is empty and a standing player looked to float; only the collision moves. A crust hang is anchored `crust_hang_lift` (3 px) higher than a catwalk's so the hands meet the crust's drawn underside; while hanging, the up-probe starts that much lower (a ray that starts inside the crust's collider never hits it, which stalled the brachiate). The crouch+jump entry from above (roadmap 6.2) works on crust too: the feet probe's `is_crust` key is true when the player stands on crust whose underside is free of liquid (`_crust_hang_is_free()`, the same test as the grab, on the standing collider), and `_try_climb_down_to_catwalk_hang()` then animates the reversed `climb_up` down to the same anchor the airborne grab uses (`WaterBasin.get_crust_underside_world_y_at()`, the crust's bottom or the seabed where that clips it). Over liquid it is refused and the ordinary crouch+jump fires; the catwalk entry is unchanged. Not hangable from the side: crust is not in `"ground"`. **Powered catwalk layer** (see Powered catwalk layer in `hazards-traps.md`): a tile hang has no `hang_platform`, so a layer that retracts calls `release_hang_from_layer(layer)` on the player, which lets go through `_force_release_hang()` (the regrab lockout applies, a mantle in progress is stopped) when the grip point, a pixel above `hang_anchor.y - catwalk_hang_grip_drop`, is in one of that layer's cells. The ladder-landing scan reads cells from every layer in group `"catwalk"`, and a retracted powered layer has left the group.
  - **`hang_style` must be set explicitly by every function that starts a hang** (`_start_hang`, `_start_catwalk_hang`, `_begin_catwalk_climb_down`, `_start_swing_hang`) rather than relying on its default — it isn't reset automatically between hangs, so a stale value silently misdirects the mantle-offset branch on the *next* hang.
  - **`is_on_floor()` and `velocity` read stale while `is_hanging` is true**: a hang skips `move_and_slide()` entirely for its whole duration (needed so the pinned hang position never gets physics-resolved), so anything that reads those mid-hang is seeing whatever they were the instant before the hang started, not the current state.
- **Swing hang** (`hang_style` `SWING`; see the Swing bullet under World objects): a third hang on the same machinery (`is_hanging`, no `move_and_slide()`, state `HANG`, the `hang` animation, `hang_hand_local_offset` for the hand) with the hand pinned to a moving bar. `_try_grab_swing()` runs with the catwalk grab's gates (airborne for `hang_min_airborne_time`, no regrab lockout, not climbing/swimming, no one-shot) and no velocity condition, so a jump can catch the bar on the way up: the nearest swing in group `"swing"` whose grip is within its `grab_radius` of the player's hand point wins. `_start_swing_hang()` snaps the hand onto the bar, calls `attach_rider()`, hands the swing the velocity the player arrived with (`catch_momentum(velocity)`, read before it is zeroed; in the air that is the frame's `velocity` after `move_and_slide()`, so horizontal momentum is whatever the air control or a carried `_air_carry_vx` set) and keeps the facing fixed for the whole hang. The pin is done by the swing (`pin_to_swing(anchor)`, called from `Swing._physics_process()` right after it moves), not by `_follow_hang_platform()` (`hang_platform` stays null). While hanging, `_update_swing_hang()` passes left/right to `Swing.set_push()` every frame; `crouch` drops, `jump` jumps, `climb_up` does nothing. `_release_swing(jump)` sets `velocity` to `Swing.get_velocity_at(global_position)` (tangential velocity of the body about the pivot), adds `jump_velocity * swing_jump_boost` (default 1) to `velocity.y` on a jump, clears `jump_buffer_timer` and sets the regrab lockout. **Momentum carry**: the ordinary horizontal block overwrites `velocity.x` with input every frame, so after a release `_air_carry_active` makes it use `_air_carry_vx` instead: untouched with no input; input steers toward `input * speed` at `carry_air_accel` (400 px/s²), except that pushing the way you are already going above walk speed never slows you. It ends on the floor, a ladder, swimming, a hang, any one-shot action, or `_interrupt_current_action()`, and a wall contact (checked right after `move_and_slide()`) zeroes it when it points into the wall. `_slide_vx` follows it, so landing on ice keeps the momentum (capped at walk speed). `_interrupt_current_action()` and `_force_release_hang()` detach from the swing.
- **Rail hang** (`hang_style` `RAIL`; a Path2D in group `\"grab_rail\"`, which a `RailBot` adds for its rail unless `rail_grabbable` is off, or which a designer adds by hand to a bot-less rail): a fourth hang on the same machinery (`is_hanging`, no `move_and_slide()`, state `HANG`). `_try_grab_rail()` runs with the swing grab's gates (airborne, no regrab lockout, not climbing/swimming/surfing, no one-shot) and no airborne-time gate: the hand point (`hang_hand_local_offset.y` above the origin) must be within `rail_grab_radius` (widened by the frame's fall step) of the nearest curve point on a rail no steeper than `rail_max_grab_degrees`, so a jump catches it on the way up too. The hand is pinned to the curve at `_rail_offset` every frame (`_pin_to_rail()`, in world space, so a moving Path2D carries the player); `hang_platform` stays null. The tangent at the offset decides the mode each frame: flatter than `rail_flat_degrees` (10°) is **brachiating** (left/right add `catwalk_brachiate_speed` along the rail, the `brachiate` animation plays only while displacing); steeper is **sliding** (the `hang` sprite; `_rail_speed` gains `gravity * rail_slide_gravity_scale * tangent.y` per second, capped at `rail_slide_max_speed`; left/right do nothing). On the flat `_rail_speed` decays at `rail_flat_friction`, so sliding off a slope coasts to a stop. Catching a slope keeps `rail_catch_momentum` of the speed along the tangent; catching the flat keeps none. **Jump** (`_release_rail(true, ...)`) sets `velocity.y = jump_velocity`, carries the slide's velocity through the air with `_begin_air_carry()` when sloped, sets `_rail_jumped_off` and sets `_skip_buffered_jump` (so the buffered-jump consume does not fire again); `_try_grab_rail()` ignores the rail while `_rail_jumped_off` and `velocity.y < 0`, so the rise cannot re-grab and the player catches the rail on the way back down (no timed lockout). **Crouch** drops with `hang_regrab_lockout`. Brachiating stops at either end; sliding off an end releases with the slide's velocity. **Rail bots hurt**: a bot's contact hitbox calls `take_hit()` (see Rail bot), which runs `_interrupt_current_action()` and so detaches the rail and knocks the player off; the hang code itself does not treat a bot as an obstacle, so brachiating or sliding into one hurts, and the way past is a jump. `_rail_pose_hits_bot()` is a shape query of the standing collider against bodies in group `\"rail_bot\"` on layer 1, used only to refuse a grab whose pose would overlap a bot (the player falls past instead of grabbing and being hit). `_interrupt_current_action()` and `_force_release_hang()` call `_detach_rail()`.
- **Swim**: held state (`is_swimming`, own lifetime, same convention as `is_climbing`/`is_hanging`), entered/exited via a hysteresis on `_water_surface_depth()` — a per-column depth query against whatever `WaterBasin` the player's column belongs to (via `water_manager`), not tile membership or an `Area2D` overlap (`swim_enter_depth`/`swim_exit_depth`; `swim_probe_offset_y` raises the probe point to roughly chest height so entry/exit read right rather than triggering at the collider's center). Vertical input reuses `climb_up`/`crouch` (with `jump` folded in as an extra up-source) and drives `velocity.y` directly (`swim_up_speed` up, `swim_speed` down/horizontal); with nothing held, `velocity.y` eases toward a slow passive-sink target (`swim_sink_speed`) via `move_toward()` at `swim_gravity`'s rate, and is hard-capped to `swim_speed` the instant swimming starts so a body that fell before reaching the water doesn't carry an unbounded velocity into it. `_set_swim_collider()` rotates the collider 90° for the horizontal swim pose, anchoring whichever wall edge is currently touched across the rotation so it doesn't leave a gap or shove the player away from the wall. The rotation is refused if the rotated collider would overlap a solid at the player's position (`_swim_pose_fits()`, a real shape query) — a gap narrower than the swim pose between two ice blocks or a block and a basin wall — since the wide pose would pin the player between both sides with no way to move or leave swim state. In that case swimming carries on with the upright collider (`_swim_rotated` false while `is_swimming` is true) and `_update_swim_state()` retries every frame until the pose fits; un-rotating a pose that was never taken is a no-op. Consequence: the swim pose can also be refused beside a catwalk tile (the shape query doesn't honour one-way collision), which only leaves the player upright until they're clear of it. Kick, crouch-entry, ledge-grab, and catwalk-grab are all gated off while swimming; the water system's ambient particle push (see World objects below) is gated off too, since a swimming player owns their own velocity directly. A basin the player swims in can be hazardous right now (`WaterBasin.is_hazardous_now()`: slag, or water a spark has electrified), and then every frame of submersion calls `take_hit()` (`SLAG_CONTACT_DAMAGE` for slag, `ELECTRIC_CONTACT_DAMAGE` for electrified water; only slag floats you up, since only slag has a `swim_float_speed`). Slag is swimmable too, but strongly buoyant: with no vertical input, `velocity.y` eases toward upward `LiquidProfile.swim_float_speed` (60 px/s for slag, 0 = none, water keeps `swim_sink_speed`) via `_swim_passive_target()`, so the player drifts up toward an escape (swim-up 80 beats it, swim-down 120 still beats it), while `take_hit()` is called each frame of submersion, paced by its i-frames. The slag damage check runs in every held state too (hanging from a ledge or on a ladder with the probe point below a slag surface still hurts, and the hit knocks the player off); only swim entry/exit is gated on climbing/hanging. A hit drops swim state through `_interrupt_current_action()` and normal entry resumes once the hurt animation ends. While hurt or dead inside a buoyant liquid, `_process_incapacitated()` floats toward the same speed instead of applying gravity. Surfacing has no special "climb out" handling — crossing back above the exit threshold just hands off to whatever the next frame's normal floor/fall logic decides. See `water-mechanic.md` for the full constant list.
- **Health / Hurt / Dead**: `max_health` is 4 (the health bar art has 4 segments). `take_hit(damage, from_position) -> bool` is the damage contract (duck-typed via `has_method`), separate from the enemy-facing `take_damage(amount) -> int`. Knockback direction is which side of the hazard the player is on. HURT is a `OneShotAction`; DEAD is a terminal bool holding the last frame. Hurt/dead/dance all route through one early-return branch in `_physics_process` (gravity + friction-decay + `move_and_slide()` only) rather than being threaded through the normal per-state logic, since most of that logic doesn't itself check for incapacitation. On death `_schedule_respawn()` starts a tween bound to the player that calls `Game.respawn()` after the `dead` animation's length (summed from the SpriteFrames frame durations and speed) plus `respawn_delay` (3s, exported); a tween rather than `await` so it dies quietly with the player if `R` reloads first. `respawn_at(pos)` is the hard teleport `Game` calls after a restore: position, zero velocity, full health, `health_changed`, both interpolation resets. Only `take_hit()` kills the player — there is no kill plane. `_physics_process` returns right after `_update_swim_state()` when `is_dead` (slag's `take_hit()` runs inside it), so the state decision can't overwrite the `dead` animation with fall/idle.
- **Gems / win**: `collect_gem()` increments `gems_collected` and emits `gem_collected(count, total)`; `gems_total` is the number of nodes in group `"gem"`, counted once at load (deferred from `_ready()` so every gem has joined; gems free themselves on pickup, so never recount; a checkpoint restore sets both numbers from the snapshot and `_count_gems()` then does nothing, so the total stays the level's original one). `gem_collected(count, total)` is emitted on every pickup, and `gems_counted(total)` once when the load-time count is made (not when a restore supplied the total); `GemDisplay` and `Finish` listen. Collecting never wins by itself: the level's `Finish` calls `dance_through(center_x, slide_time, shrink_time)`, which runs the public `start_dance()` (terminal `is_dancing` flag, like `is_dead`; plays `player/win`, emits `won`) and returns a physics-process `Tween` that slides the body's x to the door centre and then shrinks the **sprite node's** `scale` to `DANCE_SHRINK_TO` (0.02, not 0) and hides it. The sprite is centred with no offset, so the node origin is the art's centre and it shrinks toward its middle, feet leaving the floor; collider, camera and kick hitbox are untouched. Don't change `sprite.scale`/`sprite.position` elsewhere. Dance is **not** gated through `OneShotAction`/`animation_finished` the way Hurt/Dead are — it's a one-way flag, so its animation is free to loop.
- **Jump pads**: `launch(vector: Vector2)` is the duck-typed contract a jump pad calls (mirrors `take_hit()`/`collect_gem()`). It drops out of any held state via `_interrupt_current_action()` (except the surf, which keeps going: see the surf's Conveyors, jump pads and the swing bullet), then buffers the vector rather than writing `velocity` directly — a pad's `Area2D` signal can fire at an unpredictable point in the physics step relative to the player's own `_physics_process` for that frame, and a direct write can land before either the grounded `velocity.y = 0` gravity reset or the unconditional `velocity.x = input_dir * speed` line has run, which would silently cancel it. The buffered vector is applied once, after everything else that touches `velocity` that frame, so it always wins regardless of ordering; a launch with a horizontal part (more than 1 px/s) also starts the air carry (`_begin_air_carry(velocity.x, true)`, steering like a swing release), since the grounded input line would otherwise replace `velocity.x` on the next frame and leave only the vertical part (a straight-up launch starts none, so a plain pad keeps its air control); the normal per-frame decide-state logic then turns the resulting `velocity.y < 0` into `State.JUMP` on its own, the same as jumping off a ledge-hang. **Any future code that writes to `velocity` must stay above that point in `_physics_process`, not below it**, or it'll have the same problem this fixed.
- **`_interrupt_current_action()`** is the single place that tears down whatever the player was mid-way through on a landed hit (kills the mantle tween, clears climbing, drops a hang with a re-grab lockout, stands up from a crouch if possible, resets the one-shot action, zeroes jump buffer/coyote timers). **Any new held state, one-shot action, or tween must be added here too**, or a hit mid-action leaves it dangling. (The one deliberate exception is the camera cue, which a launch must not cut: it ends in `take_hit()` and `start_dance()`; see Camera.)
- **Facing**: `sprite.flip_h` plus `_update_kick_hitbox_facing()` (mirrors `KickHitbox`'s position around local x=0) fire together everywhere facing changes. `flip_h` mirrors strictly around the sprite node's own local origin and ignores `offset` — an off-center sprite (art with asymmetric canvas, e.g. the drillbot's drill) needs its `offset.x` manually negated to compensate, or mirrored around the sprite's own center instead by negating `scale.x`. An asymmetric `ConvexPolygonShape2D` collider needs its own points negated per-axis, not just its position.
- **Hard teleports** (hang grab, mantle snap, ladder mount) call `reset_physics_interpolation()` on **both** the player and the camera — a child node's own interpolation state doesn't get reset by its parent's, so skipping the camera produces a one-frame "world jumps toward the player and snaps back" glitch even though the player's own position is correct.
- **Camera** (`Camera2D` child of the player, zoom 2.5, no limit or smoothing nodes of its own): the player's `_process()` (idle time, so the offset is smooth at the display's rate; the offset isn't physics-interpolated) eases `camera.offset` toward a target read from `velocity` as the last physics step left it. *Lead*: horizontal speed above `camera_lead_start_speed` (200, so walking at 150 never moves the camera and a surf, which starts at 240, leads a little) gives `(|vx| − start) × camera_lead_gain` (0.25 s) px of offset in the direction of travel, capped at `camera_lead_max` (96 px of the 256 px half-view), easing at `camera_lead_smoothing` (4/s, which is also how fast it swings over on a reversal). *Fall*: downward speed above `camera_fall_start_speed` (320, about 6 tiles of free fall) gives `(vy − start) × camera_fall_gain` (0.2 s) px of look-down, capped at `camera_fall_max` (80 px of the 144 px half-view), easing at `camera_fall_smoothing` (6/s, and back up the same way after the landing). Upward speed never moves the camera. Anything that sets `velocity` feeds it (surf, swing release, jump pads, knockback); a wall hit zeroes the velocity and the lead eases out. Dead or dancing: both offsets ease to zero. The offset is separate from the hard-teleport reset, which still resets the camera's interpolation (see Hard teleports). The eased lead and look-down are kept in `_lead_offset`, and `camera.offset` is that unless a **camera cue** plays (the object is Camera cue in `power.md`). `show_cue(target, params)` pans onto a world point or a `Node2D` (its `global_position` is read every frame), holds, and eases back; `params`, all optional: `pan_time` (0.6), `hold_time` (1.5), `return_time` (0.6), `ease` (`&"linear"`, `&"sine"` default, `&"cubic"`), `source` (handed back through the signal). The camera has no limits or smoothing, so its view centre is its own position plus `offset`, and the offset that centres a point is the point relative to the camera: any distance works, and it holds on the point whether the player stands still or walks. The pan eases from the camera's current offset (lead included), so a cue shown mid-lead, or over another cue, never jumps; a second cue replaces the first and restarts the hold (never queued); the return eases to the live lead. It is a timer-driven state advanced in `_process()` (idle time, not a `Tween`), so pause freezes it. `is_cue_playing()` reports it and `cue_ended(source)` fires when a cue finishes, is replaced or is cut short. A damaging hit (death included) and `start_dance()` end it early through `_end_cue()`, which eases back over `return_time`; `respawn_at()` drops it at once. A jump pad launch or a stomp bounce does not end it, which is why it is **not** in `_interrupt_current_action()`, the one deliberate exception to adding every new tween there. `show_cue()` is ignored while dead or dancing. Input is never locked.
- **A tile-boundary rounding gotcha**: mapping a raw collision contact point to a tile cell with `local_to_map()` can round a boundary-exact point into the wrong (adjacent) cell depending on which side was hit. Nudge the point a half-pixel inward along the collision normal before mapping.
- **A non-looping one-shot `AnimatedSprite2D` animation left with Loop ON never fires `animation_finished`**, silently soft-locking whatever state depends on it — check Loop is off for every one-shot animation (`hurt`, `dead`, etc; `_ready()` warns if it isn't for the ones that matter).
