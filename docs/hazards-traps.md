# Hazards and traps

Sawwheel, Crusher, Cannon and Bullet, the spike tiles and the powered tile layers (spikes, catwalks, ladders). Read `objon.md` (the core: setup, layers and groups, contracts, invariants, docs index) first.

Covers: Sawwheel / Crusher hazards; Cannon / Bullet; Spikes; Powered spike layer; Powered catwalk layer; Powered ladder layer

- **Sawwheel / Crusher hazards**: poll `get_overlapping_bodies()` every physics frame and call `take_hit()` on anything that has it, rather than a one-shot `body_entered` — a hit can be ignored (i-frames/grace) while the hazard itself never moves, so a one-shot signal could mean the player is never re-hurt after immunity ends. Crusher derives its animation frame and hitbox position from one shared phase value each frame (extend/retract/dwell durations authored per-instance) rather than running the sprite and the collision as two independent clocks; its solid arm is a separate `AnimatableBody2D` sized off the same phase value the sprite and hitbox use, so all three can't drift out of sync. The crusher's danger hitbox also calls `shatter()` on anything it overlaps that has it (`MeltableBlock`), but only when the spike comes down on it (`_crushing_from_above()`): the crusher isn't on its retracting leg, and a point 1 px inside the bottom edge of the spike, on its centre line, is inside the block's collider (a physics point query on layer 1, where blocks sit). A `brittle` block breaks: ice bursts into water as a melt does, carbon fiber dissolves with no liquid; metal is untouched. A block kicked into the side of the arm, or launched up into a rising spike, is merely stopped: a block pressed against the arm has its near edge at the arm's side, so the centre-line point stays outside it. **Jam**: a block whose `jams_crusher()` is true (`MetalBlock`, unless melted; false in the base class, for ice and for carbon fiber) stalls the piston. The arm extends as normal and `_latch_jam()` fires the moment the spike touches the block from above, using the same `_crushing_from_above()` test as ice (the crusher isn't retracting and the spike's centre-line tip point is inside the block's collider), polled in the same overlap loop. That covers metal already under the arm when the extend leg starts and metal that slides in mid-stroke alike, and the arm is drawn touching the block before it starts back. The latch sets `_jammed`, plays `traps/crusher_jam` once, parks the cycle clock at the start of the retracted dwell and freezes it; the arm then reverses at once to the park position at its retract speed (from `_retracted_fraction`, last frame's pose) and stays there, the spike harmless. A block pressed against the side of the arm doesn't touch the spike from above and doesn't jam it. The latch holds whatever happens to the block afterwards (melted, kicked away). `set_powered(true)` after an off state clears it (a power cycle; the cycle then resumes from the retracted dwell, and re-jams on its next touch if the metal is still under it); an unwired crusher never receives `set_powered()`, so it stays jammed until the level reloads or an earlier checkpoint is restored. `set_powered_initial()` (the startup and restore apply from `PowerTarget`) sets the power state without being an edge, so a restored jam is kept. `jammed` is the only state the jam adds to the snapshot; a restored jam comes back fully parked. The same polled pattern is used by an electrified frozen crust (`WaterBasin._poll_electric_crust()`, see Frozen crust in the Liquid system), which hurts a player standing on its top while it is electrified.
- **Cannon / Bullet** (`cannon.gd`, `bullet.gd`): `Cannon` is an `AnimatedSprite2D` root (idle holds frame 0) with a `StaticBody2D` (layer 2, standable) for its solid body, a `Muzzle` `Marker2D` for the spawn point, and a `Timer`. `fire_rate` (exported, seconds between shots) drives the timer; each shot plays the animation once, plays a sound, and spawns a `Bullet` from `Muzzle`, facing `Vector2.RIGHT.rotated(global_rotation)` — facing is the node's own rotation rather than `flip_h`, so a turret can point any direction without the offset/collider-mirroring work `flip_h` needs elsewhere. `Bullet` is an `Area2D` (masking layers 1|2, since it must detect world tiles as well as the player, unlike this project's other trigger areas) that travels at a fixed internal `speed` and, on hitting the player or a `world_tilemap` body, calls `take_hit()` (if applicable), plays a sound, spawns an `Explosion`, and frees itself. It does not hit other traps or enemies, but has one optional duck-typed contract, `on_bullet_hit(point, direction) -> bool`, called on any other body its mask sees (layers 1|2) that has it: true means the body took the bullet and it detonates, false lets it fly on, so a body can opt out at runtime. `Mirror`, `BulletTarget` and `MeltableBlock` implement it: a metal block absorbs the bullet unharmed, an ice or carbon fiber block breaks and consumes it (see Meltable block in `meltable-blocks.md`), so a bullet no longer flies through a block.
- **Spikes** (hazard tiles: `res://scripts/tile_hazards.gd`, `class_name TileHazards`; art `sprites/tiles/spikes_sheet.png`; `Player._poll_tile_hazards()`): spikes are tiles of the ground TileSet (`ground_tileset.tres`, atlas source 3, four 16×16 tiles: points up, right, down, left, the art pre-rotated, so orientation is the tile you paint and never a flip/transpose flag) with **no collision at all**: they block, push and carry nothing, and water, steam, lasers, bullets, blocks and enemies pass through (and, with no `surface_type`, they are not ground-like for the ledge grab, basin scans or laser solver). Two TileSet custom-data layers define a hazard: `hazard_damage` (int; 0 or unset = harmless; the `take_hit()` argument, set to 1 on the four tiles) and `hazard_direction` (Vector2, the way the points face, dominant axis only; unset = up). `TileHazards.hit_in_rect(layer, rect)` returns the most damaging hazard tile overlapping a world rect (`{damage, position}`, empty if none) and `danger_zone()` is the tile's dangerous part: the half of the tile that holds the art (the side opposite the direction), `SPIKE_TIP_MARGIN` (1 px) short of the tips and `SPIKE_SIDE_INSET` (1 px) in from each side, so the gaps between teeth hurt and the tips just clear of an actor do not. The player polls it every physics frame right after `_update_swim_state()` with its current collider rect (swapped in the swim pose, so crouching, surfing and swimming all use their own collider) and calls `take_hit(damage, tile centre)`; polling means a player still standing in spikes is hurt again when the i-frames end, a hit from any side knocks it away by which side of the tile it is on, and a surf ends through `take_hit()` like any hit, with no spike code in the surf. A landed hit plays `traps/spikes_hit` (in `Audio.PLANNED`) at the tile. Nothing is saved (tiles are level geometry). Only the player asks. A tile can't carry a `PowerTarget`, so switchable spikes are painted on a Powered spike layer instead. **Painting**: put spike tiles on the `Ground` layer (the `world_tilemap`) in the cell the spikes occupy, on top of or beside the solid tile they stand on. **Live pitfalls**: a new hazard tile is a custom-data change only (`hazard_damage` on its tile), a new orientation is a new atlas tile with its own `hazard_direction`; `_ascending_landing_y()` treats any painted cell of the world tilemap as a floor, so don't paint spikes in a cell a climbing player's feet pass through (a ladder cell).

- **Powered spike layer** (`res://scenes/traps/powered_spike_layer.gd` + `powered_spike_layer.tscn`, `class_name PoweredSpikeLayer`, `@tool`, a `TileMapLayer` on `ground_tileset.tres` with a `PowerTarget` child; joins group `"hazard_layer"`; extends `PoweredTileLayer`, the base it shares with the catwalk and ladder layers below, which owns `sources`, `invert`, forwarding, the startup snap, the sound call and the warnings, so the spike script holds only the poll, its sound ids and its tile-set warning): a block of spike tiles that is out or retracted as a whole. Paint spike tiles on it exactly as on the `Ground` layer (atlas source 3, one tile per direction); it needs no other setup. **Powered = out** (drawn and dangerous), **unpowered = hidden** (`visible` off) and skipped by the poll. With no valid source the layer is not driven and stays out, so an unwired layer behaves like spikes painted on the ground; `invert` makes the spikes out while the source is off. `sources` and `invert` are exported on the layer root and `sources` is forwarded to the `PowerTarget` child (a copy, at `_enter_tree()` and whenever the list is edited, as `CameraCue` does); `all_required` stays on the child. A conductor wire attaches at the cell the `PowerTarget` child sits in, so **move that child to the tile the wire should end at** (it starts at the layer origin). Power switches the whole layer, so independent groups of spikes are separate layers; per-spike control would need a node and is not built. **Transitions are instant both ways and there is no warning phase**: extending into a cell the player stands in hurts on that physics frame (the poll's normal rules, so `take_hit()` i-frames still apply), and retracting spares the player on the same frame. `set_powered_initial()` snaps the startup state silently; `set_powered()` plays `traps/spikes_extend` or `traps/spikes_retract` (both in `Audio.PLANNED`) at the middle of the painted cells, and only on a real change. **The poll**: `Player._poll_tile_hazards()` reads the world tilemap, then every node in group `"hazard_layer"` whose `is_hazard_active()` is true, through the same `TileHazards.hit_in_rect()` and `danger_zone()`, and takes the most damaging hit, so the hit rule, knockback, surf ending and `traps/spikes_hit` are unchanged; a layer is found by `has_method("is_hazard_active")`, never by class name. `collision_enabled` is forced off in `_ready()`, so nothing painted on the layer can block, and the layer has no `surface_type`, so it is never ground-like; keeping spikes off the world tilemap also avoids the `_ascending_landing_y()` pitfall above. **Nothing is saved**: the layer is a pure function of its sources (`out` is runtime state, authored state is out), so a restore needs no code here, and the layer follows a source whose restore emits `powered_changed` on a change. Enemies and blocks still ignore spikes. `get_warnings()` is a plain method (`_get_configuration_warnings()` calls it): no `PowerTarget` child, no sources on the layer or the child (fine only with a conductor wire; unwired, the spikes stay out), a tile set without `hazard_damage` custom data.

- **Powered catwalk layer** (`res://scenes/traps/powered_catwalk_layer.gd` + `powered_catwalk_layer.tscn`, `class_name PoweredCatwalkLayer`, `@tool`, a `TileMapLayer` on `ground_tileset.tres` with a `PowerTarget` child; extends `PoweredTileLayer`): a run of catwalk tiles that exists only while powered, for a bridge a timed button summons. Paint catwalk tiles (`surface_type` `"catwalk"`, one-way) on it as on the level's `Catwalks` layer; the scene's tile set is the ground tileset (source 0 is a catwalk tile), and the Inspector's `tile_set` can be swapped for the level's own catwalk tiles. **Powered = out**: collision on, visible, and in group `"catwalk"`, so everything that finds catwalks through a physics hit (the hang probes, the laser solver, water and steam rays) or through the group (the ladder-landing scan in `_ascending_landing_y()`, which reads cells, not collision) accepts it. **Unpowered = retracted**: `collision_enabled` off, hidden, and **removed from the group**, so none of them see it (the group removal is what keeps the cell-reading landing scan from treating a gone catwalk as a floor). Unwired stays out and `invert` is as on the spike layer; `sources` and `invert` are on the root, `PowerTarget` child and `all_required` as in Powered spike layer, nothing is saved. **Not ground**: the layer is never in `"world_tilemap"` or `"ground"` and a configuration warning names any painted cell that is not a catwalk tile; ground and glass can't be switched, because the basin scans, the water manager, the laser's world lookup and the surface checks all key off the single `world_tilemap` layer. **Retract warning**: `warn_time` (default 1 s, 0 = none; hidden on the spike layer, which has no warning phase) blinks the layer at 5 Hz (the TIMED button's last-second rate), still solid, between the power falling and the layer retracting; the retract is therefore `warn_time` later than the source, so a TIMED button's `duration` is the time before the blink starts. Power returning during the blink cancels it with no sound and no change; the startup snap never blinks. A checkpoint restore that finds the source off while the layer is out gives one blink before it retracts. **Ghost view** (all three layers, from `PoweredTileLayer`): the `show_ghost` checkbox (default off; `ghost_opacity` appears when it is on, default 0.25, a fraction of the layer's own `modulate.a`) keeps a retracted layer drawn as a faint ghost so the player can see where it will appear. It is purely visual: the layer is still retracted in every other way (no collision, out of its groups, no hazard), so a ghost spike layer is harmless and a ghost ladder cannot be climbed. During the blink the layer alternates between full and ghost instead of full and hidden. Both exports are live (changing them at run time redraws). **Extending** is harmless to anyone in the cells: the tiles are one-way, so a body rises through them. **Retracting** under a standing player just drops them (the collision is gone). A player hanging from the underside, or mid-mantle onto it, would stay pinned in the air, since a tile hang has no platform to follow, so the layer calls the player's `release_hang_from_layer(self)` (duck-typed, found through group `"player"`) as it retracts, and `Player` lets go the way a crouch does (with the regrab lockout) if its grip point is in one of that layer's cells. Sounds `traps/bridge_extend` and `traps/bridge_retract` (in `Audio.PLANNED`, shared with the ladder layer) play at the middle of the painted cells on a real change after startup. `get_warnings()` is a plain method: no `PowerTarget` child, no sources (fine only with a conductor wire), a tile set with no `surface_type`, a painted cell that is not a catwalk. **Pitfall**: a static catwalk tile in the same cell as a retracted one keeps its own collision, so a hanging player there is released and may re-grab the static tile after the lockout (untested); keep a powered bridge's cells off the static catwalk layer.

- **Powered ladder layer** (`res://scenes/traps/powered_ladder_layer.gd` + `powered_ladder_layer.tscn`, `class_name PoweredLadderLayer`, `@tool`, a `TileMapLayer` on `ladder_tileset.tres` with a `PowerTarget` child; extends `PoweredTileLayer`): a run of ladder tiles that exists only while powered. Paint ladder tiles on it as on the level's `Ladders` layer. **Powered = out**: visible and in group `"ladder_tilemap"`. **Unpowered = retracted**: hidden and out of the group. A ladder cell is just "a tile is present", so there is no collision to switch; the player reads **every** layer in the group (`Player._ladder_layers()`), static and powered, and remembers which layer a hit was on for the x-snap on mount. A player climbing a ladder that retracts is dropped on the next physics frame by the ordinary "no longer on a ladder" rule (nothing here calls the player). The blink, `warn_time`, `invert`, unwired-stays-out, nothing-saved and the sounds are as on the catwalk layer. Place the layer at the same position as the level's `Ladders` layer so the cells line up. **A bridge that is a catwalk with a ladder through it takes two layers**, one of each kind, wired to the same source. `get_warnings()`: no `PowerTarget` child, no sources, no tile set.

Reads with: `power.md` (cannon, crusher, sawwheel and the spike, catwalk and ladder layers are powered devices), `meltable-blocks.md` (crusher jam and shatter), `player.md` (`take_hit()` and its i-frames, Climb and Catwalk underside hang for the powered layers), `enemies.md` (the same polling pattern, the mine blast).
